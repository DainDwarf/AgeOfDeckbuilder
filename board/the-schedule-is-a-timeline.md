# The schedule is a timeline

**Line:** **The schedule is a timeline** — the events and the schedules join the catalogue, the weights and the tempo numbers on the schedule and a second script on the event for the turns of its span; the launch rolls the schedule into a timeline the founding takes as an argument and the chronicle carries, every landing still reading the chronicle as it stands; the walkers and their bound go, a fixture timeline in their place; the boot takes a schedule id and the stand-in's short schedule brings the e2e to the victory screen in three turns; the stand-in's coherence test covers the events, the schedules and the victory line; and the two remaining deferred dogmas return. Doc-impact: `docs/CHRONICLE.md`, `docs/GLOSSARY.md`, `docs/DOGMAS.md`.

**Scope:**

In:

- The `Catalogue` type, in `src/rules/catalogue.ts`, gains two tables, every id an open `string`:
  - `events: Record<string, ScheduledEvent>`, the event as content: `reads(catalogue, chronicle)`, `lands(catalogue, chronicle)`, and an optional `continues(catalogue, chronicle)`, the second script, run on every turn of the event's span after the one it lands on. No weight: that is the schedule's.
  - `schedules: Record<string, Schedule>`, a schedule the composition: `spacing: Span`, `deal: number` (how many a due turn deals, two today), `capstone: { event: string; window: Span; span: number }`, and `entries: Record<string, (turn: number) => number>`, the event ids it draws from, each with its weight curve.
- `catalogued` validates the new content, every refusal through the units line's one refusal function: every entry id and every capstone id resolves to an event; a schedule's capstone is not among its entries; an event carrying `continues` is the capstone of some schedule, since nothing else runs it; a schedule's `deal` and its capstone's `span` are at least one, and each of its two spans has a least of at least one and a most no smaller. Two lookups that throw on an unknown id, `eventOf(catalogue, id)` and `scheduleOf(catalogue, id)`, in the shape the earlier lookups took.
- The **timeline**, in `src/rules/schedule.ts`: `Timeline = { deals: readonly { turn: number; entries: readonly string[] }[]; capstone: { event: string; turn: number; last: number } }`, the capstone's deal not among `deals`. `timelineOf(catalogue, schedule, rng) → { rng, timeline }` rolls it: the first due turn from the spacing, then the capstone's turn from the window, `last` being that turn plus the span less one, then a walk of the turns from one to `last` doing at each what the events phase does today — the capstone's turn deals nothing to `deals` and rolls the next due turn from itself, whatever was due; a due turn draws its deal from the entries weighing anything on that turn, `deal` of them, never the same one twice, what there is when fewer weigh anything, and rolls the next due turn from itself; a due turn on which no entry weighs anything writes no deal and rolls nothing, so the next turn is tried. The draws come from the generator handed in, in that order, and the generator it leaves is answered.
- The chronicle loses `nextEvent` and `capstoneTurn` and carries `timeline: Timeline`. `beginChronicle(catalogue, seed, deck, map, timeline)` takes it as it takes the map: state, not validated. `launched(catalogue, region, schedule, seed, deck)` generates the map from `seedRng(seed)`, rolls the timeline from the generator the map left, and opens the chronicle on both; the schedule id resolves through the lookup. The opening still runs the events phase, so a timeline dealing on turn one opens the chronicle on its deal.
- The events phase reads the timeline and draws nothing: on the capstone's turn it deals the capstone alone, whatever the timeline lists for that turn; on a turn the timeline lists a deal for it deals those entries in the order listed; otherwise nothing. The take lands the entry taken and rolls nothing. `dealsCapstone(chronicle)` is a deal standing on the capstone's turn. `survived(chronicle)` is the turn being the capstone's `last`. The reinforcement rule becomes the rules running the capstone event's `continues`, when it has one, on every turn after the capstone's through `last`, under the `reinforce` stage as today.
- The helpers content writes an event in are exported from `src/rules/schedule.ts`, each taking the catalogue first and resolving its ids through the lookups, on the cards line's pattern: `raided(catalogue, chronicle, warriors)`, the raid's warriors entering one after another each from its own camp; `laid(catalogue, chronicle, card)`, a card laid on top of the draw pile, the id resolved through `cardOf`; `besieged(catalogue, chronicle, camps, fromCentre)`, the siege's placement with the numbers as arguments, the camp's building and unit read from `catalogue.camp`; `reinforced(catalogue, chronicle)`, the camp's unit entering on every camp whose tile is free, in tile order. The raid's count by turn, one and one more for every ten turns, is content and leaves the rules for the stand-in.
- `src/content/stand-in.ts`: `STAND_IN` gains the three events with the numbers `schedule.ts` holds today, written through the exported helpers, `PH_Siege` carrying `continues: reinforced`; and two schedules: `PH_Schedule` — spacing three to seven, deal two, the capstone `PH_Siege` in the window twenty-seven to thirty-three spanning six, `PH_Raid` and `PH_Famine` each weighing one on every turn — and `PH_ShortSchedule`, the same in every number but the capstone's window, two to two, and its span, two. Its coherence test grows: every event has a name and a rules entry in the text table, every event a schedule names as its capstone has a victory line, read through the same lookups the screen uses; the timeline of each schedule rolls on a fixed seed; and on a chronicle launched from the stand-in's region and each schedule on a fixed seed, every event answers `reads`, lands, and, where it carries one, `continues`, without throwing.
- `src/rules/fixtures.ts`: `CATALOGUE` gains the same three event ids, the fixture's own, with numbers frozen at today's and written through the same exported helpers, and one schedule, `schedule`, with today's numbers. The walkers `landings`, `toFirstRaid` and `throughSchedule`, with `SCHEDULE_BOUND` and `LATE_CAPSTONE`, go; `scheduled` goes with them. `cityOf` carries a timeline that deals nothing — no deal, the capstone `PH_Siege` on a turn past any a test ends, its last five on — so a test that wants an event writes a timeline dealing it on the turn it wants it, and none walks to find one.
- `src/rules/schedule.test.ts` is rewritten on the fixture, test by test: what asserted the cadence over thirty walked turns per seed asserts it on the timeline `timelineOf` rolls on the fixture schedule — the first deal on the third turn to the seventh and each next three to seven turns on, the same seed the same timeline and another seed a different one, the capstone's turn inside the window and `last` its turn plus five, no deal on the capstone's turn, a due turn's deal holding both entries in an order the seed decides; what walked to a landing hands in a timeline dealing on the turn it wants and ends turns to it: nothing dealt before the due turn, the deal standing with the turn stopped, the take landing the entry taken and no other and drawing a hand, the raid's camp drawn from the generator, the raid entering what it can, the famine dealt as readily on any turn and laying its hazard, a take not offered refused, a waiting chronicle taking no command but the take. The siege tests keep their assertions on a timeline whose capstone's turn is thirty and last thirty-five, the reinforcement now the fixture siege's `continues`. `src/rules/catalogue.test.ts` gains: a catalogue whose schedule names an event it lacks, whose capstone is among its entries, whose event carries `continues` under no schedule's capstone, or whose schedule's deal or span is below one, is refused; `timelineOf` on a schedule the catalogue lacks is refused; a coherent one builds. `src/rules/enemies.test.ts`'s two walked tests hand in a timeline dealing the raid on the second turn; `cards.test.ts`'s `nextEvent: 9` and `sight.test.ts`'s `scheduled` call go with the fields.
- UI: `ChronicleScene(catalogue, region, schedule, seed, deck)`, its `begin` calling `launched` with the schedule id it holds; the opening window reads the capstone from `chronicle.timeline.capstone.event`; `eventFace(catalogue, chronicle, id)` reads the event through `eventOf`; the ending screen's victory line reads `victory.<capstone event>` through a lookup; `text.ts` gains an event name lookup, an event rules lookup and a victory line lookup that take an open id and throw when no entry names it, on the card lookups' precedent, and the key `victory.survived` becomes `victory.PH_Siege`.
- `src/main.ts`: `?schedule=` resolved through `scheduleOf`, `PH_Schedule` when the address names none.
- e2e: the launch helper in `e2e/chronicle-screen.ts` takes a schedule id, `PH_Schedule` unless given, and `open` and `openOnCapstone` pass it; `capstone.spec.ts` reads the capstone from `STAND_IN`'s schedule; `deal.spec.ts` reads its due turn from the timeline's first deal and asserts the next deal's turn past it; `camps.spec.ts` reads its raid's turn from the timeline; `e2e/victory.spec.ts`, new: opens on `PH_ShortSchedule`, ends three turns through `endTurn`, which takes the capstone's deal on the second, and asserts the chronicle ended in victory on the third turn and the victory's ending screen risen, as `fall.spec.ts` reads the defeat's.

Out:

- An ordinary event with a span: the chronicle carries no list of running events, so nothing runs `continues` but the capstone; the design page holds the door.
- The schedule as a launch choice on screen: the throwaway selection page's.
- No number changes. Every rules test yields the same stages on the fixture catalogue as before, with one exception the line owns: every schedule draw moves to the launch, so every seed's chronicle differs from today's from its first shuffle on; a spec pinned to a seed for what its schedule dealt is re-pinned, never weakened, and the specs that search seeds find them afresh.

Corner cases decided: a hand-made timeline listing a deal on the capstone's turn deals the capstone alone, that turn's rule winning; a timeline with no deal at all is content, and its chronicle runs to the capstone's `last`; a timeline handed to the opening is not validated, as the map and the deck are not, and the event lookup throws at the first read of an id the catalogue lacks; the `reinforce` stage keeps its name though what it plays is the capstone's second script; the map's and the timeline's draws chain on one generator from `seedRng(seed)`, so a region change changes the timeline, accepted and stated nowhere else; the raid's count by turn is the stand-in's and the fixture writes its own.

**Traps:**

- The units, map and cards lines ship first, and this line builds on their `catalogue.ts`, refusal function, `STAND_IN`, `CATALOGUE`, `launched` and text lookups; the ship session reads that code once, at the ship, and this dossier names shapes, not lines.
- `PH_Famine.lands` lays the literal `'PH_Hunger'` and `PH_Siege` reads `SIEGE` and `SIEGE_SPAN`; the span leaves the event for the schedule, and `survived` reads `timeline.capstone.last`, the one place the span's arithmetic survives.
- `overlay.ts` reads `SCHEDULE.capstone.event` for the opening window and `card-face.ts` reads `SCHEDULE.events[id].reads`; both go through the chronicle's timeline and the lookup.
- `text.ts`'s `rules.` keys are one namespace for cards and events; the event rules lookup reads `rules.<id>` as the card one does. `TextKey` is a closed union, so a template key over an open id does not typecheck: the lookups are the way through, and `text.ts` is hook-linted for glossary synonyms — no sentence is added, one key is renamed.
- `tsconfig.json` sets `noUnusedParameters`: a closure that takes the catalogue and ignores it names the parameter with a leading underscore.
- The e2e helpers `workerRun`, `stepRun`, `atTileRun` and `fallRun` launch through the helper too; `endedTurn` in `chronicle-screen.ts` already takes the first entry of a deal, so the victory spec's three `endTurn`s take the capstone on the second.
- The victory claim rests on three numbers: the siege's camps stand three to five tiles from the city, a warrior crosses two plain tiles a turn, and capture needs an enemy on the city's tile when the enemy phase begins; the earliest capture is turn four's enemy phase and victory is declared at the end of turn three. A road under a camp would break it; the generator lays none.
- `docs/DOGMAS.md`'s bullets, the `docs/CHRONICLE.md` paragraphs and the glossary rows are one line each; `npm run lint` refuses a wrapped one.
