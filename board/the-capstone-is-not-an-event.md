# The capstone is not an event

**Line:** The capstone is not an event — the capstone is content of its own, what it lands and its second script, named by the schedule; on its turn it lands straight, with no answers and no deal, and the hand is drawn as on any turn; the chronicle screen raises its card in the capstone's window once more at the landing, which the end of turn's play-out ends on and the draw follows; the design page says so, the Nomadic page gives the trial no answer, and the capstone paragraph carries no stand-in detail. Doc-impact: `docs/CHRONICLE.md`, `docs/ages/NOMADIC.md`.

**Spec:** `docs/CHRONICLE.md` → _The rules_ → _The turn_ and _Events and the capstone_, and _The screen_ → _The chronicle screen_; `docs/ages/NOMADIC.md` → _The capstone_. Written out:

- _The turn_, item 1. After "the draw waits on the take." add: "On the capstone's turn the capstone lands instead, and nothing is dealt."
- _Events and the capstone_, first paragraph. Delete the sentence "An event may carry a second script, run on every turn of its span after the one it lands on." Nothing takes its place: the second script is the capstone's below.
- _Events and the capstone_, the capstone paragraph, replaced whole with: "**The capstone is not an event.** It is the age's trial: what it lands, one script run on the chronicle as it stands, and a second script it may carry, run on every turn of its span after the one it lands on, before whatever that turn deals. It deals no answers and asks nothing: on its turn the events phase lands it alone, whatever else was due, its landing runs straight and the hand is drawn as on any turn, and the next deal is due from its landing as from any. 🔧 The schedule keeps dealing through the capstone's own turns — the fewest special rules until play says which feel best. **The capstone lands on a turn drawn when the chronicle opens**, seeded, from a window of turns the age's schedule names; the timeline carries the turn, and the window and the span are the levers behind "shorter in the earlier ages". The player knows what the capstone is from the opening and never when: the chronicle screen opens on the capstone's card, and the window is shown nowhere; the landing raises the card again, so the turn is learnt when it comes. **A capstone names what passes it.** Either its span: the schedule names how many turns, and a city still standing when the last of them ends has passed it, the chronicle ending in victory at that end of turn, before the turn ticks over, and carrying the ending as it carries a defeat. Or a condition: its second script reads the chronicle at the end of every turn from its landing, and the chronicle ends in victory at the end of the first turn the condition holds, with no last turn to run out. Either way the city's fall before that is defeat. What the trial is, is content, one per age: what it lands, what passes it, and what its second script does on each turn. A capstone dealt with its answers as an event's was rejected: a deal of one answer is a choice of nothing. A fixed turn known from the launch was rejected: a countdown the player can read turns the whole chronicle into arithmetic toward it."
- _The chronicle screen_, the paragraph opening "The capstone gets a **window of its own**", replaced whole with: "The capstone gets a **window of its own**, raised twice: when the chronicle opens, which every chronicle screen opens on, and when the capstone lands, where the end of turn's play-out ends on it and the draw plays out once it is closed. Both show its card alone, drawn as the deal window draws an answer and at the same width, `CAPSTONE` where a card reads its kind, under a title — at the opening, that the age ends on that capstone and nothing of the turn it lands on; at the landing, that the capstone lands. The window offers nothing to take, so no press rings its card and the inspection key does nothing while it stands; a left click on the card closes it, as the back key and a press beside the card do, and it is closed for good — the chronicle screen it stood on raises it again at the landing and never else, while a new chronicle opens on its own. The right click shows the card large over the window, and a press beside it or the back key takes it down onto the window, as on the deal. The menu opens over the window and closes back onto it."

**Scope:**

- In: a capstones table on the catalogue beside the events, each capstone what it lands and the second script it may carry, and the schedule's capstone naming one by id with its window and span; the events losing the second script; the events phase landing the capstone straight on its turn, a stage of the end of turn the draw follows, and dealing nothing on it; the catalogue refusing a schedule whose capstone it does not hold, and dropping the three rules built on the capstone being an event — a capstone dealing one answer at least, a capstone not dealt among the entries, a second script only under a capstone; the timeline naming the capstone by its id; the stand-in and the fixture moving PH_Siege out of the events into the capstones, its landing the siege's placement and its second script the reinforcement, the three stand-in schedules naming it; the screen's capstone window raised again at the landing, the play-out ending on it and the draw playing out when it closes, its card labelled `Capstone`; the ending screen and the coherence tests reading the capstone by its id.
- Out: what passes the capstone and the timeline's roll (the next line); any change to the deal window, the announcement's timing, or the events' shape beyond the second script; a landing signal on the map beyond what lands.
- Corner cases decided here: a camp's rewards standing when the capstone's turn opens are taken first, as today, and the take then opens the capstone's turn — the tick, the second script, the landing, and the draw, with the landing's window rising once the reward's closes and the draw waiting on it. The timeline lists no deal on the capstone's turn, and the events phase lands the capstone alone on it whatever was listed, as today. A capstone that lands nothing still raises its window: the window is the landing's, not the script's. The landing window is the screen's, not the chronicle's: the rules land the capstone and draw the hand in the one end of turn, and no command is added for the click. Only the capstone's own kind label changes; a reward's card reads its kind as a card of the deck does.

**Traps:**

- The text table keys the announcement's title `capstone.title`; a capstone named under a `capstone.` prefix collides with it. Pick the capstone's prefix so that `named` resolves an id and a title apart, and keep every entry one sentence.
- `map.ts` plays the arrivals of an `events` and a `reinforce` stage by name; the capstone's landing stage, whatever it is named, has to be played the same way, or the siege's warriors stand on the map with no arrival.
- `overlay.ts` raises the announcement from `render` on the first render, behind the `announced` flag, and the deal window from `render` while a deal stands; the landing's window is raised from the play-out, not from a render, and a render while it stands must not raise it again nor close it. The menu closes back onto it through the `capstone` variable the announcement already uses.
- `e2e/chronicle-screen.ts`: `stoppedTurn` waits for the play-out to end with the turn advanced, which holds with the landing's window standing once the play-out ends on it; `endTurn` takes every deal standing, and has to close the landing's window too, by a press on its card as `open` closes the announcement's, and wait the draw out. `endedTurn` on the rules changes nothing: no deal stands at the landing.
- `e2e/victory.spec.ts` opens on `PH_ShortSchedule`, whose capstone lands on turn 2 and spans two, and expects victory on turn 3; its comment says the second end of turn takes the capstone, which it no longer does. `e2e/capstone.spec.ts` reads the capstone off the schedule's capstone entry, whose field changes shape.
- `src/rules/fixtures.ts` `endedTurn` takes the answer to name when a deal stands; the schedule tests land the siege through `endedTurn(awaitingCapstone(), 'PH_Hold')`, and the capstone's turn no longer stands a deal.
- `NO_DEALS` in the fixtures names the capstone on a turn no test reaches; it keeps that and changes field.
- The catalogue's `dealtBy` loop carries the comment that a capstone deals one answer at least; it goes with the rule.
- `besieged` reads the chronicle's generator; the landing now draws it inside the end of turn instead of inside a take, which changes what a seed's siege places by nothing a test reads.
- The next line, "The capstone passes on a condition", is dossiered against the code this line leaves — the capstones table, the schedule's capstone entry, the timeline's capstone field.
