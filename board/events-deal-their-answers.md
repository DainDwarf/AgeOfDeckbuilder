# Events deal their answers

**Line:** Events deal their answers — inert, on the stand-in and the fixture alike: a due turn deals one event and the event deals its answers, two or more, as cards; the take of an answer pays its cost and lands it, and an unaffordable answer is refused; a capture deals the camp's rewards in the same window, before the turn's event, and the one taken is laid in the discard pile; the catalogue refuses an event with no answer costing no stock. Doc-impact: `docs/CHRONICLE.md`.

Player-facing text, every entry of the stand-in's written out. The deal window's title is the event's name from its `event.` entry, and the camp building's name from its `building.` entry for a capture's rewards; the two title sentences `deal.title` and `deal.capstone` go. An event keeps a name and a rules line of its own, static, which the capstone's announcement card reads; an answer has a name, a rules line with its numbers, and its cost, drawn where a card draws its cost. Which keys carry them is the implementer's; the sentences are these:

The entries `event.PH_Raid`, `event.PH_Famine`, `rules.PH_Raid` and `rules.PH_Famine` go with the two events. `victory.PH_Siege` stays.

**Doc-impact:** `docs/CHRONICLE.md`. `docs/GLOSSARY.md` gains no row: the user read the take and the answer as internal naming, so the implementer names them, and _take_ and _answer_ are the words to use.

**Scope:**

- In: the content shape — an event is a record of answers, each with a cost, what its rules line reads, and what it lands; the schedule's per-deal count goes, a due turn dealing one event; a camp names its rewards, one or several. The state's deal becomes a queue of deals, each an event's answers or the camp's rewards, the front one standing. The take names one entry of the standing deal by its place in the order dealt; it refuses no deal standing, a place the deal does not offer, and an unaffordable answer; an answer taken pays its cost, then lands on the chronicle with the deal popped; a reward taken is laid in the discard pile, the others gone; the end of turn stops before the draw while any deal stands, and the take draws only once none does. The capture queues the camp's rewards instead of laying the reward. The catalogue refuses: a schedule entry's event with fewer than two answers, a capstone's with none, any event with no answer costing no stock — every amount it names nought — a camp with no reward or one it does not hold, a deck holding any reward in either section. The deal window: the event's or the camp's name as its title, the answers as faces with their cost and the unaffordable palette, a reward as the card's own face, the refusal note on an unaffordable take, the take by place. The stand-in reworked as in _Spec_. The fixture catalogue reworked with two events of two answers each, one paid answer among them so the unaffordable take is proven, and a camp with two rewards so the one not taken is proven gone.
- Out: the capstone passing on a condition and how the timeline rolls past it (the next board line, which names the page's conflict); the camps' own roll at the enemy phase (the line after); any paid answer or second reward on the stand-in — the unaffordable take and the reward left behind show on the fixture alone until the real content lands; any change to the capstone's announcement beyond its card reading the static line; lore text on the window (an idea).
- Corner cases decided here: two camps captured on the turn an event is due stand three windows in a row, each camp's in tile order, then the event's; a capture with no event due still stops the end of turn on its rewards, the draw waiting on the take. The order dealt is the declared order of the event's answers and of the camp's rewards. The answer's `lands` sees the chronicle with the cost paid and no deal at the front, so a raid landing reads the turn it lands on as today. The capstone's turn still deals the capstone alone, and it too stands behind the rewards of a capture on that turn. A take of an unaffordable answer is one `refused` stage, nothing paid. An answer's cost naming a resource at nought costs no stock. The `deal` stage is raised only where the events phase dealt; the stop before the draw reads the queue, never the stage.

**Traps:**

- `endOfTurn` in `src/rules/chronicle.ts` drops a stage whose chronicle did not change. The captures run before the tick and queue the rewards; on a turn with no event due the events phase changes nothing and raises no `deal` stage, yet deals stand — the stop before the draw must test the queue, not the stages.
- The overlay in `src/ui/overlay.ts` clears its `dealing` on the take and closes the window, and its `render` raises a deal window whenever deals stand and none is held; so after a take's play-out the tail's render raises the next deal's window unasked. The menu's close-back onto a standing deal reads the same variable.
- The hand's refusal note (`src/ui/refusal-note.ts`, wired in `src/ui/hand.ts`) stands on the hand's surface; a note over the deal window must stand above the scrim's depth, as the inspection over a window does. The card face already draws the unaffordable palette from a refusal — hand it one naming the unaffordable resources.
- `src/ui/map.ts` plays the `events` stage as units arriving; a reward taken raises a stage of its own, and that switch's default swallows it — no case to add.
- `costOf` in `src/rules/chronicle.ts` lists a card's cost in the resource bar's order; an answer's cost needs the same listing, so the ordering becomes one helper both read.
- `e2e/chronicle-screen.ts` builds the take by hand in `endedTurn` and presses it by place in `take`; `e2e/deal.spec.ts` searches a seed by reading the standing deal's first entry as an event id, `e2e/camps.spec.ts` takes by `indexOf('PH_Raid')` — both read the answer's place now, which is the first on the stand-in. `e2e/capstone.spec.ts` reads the announcement's card id as the capstone event's, which holds. `e2e/victory.spec.ts` ends turns through the take of place 0, which holds.
- `src/rules/fixtures.ts` builds `dealing({ turn, entries })` and `endedTurn(chronicle, wanted)` where `wanted` names an event to take; every test that names a taken entry goes through there. The fixture's answers have distinct ids across its events, so `wanted` can name an answer.
- `src/content/stand-in.test.ts` asks every event its `reads`, `lands` and `continues` on a launched chronicle; the coherence test now asks every answer of every event, reads every answer's name and rules line, and every reward's name.
- A record's declared order is the order dealt; `Object.entries` keeps it for string keys, and the screen lays the row in it.
