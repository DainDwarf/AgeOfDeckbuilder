# Ideas

The unordered pool of features that may or may not happen. Nothing here is promised, designed, or scheduled, and nothing here is a bug — a defect goes to the user and the board the turn it emerges. An entry is one line, or a few, and gets no elaboration until `/todo` moves it to a [`BOARD.md`](BOARD.md) line and `/intake` designs it. Say _"jot: …"_ mid-task and the thought lands here without derailing the task. Entries untouched for 20 shipped lines go on trial at `/upkeep`.

## Harness

- **Content-stage board** (to try at the Bronze Age: it needs the headless simulator): when content exists, track each piece through Design (number-less) → Implement (provisional numbers) → Balance (measured + feel) → Polish (text, art, lore), one stage per session batched across pieces. Worked well before. Its Design stage is a ping-pong: a piece of content served as two or three takes that differ on purpose, one question a turn, never one finished table, each reason for striking or twisting a take kept on a taste list read before the next serve.
- **A spec for every card of the content**: one e2e test per card, playing it on screen, so a card's effect is proven on the game's content; the catalogue's coherence test plays no card.
- **The warlord embarks**: the warlord crosses the water on its way to a camp or a site across it, embarking and disembarking through the deck's cards where the cheapest way steps onto the coast, so its cell on the archipelago means something: 93 of 100 archipelago camps and 5 of 100 temperate ones lie across the water. The end-of-Stone deck in `decks/` then takes Embark and Fishery, one copy each, left out of it while no persona embarks.
- **A persona weighs the deck before entering a unit**: whether to enter the unit the hand offers now or hold the population a turn for a stronger one the draw pile still holds, read off the piles' counts; a lookahead over turns, which the simulator page forbids a gambit today, so the page changes with it.

## Game

- **Roads, and bridges as a technology**: an improvement that names its tile's movement cost outright, under whatever stands there, placed by a worker on the terrains it reaches; and a river edge with one on both banks crossed as if no river ran there, the bridge gated behind a technology the player unlocks, so roads alone never span a river.
- **The player's units enter beside the city**: a unit card played while a unit holds the city's tile lands the new unit on the nearest free tile around it, through the door the neutral's entries and the camps' walk, instead of being blocked; the two cities symmetrical again.
- **Copy a replay**: one action puts version, seed, deck, the commands played and the last error on the clipboard, so any game a player pastes back can be replayed to the turn.
- **Draw one card out of the top of the draw pile** (the fallback for the card Calendar, if showing the event's turn feels useless in play): a card costing science that offers the top few cards of the draw pile and draws one of them; feared too strong, and showing them alone felt underwhelming in the previous game.
- **Combat log**: a readable record of what the end of turn did — who attacked whom, what was killed, who moved where — for a player who missed the motion or wants it in words.
- **Hand-crafted challenge maps**: once the opening takes a fully generated map, a map written by hand could be one the player launches on, a challenge with a fixed layout. Not committed to at all. The screen only generates its map today, so a door handing it a hand-made one comes first; the e2e specs would walk through the same door.
- **A tile that adds sight**: a layer of a tile, a building or an improvement, adding to the sight of the unit standing on it.
- **A building that adds an entry point**: units enter the map on the city's tile alone; a keyword for a building that opens another door.
- **More in sight on the settle phase**: a technology or a settle card that widens what the settle phase sees beyond the centre part.
- **An in-game codex**: the rules by section, the card kinds and the keywords among them, read in the game; a right click on a card's kind label opens the codex on that kind, and a keyword standing in a card's text, Banish first, stands in brackets and opens on its own — today the label raises a one-line tooltip and the keyword is plain text. The rules a card's text leaves implied are read there and in the tutorial, a stock left short taking one population first: a card says what it takes, and no card text or kind tooltip says what a shortfall costs.
- **A sleep order**: a unit's own state that dims it and keeps it out of the count of units still holding a move until woken or until an enemy comes into its sight; taken only if the count and the jump key do not cure a unit forgotten at the end of a turn.
- **An age filter on the collection screen**: the collection shown by age.
- **Playing an age with a later age's cards** (Bronze Age): earlier ages stay playable, and a deck holds cards of any age, so a Stone Age chronicle played with Bronze Age cards is a walk; whether its rewards, its influence or its unlocks are limited for it, and how. The Nomadic Age is apart: it is the tutorial, and grants no influence on replay.
- **An armour from Tanning** (Bronze Age, with card modifications): a card modification that gives a unit more health or gives armor (new stat), a second reward of the technology Tanning beside its card.
- **A card that takes back from the exhaust pile** (Bronze Age, probably a technology of writing): a card that picks one among the exhaust pile's cards and brings it back into play; or a recall from the discard pile, kept out of the Stone Age for it. Which of the two is v0.0.7's question.
- **A raft that carries units** (Stone Age, if Embark and Disembark feel too cumbersome in play): a unit of its own on the water that takes a unit standing beside it aboard and puts it back ashore, in place of the two cards; naval units are wanted at some point anyway.
- **The neutral's Fishery** (Stone Age, the archipelago): the neutral's workers play Fishery on the coast its city holds, which needs a worker of its embarked; its workers play only what a worker plays ashore.
- **Onboarding opens in the chronicle**: a new player's game starts in the Nomadic chronicle instead of on the meta screens, with tutorial popups.
