# The capstone's window from the ledger

**Line:** The capstone's window from the ledger — a click on the capstone's row at the ledger's top, left or right, raises the capstone's window at any moment of the chronicle, the capstone standing on one panel with everything its text names beside it as cards, and `e2e/capstone.spec.ts` proves it on the game's content.

**Spec:** [`docs/CHRONICLE-SCREEN.md`](../../docs/CHRONICLE-SCREEN.md) _The capstone's window_ and _The pinned achievements_, and [`docs/LOOK.md`](../../docs/LOOK.md) _A panel_, _A button_ and the paragraph on inks. The sentences, written out:

- `docs/CHRONICLE-SCREEN.md`, _The capstone's window_, the whole paragraph becomes:

  > The capstone gets a **window of its own**, raised when the chronicle screen opens, on a chronicle begun and on one resumed alike; when the capstone lands, where the end of turn's play-out ends on it over the hand that turn drew, and its landing plays out once it is closed; and by a click on the capstone's row of the ledger, whenever the player asks. The opening's window closes onto the screen as the chronicle stands, a deal's window rising where a deal waits, and one the row raised closes onto the screen as it stood. Every raising shows the capstone under one title naming it the capstone, on its ribbon at the height a deal's stands at, with the capstone's lore standing under the title as a deal's does: until the capstone has landed, the promise of what passes it and nothing of the turn it lands on; from its landing on, that the time has come. Under the lore the capstone stands on one panel ([`LOOK.md`](LOOK.md)), centred on the screen: its name over its text in a column at the panel's left, drawn as an answer's are and as wide as an answer, and beside the column, in one row and in the order the text names them, everything the text names, each once, the column centred against the row. A card stands there as the landing will add it, drawn as a reward of the deal window is, and as one stack with its count where the text writes a number before its name; anything else the text names stands as the face it wears. The panel runs from the column to a little past the last thing of the row, a stack's count included, and a capstone whose text names nothing stands on its column alone. Its text says what its landing does, a line to each thing where it does several. The capstone is no card: it reads no kind, and no press shows it large. The panel is lighter under the pointer wherever the pointer is on it off the row's cards, as an answer is. The window offers nothing to choose, and the inspection key does nothing while it stands; a left click on the panel closes it, a name in its text and a card of its row included, as the back key and a press beside it do. A name in its text reacts to the rest and the right click as a name on a card does, a card of the row reacts to them as a reward does, and the right click anywhere else on the panel does nothing; what is shown large stands over the window, and a press beside it or the back key takes it down onto the window where it stood, as on the deal. The menu opens over the window and closes back onto it.

The capstone's row reads the capstone's name and, at the right end of its line, where an achievement's count stands, the word naming it the capstone; it is as tall as that one line, and never fainter. It is lighter under the pointer, as an answer is, and a click on it, left or right, raises the capstone's window, as a click on a pile opens its browse; the pointer resting on it raises nothing. An achievement's row reads its technology's name; at the right end of the name's line, the achievement's count over its need, which a need of one does not read; and under the name the goal as the plate reads it, wrapped onto as many lines as it takes, the row as tall as they stand. Once an achievement is reached its row reads the check mark and the name alone, fainter, as tall as that one line, and the rows under it close up. A name in a goal reacts to the rest and the right click as on a plate. An achievement's row reacts to no press; the wheel over the ledger zooms the map, as over the resource bar. The ledger stands on the settle phase and in city mode as on any turn.

**Scope:**

In, as picked on the mockup, https://claude.ai/artifact/BVGzr71GaNa7nbr7JAhKYe, options "Name, with Capstone at the line's end" and "One panel under everything, 16 around the cards":

- **The row.** One line, as tall as a reached achievement's row; the capstone's name in the type and ink an achievement's name wears, `Capstone` at the line's right end in the type and ink a count wears; the same thin rule under it as between any two rows, so every achievement's row now has a rule over it. Lighter under the pointer through the veil an answer wears, at the same strength, over the whole row.
- **The row's presses, a pile's.** Either click raises the window; a left click lets go of a card being aimed first, as a left click on a pile or on the tab does; the rest raises nothing. The row reacts whenever a pile's round does and is dead whenever one is.
- **The ledger stands in every chronicle**, the Nomadic ones included, the capstone's row alone where nothing pinned is read. The tally counts achievements alone. With no achievement on the ledger the folded tab reads no tally and wears its triangle in its middle.
- **The window's panel.** One kit panel, at an answer's rim. The column is an answer's drawing as it stands, name over text, 220 wide with its own inner padding, its names reacting; the panel's left edge is the column's. The row of cards starts 26 right of the column, 26 between two cards, the deal row's own gap. The panel reaches 16 over the cards' top, 16 under their foot and 16 past the last thing the row draws, a stack's peeking copies and its count included: on the mockup the Cold night's count touches the rim at 16, and the shipped panel runs past it. The panel's top stands where the deal row's top stands under the lore. The column is centred on the panel's height. The panel is centred on the screen.
- **What stands in the row.** Each thing the capstone's text names, once, in the text's order. A card is the face a name of it shows, at the counters the capstone's reading hands, as its small card is. A card whose name the text writes a number before stands as one stack of that many copies with its count, as a capture's reward of several copies does. A name of anything but a card stands as its face, once, whatever number the text writes before it; a unit kind wears its faction's sign as its small card does.
- **How the count is known.** From the text as the screen reads it: the run reads the number written just before a name, as it already reads the number written before a glyph. No declaration is added to the content, and nothing in `src/rules/` or `src/content/` changes.
- **The lore, at every raising**, is the promise until the capstone has landed and the landing's from then on, by the rules' own reading of whether it has landed. So a chronicle resumed after the landing opens on the landing's lore, where it read the promise before this line. That window still closes as the opening's does.
- **Closing.** A left click anywhere on the panel, a card of the row included, the back key or a press beside the panel closes the window. One the row raised closes onto the screen as it stood and raises nothing; the opening's and the landing's close as they do today.
- **Under the pointer** the whole panel is lighter while the pointer is on it off the row's cards; over a card, the card reacts as a reward does and the panel is not lighter.
- **A window rising over one the row raised** — a deal's, the landing's, the ending screen — takes its place, as it does a browse's.

Out: an answer keeps its own panel and its bracketed names, on purpose (the reconcile's fourth point); a capstone's count toward its need is shown nowhere new; the minimize button is its own line on the board.

The reconcile, each as chosen:

1. The row's presses go through a pile's as they are; the one difference kept is that the row raises no tooltip.
2. The row's and the panel's lighter look go through the answer's veil as it is.
3. The row's cards go through the deal window's reward drawing as it is, single and stacked; the one difference kept is that a left click closes the window where a reward's is the choice.
4. The capstone's panel and an answer's stay apart, on purpose: what they share is the column, and the panel's width is the difference.
5. Which lore is read goes through the rules' question the yield rule already asks.
6. The capstone's row goes through an achievement's row form; the differences kept are that it is pressable and never fainter.
7. A stack ending the row widens the panel past its count.

**Traps:**

- Nearly every spec opens through `openSaved` (`e2e/chronicle-screen.ts`), which closes the opening's window by a left click on the middle of the object named `capstone-card-0`, and `capstoneClosed` does the same. Whatever carries that name after this line must still close the window under a click on its middle.
- `e2e/pin.spec.ts` holds three assertions this line's design makes false, and changing them is this line's work, not a weakening: every achievement's row now stands under a rule, the first one included; a chronicle reading none of the campaign's pins shows the ledger and its tab with the capstone's row alone; and the ledger's least height, as far under its tab as over it, is now met by the capstone's row alone, no longer by one reached row.
- `e2e/resume.spec.ts` and any spec that reads the opening's lore on a chronicle resumed after its landing now meet the landing's lore.
- The landing's raising holds the end of turn's play-out until it is told the window closed; a raising from the row holds nothing and must tell nothing.
- The ledger made nothing where no pin was read; it now always stands, on a chronicle of an age with one achievement as on any.
- The panel is laid behind its column and its cards on the scrim's stratum, inside what the window's panel scrolls, and the cards' discs rise over their top edge: `docs/PHASER.md` _Layers, Containers and Groups_ for the order, _What the pointer is over_ and _Press, drag and release_ for the row's and the cards' presses, _Under a Playwright spec_ for the new test.
- The mockup's code was a throwaway and is gone; its two hacks are not the design: it named Cold night by its id to stack it, and it read its treatments from the address.
- The widest capstone's panel stands some forty units over the ledger and its tab under the scrim; accepted, not to be fitted.
