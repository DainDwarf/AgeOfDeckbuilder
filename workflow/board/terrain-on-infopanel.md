# The terrain on the infopanel as a card

**Line:** The terrain on the infopanel as a card — the infopanel's terrain card becomes one card face read off the tile, summing up everything on it outside the building and the unit: its terrain, its features, its improvements and the river along its edges. Done when: an inspected tile's terrain card is the terrain's face read off the tile — its gives row the tile's yield without its building's part, a neighbouring building's gift counted, its text naming the feature, the improvements and the buildings giving to it as names that raise their faces, its disc the tile's movement cost, its window the tile as the map draws it without building or unit — the grey paper's ledger and its texts are gone, `e2e/inspect.spec.ts` proves it, and the pages below say so.

**Spec:** settled at intake in prose, no mockup; the look is the terrain's face as shipped by **Every name in a text as a card** (6f4386b), its window carrying the inspected tile.

- `docs/MAP-SCREEN.md`, _The infopanel_, the paragraph opening "The terrain card is headed by the terrain and shows a row per thing it holds…" is replaced whole by:

  > **The terrain card is the face of the terrain the tile stands on, read off the tile**, drawn as a terrain's face is and at the size a card stands at ([`LOOK.md`](LOOK.md)): the terrain's name on the plate, its label reading Terrain. Its row reads what the tile gives at income with everything on it but its building summed — its terrain, its feature, its improvements and each kind of building beside it that gives to it — under the rule the capstone lays over the yield once it has landed; a tile that gives nothing reads no row. Under the row its text names each of those things but the terrain, in that order: the feature, each improvement in the order it was placed, and each kind of building beside it that gives to it, once however many of its kind stand there. A tile nothing crosses, ashore or embarked, reads Impassable as its text. Its disc reads what entering the tile costs in move points — the cost a unit pays, or, where none does, the cost an embarked unit pays — and a dash where nothing crosses it. Its window holds the tile as the map draws it, without its building and whoever stands on it. Its names and its marks react as any face's do.

**Scope:**

- In: the infopanel's terrain card as the terrain's face read off the tile; its gives row, names line, disc and window; the grey paper's ledger (rows, row marks, per-resource tooltips, the Mv corner, No yield, the river's row and its crossing line) removed.
- Out: the grey ghosts behind the infopanel's card, which mark the cards waiting in the cycle and stay as they are; the faces of a terrain, a feature and an improvement named in a text, which stay as shipped; any tooltip on a resource in a card's text (the user: if ever, for every card).
- Settled at intake:
  - The sum is every part of the tile's yield but its own building's: terrain, feature, improvements and each kind of building beside it giving to it, under the capstone's rule from its landing on — the parts today's rows read, summed, one glyph a point in the face's gives row. A tile giving nothing has no row.
  - The text is a line after the summed yield naming, as references, the feature, each improvement in placed order, then each kind of building beside it that gives to the tile, comma-separated; the terrain is on the plate and not named again. A Fishery in range is named as `[building:fishery]`, so a rest shows the Fishery card's face. The river is not named (it has no face) and its crossing rule leaves the card altogether (the user's pick over moving it to the disc's tooltip): the river shows in the window only. A tile nothing crosses reads Impassable as its text, as the terrain's face does.
  - The front is the plain loam oval, a feature on the tile included; the label reads TERRAIN with the shipped kind tooltip; the plate the terrain's name; no cost and no influence.
  - The disc: the tile's own movement cost read as today's corner reads it (ashore, else embarked, else the dash), on the move disc with its shipped tooltip and nothing added for a river.
  - The window: the middle tile lit at its own light with everything the map draws on it but its building and its unit — the terrain's painting (tinted by a tinting feature), the feature's decor (herd, pond) and each improvement's painting laid where the map lays them on a tile carrying no building (so an improvement under a building that fills its tile shows its painting alone), the row's discs and icons as the map draws them (an improvement on a feature turning that feature's disc pale, as on the map), the river along its real edges and the ditches run from it. The two rings round it are the same terrain, dimmed as any card's ground (not the real neighbours); the river runs on over them as far as the window reaches. Nothing centred or enlarged, unlike a feature's own face. The discs show whatever the Icons button is set to.
  - A tile in fog reads the face the map draws, as the infopanel already does.
  - Measured at intake (the game's font at 11 units, wrap 88.4): `[Cattle], [Pasture], [Irrigation]` is 134.0 wide, two lines; `[Fertile], [Irrigation]` 89.9, two lines by 1.5 units; `[Deer], [Trapping]` 80.9, one. With the river's line gone, the crowdest real tile (a cattle plain with a pasture and irrigation) reads the gives row and two lines: three, the room's count.
- Reconcile, chosen at intake (all as recommended):
  1. One face for a terrain: the terrain read off a tile. A terrain named in a text reads a bare tile of it; the infopanel reads the real tile. The gives, the names, the window's middle tile and the tile's movement cost are the parameters.
  2. The window is painted by the art box's own painters (ground, feature decor and disc, an improvement laid alone, river pieces, ditches), as the terrain face's picture is; the meant differences are that it is painted per inspected tile, not at boot, and that the middle tile carries the tile's real contents and real river edges, laid where the map lays them.
  3. The sum reads the same yield parts today's rows read (capstone rule and neighbouring buildings included), the tile's own building's part left out; nothing new is computed.
  4. The names line is a face's text: its names react as any face's do (small card on a rest, large on a right click), a neighbouring building named as a building raising its card's face.
  5. The disc is the shipped move disc reading the tile's real cost, its tooltip the shipped stat tooltip.
  6. The grey paper's ledger and its texts go; the ghosts stay.

**Traps:**

- Every art box picture is painted at boot into the art sheet from what `artSheet` (`src/ui/art-sheet.ts`) enumerates; a picture not listed is not drawn. A tile's picture (terrain × feature × improvements × river edges) cannot be enumerated: it is painted when the tile is inspected, through the same painters, and let go with the card. The unit card's live unit over its picture is the standing precedent for something read live on an infopanel face.
- `groundRiver` in `src/ui/art-sheet.ts` draws a made-up river along the middle row's upper edges for an improvement told by ditches; the tile card's river is the chronicle's own (`chronicle.rivers`), along the tile's real edges, and the ditches run from those edges as the map lays them (`ditchLaying`, `edgesAlong`).
- A tile's yield parts come from `yieldParts` (`src/rules/yields.ts`); its `building` part is the tile's own building (with its own beside gift folded in through `builtYield`) and is the one left out; `beside` parts are the neighbours' gifts, once a kind.
- `terrainFace` (`src/ui/face.ts`) reads `groundCost` for a bare terrain; the tile reads `movementCost` on the tile, through the same `movementCostRead`.
- `facePaintingOn` (`src/ui/marks.ts`) returns the tinted painting where the tile carries a tinting feature, and a building's clearing where it carries a building: hand it the tile without its building.
- The infopanel today stands on `PAPER_WIDTH` × `PAPER_HEIGHT` for its placement beside the tile and its ghosts; the unit and building faces already stand there at `CARD_WIDTH` × `CARD_HEIGHT`, so the terrain card takes their path.
- `e2e/chronicle-screen.ts` reads the ledger (`panel-yield-*`, `panel-movement`, the rows' texts) for `e2e/inspect.spec.ts` and `e2e/irrigation.spec.ts`; `e2e/hover.spec.ts` raises a tooltip from `infopanel-row-0` (that test goes with the tooltip); `e2e/press.spec.ts` clicks `infopanel-row-0`, `panel-row-0-name` and `panel-name`. They follow the face.
