# The overlay is a scene

**Line:** The overlay is a scene — the scrim and what it carries move to a scene above the chronicle screen and below the menu, started ahead of the chronicle scene and restarted with it, standing empty while nothing stands; while anything stands on it the four keys and every wheel notch are its and the pan and zoom keys pass, a scrim rising is the pointer leaving the game to the screen beneath, the resource bar stands under the deal's scrim, and the clip camera moves along untouched; `docs/INTERFACE.md` says so; `e2e/hover.spec.ts` asserts the bar under the scrim, and `e2e/browse.spec.ts`, `e2e/deal.spec.ts` and `e2e/press.spec.ts` pass, the suite on the push. Doc-impact: `docs/INTERFACE.md`.

**Doc-impact:** `docs/INTERFACE.md`.

**Scope:**

In:

- An `overlay` scene, added to the game above the chronicle scene and below the `menu` scene, running with a chronicle: started immediately ahead of the chronicle scene wherever a chronicle is opened — the boot path that opens on the address's deck, the launch page's Launch — and restarted immediately ahead of it by the chronicle scene's new chronicle. It is never put to sleep: it stands empty and awake while nothing stands, its scrim hidden and non-interactive, taking no press, no key and no wheel notch.
- The scene's strata as Layers, created in this order and each object added to its Layer with no depth: `scrim`, holding the scrim rectangle in the scrim's colour and strength; `carried`, holding whatever stands on the scrim — a window's title, its grid's frame and container, the card shown large, the ending screen — created as raised and destroyed as taken down, named as today; `note`, holding the note a refusal raises over a window's card. `DEPTH.scrim`, `DEPTH.onScrim` and `DEPTH.overScrim` leave `src/ui/depths.ts`, and nothing on the overlay scene carries a depth. The refusal note widget stands on a Layer with no depth on the overlay, and keeps its depth on the chronicle scene until the map and UI line.
- The scene's one camera laid out as the console's and the menu's are: zoomed to the render factor, centred on the design's middle, its texts re-resolved on every change of window. The clip camera moves along untouched: a second camera of the overlay scene, its viewport the frame, ignoring every object of the overlay scene but the grid, the note excluded through `raised` as today.
- The pointer barrier, from the one scene-level rule the menu line lands: the overlay scene stops every press, move and wheel that lands on an interactive object of its own, through its input plugin's `stopPropagation`, and never a release. The scrim is interactive whenever anything stands and covers the whole screen, so nothing reaches the chronicle screen while the overlay stands; the grid's frame, the card shown large and the scrim answer their presses as today, on the overlay scene's own input plugin.
- Keys through the overlay scene's keyboard plugin, heard after the console and the menu and before the chronicle scene. While anything stands on the overlay, every bare key press bound to the city key, the yield key, the inspection key or the back key is taken: the city key and the yield key are swallowed, the inspection key shows the ringed card of a browse or of the deal window large and does nothing on anything else, and the back key walks what stands back as today — the card shown large down onto what it was taken off, a browse's ring then the browse, the aim window closed with nothing paid, the deal's ring, the capstone's window closed for good — and, from the deal window with no ring standing and from the ending screen, raises the menu through the scene manager. A taken key has the event's `stopPropagation` and `preventDefault` called. A key bound to none of the four — the pan and zoom keys among them — passes, and the map pans and zooms under the window. A key pressed with Ctrl, Meta or Alt held is never taken. Releases are never taken. While nothing stands the overlay takes no key.
- Mouse keys on the game's emitter follow the same rule by what they bind, marked taken as the menu line marks them. A wheel notch is the exception: while anything stands on the overlay, every notch is taken whatever it binds — it scrolls a standing grid, as Phaser's wheel on the frame does today, and reaches no scene that started later — so the map neither zooms nor pans under a browse being scrolled. A notch's release passes like any release.
- The chronicle scene's `covered` gate on its four keys goes: no key the overlay lets through is gated beneath. The map's `live` stays, fed by the menu's cover alone: a pan key held as a menu window rises keeps panning unless the map puts its keys down, which is what the menu line landed it for; the overlay's cover no longer feeds it, so the map's pan and zoom keys act under a window on the overlay.
- The cover as the pointer leaving the game. The chronicle scene is told of the overlay's scrim rising and falling, as today through the factory's callback, and of the menu's through the announcement the menu line landed, and answers both the same way: it is away while any scrim stands over it, the overlay's or the menu's, and back when the last of them falls. The two cover flags, the map-freeze point and the let-go point the drag line landed in `src/ui/chronicle-scene.ts` are that handler already: the let-go's guard, dead on the overlay path today, is where first-rise-only lives, and the away state is added beside it, not built anew. Going away, it says `gameout` to its own input plugin; coming back, `gameover`. The hover helper in `src/ui/design-space.ts` reads those two events for whether the pointer is away, in place of the input manager's over flag, so every hover on the chronicle screen ends on the rise — a hand card lifted by the hover settles, the end-turn button reads the turn and drops the hand cursor, a tooltip goes down — and resumes on the first move after the fall, as it does for a pointer that left the canvas and came back. An owner resuming a hover while the screen is away, the end-turn button coming live at a play-out's end under the deal window among them, gets no hover.
- The resource bar under the scrim: `src/ui/resource-bar.ts` loses `overScrim`, the depth swap and the press guard with it; the bar stands where it stands while a deal waits, reads dimly through the scrim, and its readings raise no tooltip and take no press until the deal is taken.
- `e2e/hover.spec.ts`: "a reading hovered while the deal window stands raises its tooltip" becomes "a reading hovered while the deal window stands raises no tooltip": the same run, the deal standing, the pointer moved onto the food reading, the tooltip still down past the rest. `e2e/chronicle-screen.ts`, one change in `readNames`: a Layer whose name matches no camera of its scene reads under that scene's main camera, instead of being skipped. Nothing else in the harness and no spec changes.
- `docs/INTERFACE.md`, the two edits above, verbatim. `workflow/BRANCH.md`: the two edits above, verbatim, and the line deleted.

Out:

- The map and the UI: the chronicle scene keeps its two Layers, its two cameras and the depth table's remaining rows.
- The mask: the clip camera and its ignore lists stay, and the note's `raised` with them.
- A tooltip or a small card on the overlay: nothing raises one there yet; the card references line is the first to.
- Any change to what a window offers, how the ending rises or what a press on the chronicle screen does.

Corner cases decided here:

- A hand card the pointer is lifting when a right click shows it large: it settles under the scrim; the card shown large taken down by the back key, the pointer still on the hand card, lifts it again at the pointer's first move and not before.
- The pointer resting on a reading when the deal window rises: the tooltip goes down and stays down after the take until the pointer moves.
- The pointer resting on the end-turn button when the deal window rises at a play-out's end: the button comes back live under the scrim reading the turn, with the arrow cursor; after the take, the first move reads `End turn` on it.
- The menu opened over a browse and closed again: the chronicle screen stays away under the browse's scrim, and no hover resumes until the browse closes too.
- A pan key held when a window rises: it passes and the map keeps panning. A pan key pressed under a browse: the map pans under it. A keyboard zoom key under a browse: the map zooms.
- A wheel notch over the card shown large, over the deal window or over the ending screen: taken, nothing scrolls, the map holds. A wheel notch bound to the city key, under a browse: taken and swallowed.
- The inspection key over the aim window, the capstone's window, a card shown large or the ending screen: taken and does nothing, as today.
- The back key on the ending screen: the overlay raises the menu through the scene manager, and the chronicle scene never hears the key.
- The map's refusal note standing when a scrim rises: it stays under the scrim, as today, and the first press on the chronicle screen after the fall takes it down.
- A chronicle opening — the chronicle scene created, a restart included — finds the overlay scene running and empty, its `create` done, since it started first and a scene with nothing to load creates inside its start.
- New chronicle pressed over a browse, a deal, the capstone's window or the ending screen: the overlay restarts with the chronicle and what stood is gone with it, the new chronicle screen opening on its own capstone's window.
- The console open over a browse: the console takes every key first; Escape closes the console and the browse stays.
- A right click on the browse's frame while its grid is being dragged: Phaser ends the drag there and the fling is lost, the grid stopping where it stands; on a card the right click shows it large and the grid goes anyway, on a gap it does nothing. Decided as it stands: the frame takes no carry of its own.
- A browse or a deal grid being dragged when the menu rises: the let-go the drag line landed reaches the frame's drag end as a release off the canvas, and the grid stops dead with no fling, as on `main`.

**Traps:**

- **Ships on the landed state of the console, menu, Phaser-traps and drag lines**, which the implementer reads at the ship: the start-and-add mechanism in `src/main.ts`; `named` and `counted` in `e2e/chronicle-screen.ts` walking every running scene; `holdDesignSpace` laying out a scene's one camera; `globalTopOnly` off; the menu scene's one-stop-per-scene rule through `gameobjectdown`, `gameobjectmove` and `gameobjectwheel`, and never a release; `readsKeys` and `takesMouseKeys` in `src/ui/keys.ts`, the taken mark on the game's emitter being the mouse-key barrier this line's "every wheel notch are its" is built on; `raiseMenu` and `resetMenu` reaching the menu through the scene manager and announcing its scrim; `letGoOfPress` and the chronicle scene's two cover flags, its `liveMap` and its `scrimRose`; the hand's `carry`, `resolve` and `abandonDrag`. `docs/PHASER.md` holds every Phaser fact below with its citation.
- **The game's emitter runs its listeners in subscription order, not scene start order.** The keyboard plugin's order is the start order because the plugin itself subscribes at the scene's start, whatever handlers are put on it later; a `whileUp` on the game's emitter is ordered by the moment it is called. The menu's taker subscribes from the menu scene's own `create`, so it is ahead of everything a chronicle subscribes. The overlay's widget is built from the chronicle scene's `create`, and the map's own listener on that emitter is subscribed there too: the overlay's taker has to be subscribed before the map's, or a wheel notch under a browse still zooms the map with the mark set too late. However the implementer cuts it, the taker's subscription comes first.
- **The harness skips a Layer with no camera of its name.** `readNames` reads a Layer's contents under `scene.cameras.getCamera(layer.name)`, which answers `null` for a name no camera carries (`src/cameras/2d/CameraManager.js:470-483`), and `named` then passes the place over. Every object on the overlay's three Layers is unfindable until the fallback in Scope lands; a dozen specs fail at their first lookup without it. The map and UI line needs the same fallback.
- **Every scene-plugin call is queued, in order** (`ScenePlugin.js`: `launch`, `restart`, `stop` all `queueOp`), and `SceneManager.start` on a scene that is running, paused or sleeping shuts it down before starting it (`SceneManager.js:1228`). So `launch('overlay')` then `restart(data)` from the chronicle scene lands as start overlay, stop chronicle, start chronicle, and the overlay's keyboard plugin resubscribes ahead of the chronicle's. A scene with nothing to load runs `create` inside its start, so the overlay's `create` is done before the chronicle's.
- **Start order runs opposite to render order.** The overlay renders above the chronicle scene and starts before it; the console line's mechanism in `src/main.ts` already separates the two orders for the console and the menu, and the overlay joins it. The launch page's Launch starts the overlay ahead of the chronicle the same way.
- **`onHover.resume` reads `input.isOver`**, the input manager's own flag, set by the canvas's `mouseover` and `mouseout` alone (`InputManager.js:339`, `:356`); a `gameout` said to a scene's input plugin does not flip it. The helper has to keep the away state itself, from the plugin's `gameout` and `gameover`, which it already listens to. Phaser's per-pointer over list still holds what was hovered when the scrim rose, so no `pointerover` comes after the fall; `resume` already pushes the target into that list by hand for the canvas return, and the same path serves the scrim's fall.
- **`endTurn.live(true)` runs after `paint()` in the play-out's `finally`**, and `paint()` is what raises the deal window: the resume comes after the cover, and only the away state stops it.
- **The chronicle screen's cover has two sources**, the overlay's callback and the menu's announcement, and the menu can rise over a standing overlay window: the chronicle scene says `gameout` on the first rise and `gameover` on the last fall alone, or the menu's fall would resume hovers under the browse's scrim. The overlay calls its callback with `true` at every raise, a browse re-raised from a card shown large among them, while its scrim already stands; the flag already gates the rise. The deal window can rise under a standing menu window, at the end of a play-out the menu rose over: the chronicle stays away, and the let-go's `underMenu` guard is live there.
- **The menu's announcement fires synchronously inside whichever handler raised it**: the overlay's back key on the deal or the ending raises the menu from inside the overlay scene's keyboard handler, and the chronicle scene hears the cover before that handler returns.
- **The map's refusal note hides on its scene's `pointerdown`**, which no press on the scrim reaches any more; the behaviour is unchanged because every raise of the scrim follows a press on the chronicle screen or a play-out's dismiss, but the reason has moved.
- **`Surface` bundles one Layer with one camera** and `surfaceOf` is private to `src/ui/design-space.ts`; the refusal note adds to `on.layer` and the clip reads `on.camera`, while the overlay has three Layers under one camera. However the implementer cuts it, the note lands on the `note` Layer and the clip's `on.camera.ignore(only)` targets the overlay's main camera.
- **The clip camera's `ignore` walks `scene.children.list`** into Layers: on the overlay scene that is the overlay's own objects alone, which is what makes the ignore lists shrink; its `place` runs on the overlay scene's resize, and the overlay's resize must re-resolve its own texts as `applyDesignSpace` does for the chronicle's.
- **`readMouseKeys` prevents the DOM event's default before it emits**; nothing on the overlay may prevent it again, or for the keyboard's sake prevent a key upstream of Phaser.
- **The bar's `overScrim` guards presses too** (`resource-bar.ts:102`): under the scrim no press reaches the readings, so the flag goes whole, not just the depth swap.
- **`named` walks the scenes bottom-first** and answers the first object of a name: `refusal` stands on the chronicle scene and on the overlay, never both at once in a spec, and `refusalLines` reads whichever stands.
- **`e2e/press.spec.ts` "a browse released off the canvas stays open"** relies on `releasedOffCanvas` reading the pointer's `upElement` on the overlay's frame drag; `dragend` fires on the overlay scene's plugin as it did on the chronicle's.
- **The browse's frame drag ends at any button's release**, `docs/PHASER.md` _The pointer's readings_: a right click mid-drag fires the frame's `dragend` with the left button still down, and the fling is lost. Decided in Scope: the frame is moved to the overlay scene as it stands and takes no carry of its own.
- **The overlay's own listeners die with the overlay scene**, not the chronicle's: the widget's `whileUp` calls name the scene they draw on, the input plugin removes every listener at its shutdown (`src/input/InputPlugin.js:3107-3125`), and the restart ahead of the chronicle's destroys the widget's objects before the chronicle's `create` builds them again.
- **The menu line's recall test "the aim window waits under the menu"** opens the menu over the aim window: the overlay's window stays standing under the menu's scrim, and the menu's fall leaves it standing.
- `stopAllMotion(this)` in the chronicle scene's new chronicle stops the chronicle's tweens; the overlay's die with its restart, the ending's rise among them.
- The hook flags a comment block longer than three lines under `src/` or `e2e/`; the overlay's header comment and the clip's comment name the one-scene layout and are cut to their traps.
