# The menu is a scene

**Line:** The menu is a scene — the Menu button, the menu's windows and their scrim move to a scene running on every screen, below the console; while a window stands no key and no press reaches the screen under it, a release excepted; the overlay's kept-aside states for closing the menu back onto a window go; `docs/INTERFACE.md` says so; `e2e/menu.spec.ts`, `e2e/boot.spec.ts` and `e2e/recall.spec.ts` assert it. Doc-impact: `docs/INTERFACE.md`.

**Spec:** [`BRANCH.md`](../BRANCH.md) _The design_: the tower's `menu` row, _The barriers_ paragraph, _What the interface page changes_, _Restart_. `docs/INTERFACE.md` _The menu_ and _What stands over what_, `docs/CHRONICLE-SCREEN.md` the sentence "The menu opens over the chronicle screen as over any screen, and the screen waits under it". The one sentence to add, in `docs/INTERFACE.md` _The menu_, after "Nothing pauses, because nothing runs: the game is untimed, and a menu over the chronicle screen is the chronicle screen waiting.":

- "While a window of the menu stands, no key and no press reaches the screen under it."

No player-facing text entry changes: the windows' titles and buttons read as today.

**Doc-impact:** `docs/INTERFACE.md`.

**Scope:**

In:

- A `menu` scene, added to the game below the `console` scene and above every other, started right after the console and before the chronicle's scenes, never stopped and never restarted, on the mechanism the console line landed in `src/main.ts`; the trap comment there grows by the menu's row. Its display list holds, in this order and on the scene's own display list with no Layer and no depth: the Menu button as today, named `menu-button` with its label; the scrim, a design-space rectangle in the scrim's colour and strength, hidden while no window stands; and the window standing, created as it is raised and destroyed as it is taken down, named as today (`menu`, `settings`, `controls`, and their buttons and slots by their names). `DEPTH.overScrim` loses the button; the row stays for the resource bar until the overlay line.
- The scene's one camera laid out as the console's is: zoomed to the render factor, centred on the design's middle, its texts re-resolved on every change of window. The button stands where the bar's layout stands it today, at the bar's right end, on every screen, the launch page among them; the bar's layout goes on leaving that room.
- `globalTopOnly` off, set where the game is made, with its trap comment. The menu scene stops every press, move and wheel that lands on one of its interactive objects, through its input plugin's `stopPropagation`, from one scene-level rule and not per object; it never stops a release. While no window stands the button is its only interactive object, so everything beside it passes to the scene beneath.
- The menu raised: by the Menu button, by the chronicle scene's back key on a clean chronicle screen, by the deal window's back key with no ring standing, and from the ending screen, as today; the chronicle scene and the overlay reach the menu scene through the scene manager at the press and hold nothing of it. The menu's windows walk as today: a window's buttons open the next, Back and the back key step back one window, a left press on the scrim steps back one window, and the last step takes the scrim down. The Menu button under a standing scrim is not pressable: a press there lands on the scrim and steps back one window.
- The menu window lists **Settings** on every screen and **New chronicle** over a chronicle alone, read from the scene manager at the raise. New chronicle asks the chronicle scene for a new one through the scene manager; the menu holds nothing of the chronicle scene between presses.
- A chronicle opening — the chronicle scene created, a restart included — closes the menu: the window down, the scrim down.
- Keys through the scene's keyboard plugin, heard after the console and before the chronicle's scenes. While a window stands every bare key press is taken: a slot of the Controls window listening binds it, the back key steps back one window, every other key is swallowed; a taken key has the event's `stopPropagation` and `preventDefault` called. A key pressed with Ctrl, Meta or Alt held is never taken. Releases are never taken. While no window stands the menu takes no key.
- Mouse keys and wheel notches follow the same rule, on the game's emitter: while a window stands every one is taken, a listening slot binding it and the rest swallowed, and a taken one reaches no scene that started later; a wheel notch's release passes like any release. The mark that says a press on the emitter was taken is the implementer's to shape; the emitter's listener order is the start order, so the same mark serves the overlay line.
- The menu announces its scrim rising and falling, on the game's emitter; the chronicle scene hears it through `whileUp` and takes its tooltips down on the rise, as it does for its own scrim. The chronicle scene's `covered` state, its `view.live` and its refusal note are not touched by the menu: the screen waits under it as it stands, selection, aim, inspection and note included.
- `src/ui/overlay.ts` loses the window variant of what it carries, `menu`, `binds`, `windowStanding`, `showWindow`, `shut`, and the three kept-aside states as far as they served the menu's closing back: `raisedOn`, `standingDeal` and `capstone` also guard `render` against raising the ending, the deal and the capstone's window twice, and that guard stays in whatever form the implementer picks. `src/ui/resource-bar.ts` loses the button and the `menu` press. `src/ui/chronicle-scene.ts` loses `menu` as a dismissal: the back key's last step raises the menu and drops nothing; the `aimDiscardPile` comment that says the Menu button closes the aim window goes.
- `e2e/menu.spec.ts`: "the Menu button drops the selected tile before it raises the menu" becomes "the selected tile waits under the menu": the city's tile clicked and ringed, the Menu button clicked and the menu standing, the ring still on the city's tile, Escape and the menu gone, the ring still there. `e2e/recall.spec.ts`: "the Menu button stands over the scrim: it closes the aim window and lets the card go" becomes "the aim window waits under the menu": aiming at the pile, the Menu button clicked and the menu standing, the aim window still standing and the chronicle unchanged, Escape and the menu gone, the aim window still standing and the card still selected. `e2e/boot.spec.ts` "the bare address boots the launch page": the Menu button standing on the launch page, clicked, the `menu` window standing with `menu-settings` and without `menu-new-chronicle`, Escape and the window gone.
- `docs/INTERFACE.md`, the sentence above, verbatim. `workflow/BRANCH.md`: the line deleted.

Out:

- The overlay, the map and the UI: the chronicle scene keeps its two Layers, its two cameras, its scrim and everything the scrim carries but the menu's window, and `covered` for its own scrim.
- The resource bar standing over the deal's scrim, and `e2e/hover.spec.ts` "a reading hovered while the deal window stands raises its tooltip": the overlay line, where the bar can no longer stand over a scrim on another scene.
- The back key on the launch page: the page is a stand-in and reads the console line's four keys and no other.
- Any change to what the windows list or how a key is bound.

Corner cases decided here:

- A card dragged off the hand and let go over the Menu button: the release passes, the drag ends on the chronicle scene where it began, and the card lands as a release there does; the button is not pressed, since its press never began.
- The menu raised by the back key while a map drag is held: the release lands on the scrim, passes, and ends the drag; the map has not moved meanwhile, the moves having stopped at the scrim.
- A pan key held when a window rises: its release passes, and the map stops. A pan key pressed under a standing window: swallowed, the map still.
- A middle click bound to the city key, pressed under a standing window: taken by the menu and swallowed, city mode not entered.
- A wheel notch under a standing window: swallowed, the map neither zooms nor scrolls a browse. A wheel notch while a slot listens: bound there, and it reaches nothing beneath.
- Enter on the launch page while the menu stands over it: swallowed, no chronicle opens. Enter on a clean launch page: the chronicle opens as today, and the menu, closed already, stays closed.
- New chronicle pressed over a deal, a browse, the capstone's window or the ending screen: the menu closes with the chronicle opening, and the new chronicle screen opens on its own capstone's window as today.
- Two scrims stand when the menu rises over a deal, a browse, the capstone's window or a card shown large: the screen reads darker there, and no change is made for it.
- The console open over a standing menu window: the console takes every key first; Escape closes the console and the window stays.

**Traps:**

- **Ships after the console line**, on its landed state: `named` and `counted` walking every running scene, keys through the scenes' keyboard plugins with `readsKeyboard` gone, the camera layout of a scene with no Layers shared out of `applyDesignSpace`, and the start-and-add mechanism in `src/main.ts`. The implementer reads those at the ship.
- **Phaser's default stops the release with the rest.** `updateInputPlugins` walks the scenes top-down and returns at the first whose `update` counted an interactive object under the pointer (`InputManager.js:538`), for a down, a move, a wheel and an up alike; a drag begun on a lower scene and released over a higher scene's object never reaches `processDragUpEvent` there, so `dragend` never fires and the next up ends it wherever the pointer then is. With `globalTopOnly` off the walk goes on unless a handler called the plugin's `stopPropagation`, which sets the manager's `_tempSkip` for that one event.
- **One stop per scene, not per object.** With `topOnly` on inside a scene only the topmost hit object gets the event, so a stop on the scrim's own handler is skipped whenever a window's button or slot is over it, and the press would reach the scene beneath. The plugin emits `gameobjectdown`, `gameobjectmove` and `gameobjectwheel` for every hit object with the event container, so one scene-level listener on each is the choke point; `gameobjectup` and `gameobjectout` are left alone.
- **A scrim risen under a resting pointer sends no `pointerout`** to what it covered on any scene, hence the announcement: the chronicle scene's tooltips come down on it, as they do for the overlay's scrim today. The chronicle scene listens on the game's emitter through `whileUp`; the menu never holds a callback or object of a chronicle scene, which goes stale at the restart.
- **The game's emitter has no stop.** Mouse keys and wheel notches arrive there from `readMouseKeys` and every scene's `onKeyDown` hears each; listeners run in subscription order, `whileUp` subscribes at create, so the order is the start order and a restarted scene goes to the back, as the keyboard's. A press taken must be marked so that the listeners after it leave it alone. `readMouseKeys` prevents the DOM event's default before it emits, so nothing here may prevent it again or for the keyboard's sake.
- **Keys are heard in start order, not scene order**, and at boot the start order is the add order unless the scenes are started once booted; the console line settled the mechanism in `src/main.ts` and the comment there says why. The menu is added just below the console and started just after it.
- **`preventDefault` on a taken key** works from the plugin handler because the dispatch is synchronous; the keyboard manager drops an event already prevented, so nothing upstream of Phaser may prevent one.
- **The bar's layout leaves the button's room** (`layOutBar`'s `menu` width, the label's width plus its padding): the button on the menu scene and the room the bar leaves must agree, from one source, however the implementer cuts it.
- **`raisedOn`, `standingDeal` and `capstone` in `src/ui/overlay.ts` double as `render`'s guard** against raising the ending, the deal and the capstone's window twice; the menu's closing-back is what goes.
- **`standing(page, 'menu')` reads the window named `menu`**, and `click(page, 'menu-button')` clicks the button's centre wherever it stands; a spec clicking the button under a standing scrim gets one step back.
- **`e2e/controls.spec.ts` binds keys through the Controls window** with the pointer and the keyboard, rebinds Escape away and binds `b` to back, and measures the map afterwards; it is the spec that proves the slot's take on both paths.
- The hook flags a comment block longer than three lines under `src/` or `e2e/`; the overlay's header comment names the menu and is cut to its trap.
