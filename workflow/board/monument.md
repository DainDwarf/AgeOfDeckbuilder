# The monument

**Line:** The monument — the temperate schedule names the monument beside the long winter: its landing puts Foundations on top of the draw pile, a chain of three stage cards raises the monument through a worker over three plays, one hazard drawn among three is added every turn after the landing, the monument standing is victory on the play and a stage removed is defeat on the spot; the catalogue's coherence test passes and the age page says so. - `docs/ages/STONE.md` _The capstones_, a paragraph after the long winter's: **The monument.** When it lands it adds **Foundations** to the top of the draw pile, the first of a chain of three worker actions, each becoming the next once played: Foundations lays the monument's first stage on plain, hills or desert inside the border for production, **Great stones** raises the second stage over the first for production, more of it, and **Ornaments** finishes the monument over the second for money and culture; a stage stands in the tile's slot and gives nothing. On every turn after the landing one of three hazards, drawn each alike, is added to the discard pile: **Unrest** takes culture while held and costs military, **Thieves** takes money and costs military, **Weary builders** takes production and costs food. The chronicle ends in victory the moment the monument stands, in the middle of the play that finishes it, and in defeat the moment a stage is removed, whatever removes it, since the chain cannot lay the ground again. So the stones are raised over three cycles of the deck at the least while the hazards pile up, and the last stage is paid in what only the tree brings: the trial is whether the city has every resource.

**Traps:**

The fail is a stage leaving the map, read on the change that removes it, never a state of the piles.
- A building card is exhausted by its kind (`goesTo`), so a stage card is an action card, as Trapping and Irrigation are, and its face reads "Worker action"; the Shelter is a building kind and is not the precedent for the stage cards' kind.
