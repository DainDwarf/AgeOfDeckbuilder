# Irrigation

**Line:** **Irrigation** — Irrigation stands in the Stone Age's content as the field's technology needing Agriculture and Herbalism: its achievement counts the Farms built on a tile a river runs along toward 2, unlocks one copy of the card and pays 1 influence; the card places the improvement, giving food, on a plain or a desert tile a river runs along and on no other; a river gives a tile nothing of its own, in the rules, in both ages' content and on the pages; a layer that names the river goes only on a tile a river runs along, and a count of a card's plays on tiles a river runs along, each proven on the fixture; the four doors are declared Herbalism, Agriculture, Trapping, Fire; `e2e/irrigation.spec.ts` plays the card on screen; `npm run check`, `npm test` and `npm run lint` pass. Doc-impact: `docs/MAP.md`, `docs/GLOSSARY.md`, `docs/CHRONICLE-SCREEN.md`, `docs/ages/NOMADIC.md`, `docs/ages/STONE.md`.

**Scope:** In: the river's yield taken out of the rules, the fixture, both ages' terrains and the screen's reading of it; the layer naming the river, in the rules, with its test on the fixture; the count of a card's plays on tiles a river runs along, in the rules, with its test on the fixture; Irrigation's card, improvement, technology and achievement in the Stone Age's content; the four doors re-declared Herbalism, Agriculture, Trapping, Fire; the texts; the improvement's mark; `e2e/irrigation.spec.ts`; `e2e/inspect.spec.ts`'s river test; the pages.

The numbers, provisional and to be played: the card costs 2 production and the improvement gives 1 food, Trapping's price for the same food and Farm's rate; the need is 2, kept low so that one Farm copy bought, at 1 influence, is the whole of what the goal asks of the collection; the technology unlocks one copy and its achievement pays 1 influence. Measured on 200 seeds, a Stone Age temperate map holds 17.4 plain and 2.0 desert tiles a river runs along, and 107 of the 200 hold a plain, forest or desert one within 3 of the centre; a Nomadic map held 16.1 fed tiles, 91 of 200 with one within 3.

Corners decided at intake:

- The river gives nothing in every age: the Nomadic Age's rivers are a crossing and nothing else, and nothing is given to that age in exchange.
- The river's yield leaves the rules, not the content alone: no terrain declares one and nothing reads one.
- Irrigation is an improvement as every other: it cycles, it stacks on a Farm's tile and with any other improvement, it goes inside the border or outside it, and it counts outside through Gather.
- A river never leaves, so nothing removes an irrigation but a terraform into a terrain it does not name, by the standing rule.
- A tile of a terrain the card names with no river along it answers "Needs a river", and so does a tile wrong on both counts: the river's reason comes before the terrain's, after the worker's own.
- A civilization's city building and an age's camp building name no river, refused when the catalogue is built as one naming a feature is: neither is placed through the door that asks for it.
- The goal counts a Farm when it is built on a tile a river runs along, and nothing uncounts it; a Farm built elsewhere counts nothing.
- The rules tests that held the river's food are deleted on the user's order: one food however many edges, one food where two rivers meet, nothing on a terrain the river does not feed, and the terraform ending the river's food.
- A campaign and a chronicle begun before get no care.

What the reconcile chose:

- A card that goes only on a tile with something on it: Irrigation goes through the door Trapping's feature goes through, the river being a second thing a layer names, a building's or an improvement's alike on the user's choice. What differs is meant: the refusal's words, and nothing removing the layer with the river.
- The refusal's words follow "Needs a worker".
- The goal counts each play as Trapping's counts Hunt, and reads the tile as the play was aimed, as Herbalism's reads its terrain; it shares no counting rule with either, and is held by its own test on the fixture.
- The River row of the terrain card stands as it is and reads no yield.
- The mark is a flat polygon of its own, as the trapping's is; the texts take the shapes of Hunt's "or" and of "Build [card:shelter]".
