# The warlord

**Line:** The warlord — `--persona warlord` drives a chronicle through the twenty-four gambits below, `--personas` prints them as written, every command it sends declares no war and moves no unit off a camp or a site, and each gambit of its own has its test on the fixture. Doc-impact: `docs/SIMULATOR.md`.

**Spec:** `docs/SIMULATOR.md` _The persona and the gambit_, one paragraph added after the builder's and before the goal's, and the persona's sentences, which are what `--personas` prints and what the trace names, written out here so the implementer authors none.

> The **warlord** is the stance that fields units and goes for the camps: it settles, staffs, claims and gathers as the builder does and keeps one worker, turns its people into units that attack down to a floor of population, taking them off the fields for it, scouts with them until a camp or a site is charted, then marches all but a small garrison on the nearest as a band, attacking the enemy of least health they reach on the way, and a unit standing on a camp or a site holds it to the capture; it refreshes the move points of the unit furthest out and heals the one that has lost the most. It never embarks and never declares war.

The persona, `warlord`, its gambits in this order, each sentence verbatim and ending in no period; a gambit marked B _n_ is the builder's gambit _n_, the same object:

1. B 1: While a deal stands, take the entry whose trial brings no defeat, no unit acting as an enemy and no card that strikes, of those the one losing the least population and then leaving the most stock together, and where none qualifies the first not refused, one bringing defeat last
2. B 2: On the settle phase, settle on the charted tile the city's building stands on whose yield summed with its six neighbours' is the most, the nearest the centre of equals
3. B 3: On the settle phase, play a settle card that enters a unit on the free tile beside the city that yields most
4. B 4: Pay a hazard in hand the stocks cover
5. While a population is idle and more than three stand, play a unit card whose trial enters a unit that attacks
6. While none is idle, more than three stand and a unit card in hand that enters a unit that attacks is held back by the idle alone, unassign the worked tile that yields the least of everything together, the least food of equals
7. B 5: While a population is idle and the city holds a tile nobody works, assign it to the free held tile that yields the most food, the most of everything together of equals
8. B 6: While a worked tile yields less than a free held tile, reassign the population onto it
9. B 7: While culture covers a claim, claim the tile that yields most among those the city may claim, the nearest a charted tile that yields more of equals
10. B 8: A worker an enemy in sight could reach next turn walks to the reachable tile nearest the city
11. B 9: Move a worker holding its move points to the reachable tile where the best play in hand lands the most, where that beats where it stands
12. B 10: Play through a worker the card that places something, on the tile where it adds the most food to the yield, the most of everything of equals, the nearest the city of equals, one adding nothing still played, never one lowering it
13. B 11: Play through a worker the card whose trial raises the stocks the most, never one whose trial lowers a tile's yield
14. B 12: Move a worker with nothing to play to the reachable tile outside the border that yields most
15. While no worker stands, a population is idle and no held tile is free, play a unit card whose trial enters a worker
16. A unit that attacks, attacks the enemy of least health within its reach, walking to the tile it reaches it from first, the cheapest of equals
17. While no camp and no site is charted, a unit that attacks walks to the reachable tile whose trial charts the most tiles, the cheapest of equals
18. While at least three units that attack stand, a unit that attacks not the nearest the city's tile walks toward the nearest charted camp or site by the cheapest way over charted tiles, to the reachable tile that way weighs least from
19. A unit that attacks that is not marching, the nearest the city's tile or any while fewer than three stand, with nothing to attack walks to the reachable tile within five tiles of the city whose trial charts the most tiles, and charting none, to the free held tile nearest the city's own, never onto it
20. Play a card aimed at a unit whose trial refreshes a unit's move points, on the unit that attacks furthest from the city that has spent them and stands on no camp or site
21. Play a card aimed at a unit whose trial raises a unit's health, on the unit that attacks that has lost the most
22. B 17: Play a card aimed at nothing whose trial raises the stocks' sum or the population
23. B 18: Play a card aimed at the hand on a hazard in hand, else on its first other card
24. B 19: End the turn

No player-facing sentence: nothing of this reaches the screen.

How each gambit of the warlord's own reads, decided here:

- The persona's numbers, three of population, one garrison, a band of two, five tiles, are literals in the persona, not content and not named constants: each stands in one sentence. "At least three units that attack" in 18 is the garrison and the band together; "more than three stand" in 5 and 6 is the floor, the population a unit card leaves being at least three.
- The stay rule is a wrapper over every gambit like the war check: a `move` command for a unit of the player's standing on a tile whose snapshot's building is a camp or a site of the age is dropped as if its gambit had answered nothing; an attack from that tile stands. So 16 attacks from the camp and never walks off it, 18 and 19 move nothing off it, and the unit holds the tile through the enemy phase to the capture.
- Gambit 5 is the builder's unit card reading, the first card in hand order whose trial enters a unit that attacks, read on the unit the trial adds, under its own condition: one idle and the population above three. It sits above the assign so an idle population becomes a unit before a free held tile takes it.
- Gambit 6 reads the rules' refusal of each unit card in hand: the stocks cover it and the blocks are the idle one alone, no `population` and no `city`; and the population above three. It sends the city's assign command on the worked tile ranked lowest by every resource together, then by food, then tile order, which takes that population off the tile. The next decision plays the card through 5; no second unassign comes before it, since 5 sits above 6 and answers while one is idle. The floor ends the pair: at three the condition fails and the assign below takes the idle.
- Gambit 15 is the builder's gambit 13 with its number at one: the settle's worker gathers and is replaced when killed.
- Gambit 16 is the builder's gambit 15 reshaped, the landings an attack may go from its parameter: the builder's are its own tile where held and the held landings, the warlord's are its own tile and every landing. The reading is the same: among the targets in sight within the unit's range from one of those, the least health, ties in tile order; the unit attacks where it stands on such a tile, else moves to the cheapest such landing and the walk restarts, the attack following.
- Gambit 17 is the charting walk the builder's gambit 16 opens with, shared, the radius its one parameter and none here: among a unit's landings, the one whose move's trial charts the most tiles for the first time, the snapshots grown, at least one; ties the cheapest, then tile order. It answers for every unit that attacks holding move points while no snapshot's building is a camp or a site of the age.
- Gambit 18 reads the targets off the snapshots: a tile whose building the rules name a camp or a site, never the neutral's city; a camp or a site in fog is read as the player last saw it. The nearest is by the unit's own walk over the whole map with the player's passage, ashore only, uncharted tiles shut and the neutral's land an end, ties in tile order; a target no such walk reaches is not nearest. The unit then moves to the landing, among its own tile and the ones the rules give it, that the cheapest route to the target weighs least from, only where strictly less than its own tile's weight; ties the cheapest landing, then tile order. The garrison is the one unit that attacks nearest the city's own tile, ties in tile order, and marches only when it is no longer the nearest; the band marches while three or more stand and falls back through 19 below that.
- Gambit 19 is the builder's gambit 16 reshaped, the units it governs its parameter: the builder's every unit that attacks, the warlord's the ones 18 does not march, the garrison and every unit while fewer than three stand. Its charting half is the shared walk with the radius at five; its rest half is the builder's as it stands.
- Gambit 20 plays, among the cards in hand aimed at a unit the refusal lets through, the first in hand order whose trial on a unit of the player's leaves that unit with more move points, on the unit that attacks furthest from the city's tile whose move points are below its move and whose tile's snapshot holds no camp or site; ties in tile order. Gambit 21 plays likewise the card whose trial leaves a unit with more health, on the unit that attacks whose health lies furthest under its full health; ties in tile order. Both read the trial and never the card.
- On the settle phase every gambit from 4 on may be asked; whatever the rules refuse is dropped.

The reconcile: the deal, the settle, the hazard, the assigns, the claim, the worker's gambits, the gain and the discard go through the builder's gambit objects as they are; the attack and the garrison walk become one function each with the difference as the parameter, the builder's reshaped in this line; the charting walk becomes one helper with the radius its parameter, both personas reading it; the unit card's reading goes through the builder's, each gambit's condition its own; the walk toward a camp stays apart from the enemies' script's walk, meant, the player's knowledge, and nothing moves out of `src/content/scripts.ts`.

**Traps:**

- The driver drops a command whose chronicle is the one it started on, restarts the list from the top after every command landed, and stops a turn at a hundred commands. Every move above is "strictly" nearer or less for that reason, and 6 never unassigns twice before 5 plays: its condition reads the refusal, which the unassign lifts.
- A unit card's blocks are `population` for a city that would keep none, `idle` for none idle, and `city` for a unit standing on the city's tile, `enters` in `src/rules/cards.ts`; its cost is read beside them through `refusalOf`. The city's `assign` command on a worked tile takes the population off it, `src/rules/city.ts`; the unassign is that command.
- `passageFor` in `src/rules/units.ts` already keeps the player's walk off uncharted tiles and ends it on the neutral's land at peace, so the warlord's own walks go over `chronicle.tiles` with the player's passage; the builder's gambit 8 builds a `known` chronicle because it reads an enemy's walk, whose passage is open. `routesFrom` and `routesToward` in `src/rules/map.ts` take `Moves`; one naming `ashore` alone never crosses water, so an island camp on the temperate map is unreached and never nearest.
- A camp or a site is read off a snapshot's `tile.building` through `captureOf` in `src/rules/catalogue.ts`, which answers `camp`, `site` or `neutral-city`; the last is no target. A snapshot in fog may be stale, a camp the neutral has since captured among them; the walk goes there and charts it again on arrival.
- `reachable` offers no tile a unit stands on, so a camp's tile is a landing only once its guard is dead; a capture lands when the next turn begins with the unit still standing there, `captures` in `src/rules/chronicle.ts`, and the deal it raises is taken by gambit 1. `byHand` answers no landings and no targets on the settle phase.
- `spentAction` in `src/rules/units.ts` zeroes a unit's move points: a unit that has attacked holds none, so 17, 18 and 19 skip it that decision; a marching unit may hold points after 18 moved it, which is why 19 governs only the units 18 does not.
- A `unit` aim's admitted tiles are those a unit of the player's stands on, `admitted` in `src/rules/chronicle.ts`; the refresh is refused on full move points, `move-full`, the heal on full health, `health-full`, and the heal's own aim keeps it inside the border. `fullHealth` in `src/rules/catalogue.ts` gives a unit's full health.
- The builder's helpers in `tools/builder.ts` are private today: the ranking, the trial, the war check, the tile order, the unit and move helpers. The warlord shares them from one module and re-copies none; where that module is, is the implementer's cut. A tool imports `src/rules/` and never `src/content/scripts.ts`.
- The row has no column for captures: a capture is read off `deals` as the building captured, the reward taken and the turn, so the hand-back's numbers come from the skill's listing query over `deals`.
- A unit resting on the city's tile blocks every unit card; the builder's rest never lands there, and the warlord's 19 is the same rest.
- The builder costs about 3 s a chronicle on the Stone Age, 18 at worst, measured 2026-10-21 over 20 seeds; 18 runs two route walks over the whole map for each marching unit on each decision, a few milliseconds each on a 470-tile disc.
- `tools/sim.test.ts` holds the idle's, the driver's and the builder's tests on the fixture; the fixture in `src/rules/fixtures.ts` holds a camp, `camped`, unit cards and enemies; a gambit's test is one choice a player could state, on fixture numbers, never on the game's content.
- Prettier keeps every markdown paragraph on one line; `npm run lint` refuses a wrapped one.
