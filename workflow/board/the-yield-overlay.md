# The yield overlay

**Line:** **The yield overlay** — the overlay reworked with the glyphs over the map: its dim, whether the dim lies over the selection's marks, and whether the yield glyphs stand over the path and the units. Done when the overlay lays no dim over the map, every tile's yield glyphs stand 8 units a side and touching, four to a row, on a soft dark patch fitted to them, over the units; the plate of the yield an aimed card would give sets its glyphs touching; `docs/LOOK.md` and `docs/MAP-SCREEN.md` say so; and `e2e/yields.spec.ts` passes.

- The user may redo the look after seeing it react to inputs in the game ("I'd need to see it in game with it reacting to inputs to be sure"): the line ships as picked.
- Corner cases decided: a tile shows a patch only where it shows a glyph, fitted to the glyphs shown, so one resource latched alone gets a patch as wide as that resource's glyphs; the tile wearing the culture threshold wears neither glyph nor patch, as it wears no glyph today; a tile in fog wears its glyphs and its patch undarkened; city mode's glyphs inside the border, with the overlay off or on, are the same glyphs on the same patch; every patch lies under every glyph, so a neighbour's rim never covers a tile's glyphs; a tile of more than eight points takes a third row by the same rule; the patch answers no press.
- Reconcile, each chosen by the user as recommended: (1) the monument's patch and the yield's stay two things to the player, the monument's 42 by 30 in dark earth at 90% under its building and darkened by fog, the yield's fitted to its glyphs in near-black at 70% over the units and never fogged, the two sharing only the fade, full to six tenths of the radius then falling evenly to nothing; (2) the glyphs on the aimed card's plate and the overlay's become one spacing, touching, the plate's 1.5 units between two glyphs going, so a plate of three holds 24 units of glyphs where it held 27; (3) the dark plate under the culture threshold and the aimed yield stays as it is and stays apart from the patch, a hard plate for what a press would change and a soft patch for what the land gives, the plate standing over the patch on a tile that wears both.
- Numbers, the user's picks on the page: glyph 8 units, no room between two glyphs across or down, four to a row, on the tile's middle; the patch near-black `0x0d1014` at 0.7, reaching 6 units past the glyphs' block on every side, so 20 by 20 under one glyph, 44 by 20 under a row of four, 28 tall under two rows. The patch's colour is a look entry of its own, named for what it is.

**Traps:**

- The dim's rectangle is handed to the map's camera, which places and sizes it at every frame change (`createMapCamera`, `src/ui/map-camera.ts`), and it is the one thing on the `dim` stratum of `MapStrata` (`src/ui/map-scene.ts`); `e2e/map-order.spec.ts` lists `yield-dim` in the order it asserts.
- Five specs read `yield-dim` shown as "the overlay is on": `e2e/yields.spec.ts` throughout, `e2e/map-order.spec.ts`, `e2e/fishery.spec.ts`, `e2e/fog.spec.ts`, `e2e/city-mode.spec.ts`. What still says it on screen is the bar's latched readings, which `yields.spec.ts` already reads through its `latched` helper, and the glyphs.
- The patch's size varies by tile while the monument's is one frame at one scale: `asPainted` sets a scale back to one (the comment over the patch's image in `src/ui/map-marks.ts`), and a frame painted into a canvas sheet uploads the whole sheet (`docs/PHASER.md`, _A canvas texture's `clear` and `draw`_; `outlineOf` in `src/ui/glyphs.ts` shows the one-upload-a-frame pattern).
- The glyphs sheet packs one outline frame for every symbol and square asked for and throws when full (`GLYPH_SHEET`, `src/ui/marks.ts`): a yield glyph at 8 units asks for the frames the threshold's glyph already asks for.
- Glyphs and patches come and go in numbers with every render: pooled, hidden and reused (`DOGMAS.md` _Stack_), as the glyphs are today.
- `e2e/aimed-yield.spec.ts` reads a plate's glyphs by file and colour, not by place, so the plate's new spacing moves nothing in it.
