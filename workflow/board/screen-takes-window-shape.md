# The screen takes the window's shape

**Line:** The screen takes the window's shape — the chronicle screen laid out to the window's shape at 100%, or at the largest size holding 1280 by 720 units in a smaller window, its pieces keeping to their edges and the map filling the rest, the windows on the scrim and the menu's centred with the scrim over the whole window, the meta screens fitted as today.

Done when: the chronicle screen is laid out on a design space of the window's shape, one unit drawn across one pixel of the window as the system scales it (a CSS pixel), or at the largest size that holds 1280 by 720 units where the window is smaller; the bar and the band span the window; the pinned achievements, the Icons button and the draw pile keep to the left edge, the hand to its lane between the piles, End turn and the discard and exhaust piles to the right edge, Menu to the top right; the map's frame runs the window's width between the bar and the band; the scrim covers the whole window and what stands on it is centred on the window; the campaign, launch and collection screens stay one 1280 by 720 design drawn as large as the window's shape allows, the page's colour bare around them; the menu and the debug console take the shape of the screen under them; `e2e/window.spec.ts` proves it, and the suite is green on the push.

**Spec:** `docs/INTERFACE.md` → _The window_, written at the cut and quoted here as the contract:

> The screen is laid out in units, 1280 by 720 at the least, and drawn at the window's own pixels, every line, glyph and letter painted at the window's pixel density, so the game is as sharp as the screen it stands on at every size. The chronicle screen takes the window's shape: a unit is drawn across as many of the window's pixels as the interface size says, the bar, the hand, the piles and the buttons keep to their edges, and the map fills what they leave. Where the window cannot hold 1280 by 720 units at that size, the interface is drawn at the largest size that holds them. A window on a scrim stands centred at that size, its scrim over the whole window. The meta screens are one design of 1280 by 720 units, drawn as large as the window's shape allows, the page bare around them. A window smaller than 1280 by 720 of the screen's own pixels shrinks the game below what its type was sized for ([`LOOK.md`](LOOK.md)). It follows every change of the window while the game runs — a resize, the browser's zoom, fullscreen entered or left, a move to another monitor — and lays the screen out again for it.

The interface size is 100% throughout this line; its Graphics row is the next line. No player-facing sentence is added.

**Doc-impact:** `docs/PHASER.md` — _The scale manager_'s first bullet ("the game keeps the backing store at the canvas's on-screen size in device pixels, which `Scale.FIT` fits by the same rule") and _Under a Playwright spec_'s "Screen coordinates convert through the canvas's client rect" bullet ("at a viewport of 1280 by 720 alone is a mouse coordinate the design coordinate one to one") restated for what the code then does: at 100% a mouse coordinate is a design coordinate on the chronicle screen at every window that holds 1280 by 720 CSS pixels. `docs/INTERFACE.md` already carries the design.

**Scope:**

- In: the canvas filling the window at every size; the interface size as one value, 100%, read in one place, so the next line sets it; the size a unit is drawn at, the interface size capped by the largest size that holds 1280 by 720 units (`min(size, innerWidth / 1280, innerHeight / 720)`, in CSS pixels; the device factor is that times `devicePixelRatio`); the chronicle screen's scenes (`map`, `ui`, `overlay`) laid out on the window's shape and laid out again on every change of the window; the menu and the console taking the shape of the screen under them; the meta screens fitted as today.
- Out: the Interface size row (next line); laying the bar, the piles or the windows out anew for a wider screen (the glyph bar's, the piles' and the windows' lines); a desktop build's windowed or borderless modes.
- **One rule for every placement** on the chronicle screen and on the menu and console over it: what is placed against the middle of 1280 by 720 is placed against the middle of the window's design space, and what is placed against an edge, against that edge. So:
  - The bar's panel and its paper span the window; its seven readings keep today's places, laid over 1280 units from the left as now; Menu stands at the top right of the window.
  - The band spans the window. The draw pile and the Icons button keep to the left edge; the discard pile, the exhaust tab and End turn to the right. The hand's lane runs between the piles where they stand, so a crowded hand spreads wider on a wider window, the advance still capped as today.
  - The map's frame is the window's width between the bar and the band; the pinned achievements stack down its left from its top as now; the mode's frame and chip follow the frame.
  - The scrim covers the window. A browse keeps its columns, centred, and runs to the window's bottom; the deal window, the capstone's window, the ending screen, a stack of cards shown large and the menu's windows are centred on the window. A tooltip, a refusal's note and a small card keep clear of the window's edges as they keep clear of the design's now. The aim's line stands centred over the band as now.
  - The debug console's panel spans the screen under it.
- **The map scales with the interface** (the reconcile's call): a tile stays 42 units across at zoom 1; the zoom's limits, its notch and the frame's bounding stay in units as now. A change of window keeps the point at the frame's middle where it is, bounded as `place` bounds it today.
- **The meta screens** stay one 1280 by 720 design, centred, drawn at the largest size that fits, nothing of theirs drawn outside it (the campaign screen's tree reaches past its right edge today), the margin the page's colour and reacting to nothing.
- **A window smaller than 1280 by 720 CSS pixels** draws the chronicle screen at the largest size holding 1280 by 720 units, the design space taking the window's shape at that size (a 1000 by 900 window is 1280 by 1152 units).
- **Reconcile:** the cap is today's fit, `renderFactor`'s `min`, one calculation the interface size caps; the meta screens hold their design as `holdDesignSpace` holds it now; the menu's Graphics rows are untouched here.
- A resize mid-motion: a card in flight or dragged lands, or comes home, where its target stands after the resize. The implementer may pick how; it is reported.

**Traps:**

- `docs/PHASER.md` _The scale manager_: the scale manager refits the canvas, not the backing store; `scale.width`, `scale.height` and a pointer's `x`, `y` are backing-store device pixels; it hears `resize` and `fullscreenchange` and nothing of the pixel ratio (`followPixelRatio` covers that). `src/ui/design-space.ts`'s comment above `onResize`: the resize `followWindow` triggers emits RESIZE again nested inside the one being handled, so a placement reads the window live, never the event's arguments.
- `docs/PHASER.md` _Cameras_: Phaser's camera manager resizes only the cameras that filled the old canvas (`src/ui/map-camera.ts`'s `place`); a camera cut to a box — the map's, and a meta screen's if its viewport is cut to its fitted design — is cut again by hand on every resize. A new scene's camera paints one to one until zoomed.
- Many places are module-level constants computed once at import from `DESIGN_WIDTH` and `DESIGN_HEIGHT`: `MAP_FRAME` and `BAND_TOP` (`band.ts`), `TAB_EDGE` and `PILE_PLACE` (`piles.ts`), `CARD_BASELINE` (`card-face.ts`), the hand's `laneLeft` and `laneWidth` (`hand.ts`), `RIGHT` (`launch-screen.ts`, a meta screen, stays), `ROW_RIGHT` (`menu.ts`). Today a resize moves nothing but the scale, so most surfaces lay out once at create: everything placed from these is placed again on a resize now.
- `positionOf` in `src/ui/map-marks.ts` centres the map's lattice on `DESIGN_WIDTH / 2, DESIGN_HEIGHT / 2` of the map's world, not of the screen: it does not move with the window.
- `e2e/hover.spec.ts` and `e2e/press.spec.ts` (17 tests) open a 1280 by 900 window "so the canvas letterboxes and bare page is left to release on": that bare page is gone from the chronicle screen, and from inside the canvas everywhere once the canvas fills the window. Those tests release off the canvas another way; `letGoOfPress` in `src/ui/design-space.ts` shows how Phaser reads a release off the canvas (a window `mouseup` whose target is not the canvas).
- `e2e/window.spec.ts` asserts the backing store equals the canvas's on-screen size at 640 by 360, 1280 by 720 and densities 1.5 and 2; it keeps holding.
- `docs/PHASER.md` _Under a Playwright spec_: a spec cannot import Phaser, nor a module reaching it (`design-space.ts` does); a constant a spec needs lives in a Phaser-free module. A camera takes its zoom and scroll at render: rest a frame before reading a place.
- The e2e window is 1280 by 720, where 100% draws exactly today's screen: specs reading places by design coordinates hold there.
- `resolutionAt` and `dragDistanceThreshold` read `renderFactor()`: both follow the device factor the interface size makes.
