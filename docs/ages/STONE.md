# The Stone Age

> What the Stone Age is made of: its land, its city, its neutral, its technologies, its units, its cards, its events, its capstones, its achievement and its camps. A content page under [`DESIGN.md`](../DESIGN.md)'s legend. The rules it is played by are [`CHRONICLE.md`](../CHRONICLE.md)'s and its map [`MAP.md`](../MAP.md)'s, and this page repeats none of them; no number stands on it. The terrains, the features, the biomes and the sites it shares with the Nomadic Age are [`NOMADIC.md`](NOMADIC.md)'s; what its sites deal here is this page's.

## The land ✅

The Stone Age's camps stand across the water, in every one of its regions: a camp may stand on an island, wherever a unit comes from the centre by embarking.

The Stone Age's temperate region is the Nomadic one on a bigger disc, with a desert on it: the Nomadic terrains and features in the same shares, the Nomadic biomes cut as fine, more of each of them, and rivers rising in its ranges. The ground around the centre is the same biome of its own, and the centre part reaches as far, so the settle chooses among as many tiles. More camps stand on it, kept as far from the centre and from one another as the Nomadic ones, so the nearest is as near and the rest lie further out.

The desert is a biome of its own, desert nearly throughout with hills among it, rolled rounder than the land and kept away from the seas. A desert tile gives nothing, lies flat and is walked as plain is. A city and a camp stand on it. The oasis, its feature, gives food and is dealt as rarely as the others. So the desert is ground to cross, and its oases and the river banks Irrigation goes on are what is worth the walk.

| Terrain | Gives   | Feature   | Gives |
| ------- | ------- | --------- | ----- |
| desert  | nothing | **oasis** | food  |

The archipelago is the age's second region, the one Raft unlocks: a disc as big as the temperate one, of islands in a sea. The ground around the centre is the temperate region's own, the same biome dealt to the same size, an island here. The other islands are a biome of their own, the land's terrains dealt to a size and rolled rounder than the centre's, and two dealt side by side are one island. Between them lie the shallows, coast throughout and grown in arms, and the open sea, ocean throughout behind a coast rim as wide as the temperate sea's, with no island in it. No mountain range and no desert is dealt, so no river runs there and no oasis lies there; the other features lie in the temperate shares. The centre part reaches as far, and as many camps stand on it as on the temperate region, kept as far from the centre and from one another.

## The sites 🔧

The sites are the Nomadic Age's, and in this age each deals two rewards of the age's own, so a site found is a choice; a site's trophy gains influence, as the camp's do. The **painted cave** deals **Cave paintings**, a trophy, culture once, more than the Nomadic card gives, and **Old stories**, an instant, culture each time it is played, cycling with the deck for the rest of the chronicle: the find spent or kept against the find told again. The **old cairn** deals two trophies, **Honour the dead**, culture once, more than the Nomadic card gives, and **Dig the graves**, money once, worth something to a deck that trades and nothing to one that does not: the dead honoured against the dead robbed. The **bones of a great beast** deal **Bone carvings**, a trophy, culture once, more than the Nomadic card gives, and **Bone tools**, an instant, production each time it is played, cycling with the deck for the rest of the chronicle: the beast remembered against the beast used up.

## The player's city 🔧

The **village** is the player's city's building Settlement unlocks in place of the hearth, so every chronicle after the Nomadic Age's victory is played on it, a Nomadic one replayed among them. It gives **military and culture** on top of its terrain and nothing else, and stands on desert as on plain, forest and hills.

## The neutral 🔧

The neutral is another band that settled: a city in every one of the age's regions, standing a little further out than the centre part and no further than the camps may, with the camps and the sites kept from it. Its building is a **village** of its own, standing where the village stands and giving what the village gives, military and culture, so its border and its warriors have their sources. Its units are the age's own, warriors, archers and workers, entered out of its population and its stocks as the player's unit cards enter them, on its village or beside it, and kept near a ratio of warriors to archers to workers its content names, the kind whose share falls shortest entered first and the warrior of equals, and it opens with a worker and warriors standing guard; its script keeps it growing and sends its warriors at the camps: it claims the tile that yields most among those it may claim, the nearest to its city of equals. It works the tiles that pay its upkeep first, then the tiles that feed it: its population stands on the tiles its city holds, for each resource its upkeep takes the tile giving the most of it, until the income covers the upkeep or no tile left gives any, then the tile giving the most food and the most of everything together of equals, as many tiles as it has population; a population with no tile to work is idle, the one its next unit is made of. Its units follow one of three scripts. **Guard** keeps the tiles its city holds: it attacks an enemy it can reach from inside the border, walks inside it toward one it sees, otherwise wanders inside, rests on its city's own tile only while no other tile it holds is free, and never embarks. Once enough guards stand, some leave together as a **wave** for the nearest camp, crossing the water as the camp's enemies do and attacking the enemies they can reach on the way; with no camp it can reach, a wave unit stands where it is. A **worker** builds: it goes for the tile its city holds where one of its cards — Farm, Clay pit, Irrigation, Pasture and Trapping — adds the most to the yield, food first and everything together of equals, the nearest its city of equals, and plays it there, never one that takes a resource's income, what the tiles its population then works yield less the upkeep, below nothing or lower while it is; with nothing to play it rests as the guard does, off its city's tile while another is free. Hostile it works on, and of no city it stands where it is. Hostile, guard and wave alike go for the player's city as the raider does, attacking what they can on the way and preparing the capture on its tile. Of no city at peace, a guard stands where it is and attacks what it can, and a wave goes for the camps still, its own fallen village among them.

Its fall to the player deals three trophies, each several copies of one card, and the player chooses one: **Capture**, the camp's own, one population a copy, the village's people taken in; **Their land**, a tile the player's city may claim taken into the border for nothing, the village's land taken; **Their stores**, some of every core resource. The tiles the fall frees are the rest of the pay.

## The technologies 🔧

A goal is a focus themed on its technology: it is read from the technology's fiction and asks for more than a chronicle played as usual gives.

- **Herbalism** needs Settlement. Its goal counts a deed: the kinds of terrain Gather is played on, each read as the tile stands when the card is played, a Gather that gains nothing counted like any other. It unlocks the card **Heal** and pays influence.
- **Agriculture** needs Settlement. Its goal reads the chronicle as it stands: the plain tiles inside the border, the player's city's own tile counted when it stands on plain. It unlocks the card **Farm** and pays influence.
- **Trapping** needs Settlement. Its goal counts a deed: the times Hunt is played, each a deer or a cattle removed from its tile. It unlocks the card **Trapping** and pays influence.
- **Fire** needs Settlement. Its goal counts a deed: the turns on which as many cards were played as a hand is drawn to, a hazard paid for among them, each turn once however many more are played, and the settle phase never. It unlocks the card **Fire** and pays influence.
- **Irrigation** needs Agriculture and Herbalism. Its goal counts a deed: the times Farm is built on a tile a river runs along. It unlocks the card **Irrigation** and pays influence.
- **Domestication** needs Trapping and Agriculture. Its goal counts a deed: the times Gather is played on a tile carrying cattle, the same tile as often as a worker gathers there. It unlocks the card **Pasture** and pays influence.
- **Bow and arrow** needs Trapping. Its goal counts a deed: the units killed by a Scout's attack. It unlocks the card **Archer** and pays influence.
- **Pottery** needs Fire. Its goal counts a deed: the production gained from hills tiles, at income and through Gather alike. It unlocks the card **Clay pit** and pays influence.
- **Calendar** needs Irrigation. Its goal counts a deed: the times the draw pile is emptied, its last card drawn. It unlocks the card **Calendar** and pays influence.
- **Bread** needs Irrigation. Its goal reads the chronicle as it stands: the player's city's population, idle and assigned alike, a unit never counted. It unlocks the card **Bread** and pays influence.
- **Tanning** needs Domestication. Its goal reads the chronicle as it stands: the tiles inside the border carrying a Pasture or a Trapping, the player's city's own tile counted when it carries one. It unlocks the card **Tannery** and pays influence.
- **Raft** needs Bow and arrow. Its goal reads the chronicle as it stands: the coast tiles charted, the centre part's among them. It unlocks the card **Embark** and the archipelago region and pays influence.
- **Megalith** needs Calendar. Its goal reads the chronicle as it stands: how far the furthest tile inside the border lies from the player's city's own, counted in tiles. It unlocks the card **Megalith** and pays influence.
- **Bartering** needs Tanning and Bread. Its goal reads the chronicle as it stands: the money in the player's city's stock. It unlocks the cards **Food trade** and **Goods trade** and pays influence.
- **Fishing** needs Raft. Its goal counts a deed: the food gained from coast tiles, at income and through Gather alike. It unlocks the card **Fishery** and pays influence.

## The units 🔧

- **The archer** attacks over a range the warrior does not have and holds less health: it is the unit that attacks from behind another.
- **The leader** holds more health and more damage than the warrior and carries a bonus: the units of its band beside it hit harder. No card enters it; it comes with the siege.

## The cards 🔧

- **Farm**, a building on plain giving food, built through a worker for production: the copies a deck holds are how many farms the player's city builds.
- **Trapping**, an improvement giving food, placed through a worker for production on a tile carrying deer and nowhere else. It goes with the deer, so a Hunt played there removes both. An improvement counts wherever the tile is — inside the border at income, outside it through Gather.
- **Fire**, an instant costing nothing, aimed at another card of the hand: that card is discarded and the player's city gains science. A hazard discarded so does not strike that turn, and comes around again. Science has its source here, and Calendar is what costs it.
- **Heal**, an instant costing food, aimed at a unit standing inside the border, the player's city's own tile included: the unit is healed to full health and keeps its move points and its action, and a unit at full health is refused. Nothing else in the age heals.
- **Irrigation**, an improvement giving food, placed through a worker for production on a plain or a desert tile a river runs along and nowhere else.
- **Pasture**, an improvement giving food, placed through a worker for production on a tile carrying cattle and nowhere else. It goes with the cattle, so a Hunt played there removes both.
- **Archer**, costing military: one population leaves the tiles to become a unit.
- **Clay pit**, an improvement giving production, placed through a worker for production on hills and nowhere else.
- **Calendar**, an instant costing science, aimed at nothing: the turn of the next event is shown until that turn comes, the capstone's turn read as an event's where it lands first, and the card is refused while a turn is shown.
- **Bread**, an instant costing food, aimed at nothing: the player's city gains culture. Culture has its second source here, after that city's own tile, so food is kept for the next population or paid toward the next claim.
- **Tannery**, a building giving money, built through a worker for production on a tile carrying deer or cattle and nowhere else. It goes with the feature, so a Hunt played there removes both. Money has its source here.
- **Embark**, an action card costing production, aimed at a coast tile beside a unit: the unit embarks onto it, spending its action. It becomes Disembark. An embarked unit crosses the coast slowly, whatever it was ashore, so the copies a deck holds are how many units are on the water at once, and the deck's cycle is how long a crossing takes.
- **Disembark**, the action card Embark becomes, costing nothing, aimed at a tile beside an embarked unit that the unit stands on ashore: the unit disembarks onto it, spending its action. It becomes Embark.
- **Megalith**, a building on plain, hills or desert giving culture, built through a worker for production. Culture has its third source here, the one the player's city builds.
- **Food trade**, an instant costing money, aimed at nothing: the player's city gains food.
- **Goods trade**, an instant costing money, aimed at nothing: the player's city gains production. Money is paid in the two trades, each for one good, so which good a deck's money goes to is chosen when the deck is built.
- **Fishery**, a building built through an embarked worker for production on a coast tile. It gives food to its own tile and to every coast tile beside it, once however many stand around a tile, so it pays by the coast the player's city works around it.

## The events 🔧

The temperate region's schedule is its own: its deals come closer together than the band's and its capstone later. It deals A rival band and The herd as the band knew them, Wildfire with a third answer of the age's own, and Departure, this age's for good, culture being its answer's price; Lean season is the band's alone. The age's own events are the settled city's problems: what the river, the sky, the crowd, the herds and the neutral's peace can do to it, and a fortunate one. The camp's and the neutral's rewards are trophies, and so is a site's find that pays once; an event may deal a challenge. The archipelago's schedule is its own: it deals what the temperate's deals, on the temperate's tempo, and names the same trials.

- **Wildfire.** The band's event, dealt with a third answer between its two. _Burn the forest_: the challenge **Embers** is added to the discard pile; played by nothing, at the end of every turn it stays in the hand it burns one forest tile into plain, drawn near the player's city, the feature and what stands on the forest going with it and the population working the tile staying on it, nothing once none stands; it gains influence on victory. Wildfire is food now against the forest over time for influence against production now.
- **Departure.** _Let them go_: one population leaves the player's city, its last no exception. _Keep them_: culture paid, more the larger the player's city. The event is dearer the larger that city: the population that leaves dearer to grow back, the one kept dearer in culture. Departure is culture against population.
- **Flood.** The low ground floods, wherever the player's city stands. _Lose the fields_: improvements are removed, drawn near the player's city, more on a later turn; once none stands, the buildings inside the border go in their place, drawn seeded among those standing, the player's city the last of them. _Settle the floodplain_: the challenge **Floodplain** is added to the discard pile; played by nothing, it removes one, in the flood's order, at the end of every turn it stays in the hand, and gains influence on victory. _Dig channels_: production paid for each thing the flood would take, more than an improvement costs to place, so paying is never the plain saving while improvements stand. Flood is production now against what stands now against improvements over time for influence.
- **Bad air and angry spirits.** A sickness runs through the player's city, wherever it stands. _Bury the dead_: population killed, more on a later turn, the player's city's last no exception. _Live with it_: the challenge **Sickness** is added to the discard pile; played by nothing, it kills one population at the end of every turn it stays in the hand, and gains influence on victory. _Help the healers_: science paid for each population the sickness would take, so a deck that plays Fire has the way out the others do not. Bad air and angry spirits is science now against population now against population over time for influence.
- **Strangers.** Strangers come to the player's city and ask to stay, speaking no word of its language. _Take them in_: population arrives idle, more on a later turn, and the challenge **Misunderstandings** is added to the discard pile; played by nothing, it takes culture at the end of every turn it stays in the hand, and gains influence on victory. _Refuse them_: nothing happens. Strangers is population and influence now against culture over time, or neither.
- **Predators.** Wolves come for the herds. _Abandon a herd_: deer and cattle tiles lose their feature, drawn near the player's city, more on a later turn, what stands on the feature going with it, nothing once none stands. _Let the farmers handle it_: the challenge **Wolves** is added to the discard pile; played by nothing, it kills one population at the end of every turn it stays in the hand, and gains influence on victory. _Defend the herds_: military paid for each herd the wolves would take, whatever stands, so defending is never free. Predators is the herds now against population over time for influence against military now.
- **Drought.** The rains fail, wherever the player's city stands. _Rain dance_: the challenge **Rain dance** is added to the discard pile; played by nothing, at the end of every turn it stays in the hand it dries one plain or forest tile into desert, drawn near the player's city, the feature and what stands on the old terrain going with it and the population working the tile staying on it, nothing once none stands; it gains influence on victory. _Rationing_: food paid, more on a later turn. _Buy grain_: money paid, more on a later turn, so a deck that builds the Tannery has a way out that keeps its food. Drought is the land over time for influence against food now against money now.
- **Travellers.** A band passes by the player's city for a night. _Trade with them_: money gained, more on a later turn. _Share stories_: culture gained, more on a later turn. Both are gains: Travellers is money against culture, a breath and not a decision.
- **A grievance.** The neutral accuses the player's city of a wrong and demands amends. _Escalate_: while the neutral's city stands at peace, war is declared; otherwise a camp is placed near the player's city with warriors on and around it, more on a later turn, and where no tile takes a camp those warriors enter as a raid; the card reads whichever of the three stands. _Pay tribute_: the challenge **Tribute** is added to the discard pile; played by nothing, at the end of every turn it stays in the hand it takes food, production and money, each more than the strike before, and it gains influence on victory. _Send gifts_: culture paid once, more on a later turn. A grievance is the peace against a tribute over time for influence against culture now.

## The capstones 🔧

Each region names the same three trials and the opening draws one: a long winter, a monument built, a siege endured. Each is hard to impossible on the deck the age opens with and beatable once its tree is climbed, on a chronicle longer than the Nomadic one.

**The long winter.** When it lands it adds **Cold night** to the discard pile, several copies: a hazard costing production, and while it stays in the hand it kills one population at the end of the turn. From the landing on, the terrain's food and the feature's are nothing on every tile of the map, the neutral's land as the player's; what a worker placed or built keeps giving, and a card's own gain is a card's. The chronicle ends in victory the moment no Cold night is left in any pile, in the middle of the play that pays the last. So the winter is paid in production or in people, the food for the next population comes from what the city built, and a city that never built shrinks: the trial is whether the economy came home.

**The monument.** When it lands it adds **Foundations** to the top of the draw pile, the first of a chain of three worker actions, each becoming the next once played: Foundations builds the first of the monument's buildings on plain, hills or desert inside the border for production, **Great stones** builds the second over the first for production, more of it, and **Ornaments** builds the monument over the second for money and culture; each stands in the tile's slot and gives nothing. On every turn after the landing one of three hazards, drawn each alike, is added to the discard pile: **Unrest** takes culture while held and costs military, **Thieves** takes money and costs military, **Weary builders** takes production and costs food. The chronicle ends in victory the moment the monument stands, in the middle of the play that builds it, and in defeat the moment one of its buildings is removed, whatever removes it, since the chain cannot begin again. So the monument is built over three cycles of the deck at the least while the hazards pile up, and its last building is paid in what only the tree brings: the trial is whether the city has every resource.

**The siege.** When it lands a raid enters through the raid's door, warriors and archers led by a **leader**, every one of them going for the player's city whatever city lies nearer and preparing the capture on its tile alone, attacking what they can on the way. The chronicle ends in victory the moment the leader is killed, whoever kills it, in the middle of the blow; the capstone names no second script and nothing that fails it: the band at the city is the clock, and the schedule keeps dealing. So the siege is broken by reaching the leader through the band around it, which strikes the harder for standing beside it: the trial is whether the city has units enough, and the military to pay for them.

## The achievement ✅

The age's victory is its achievement beside the technologies'. Its technology is **Metalworking**, which needs Settlement and unlocks the Bronze Age, and it pays influence.

## The camps 🔧

A camp is a rival band's, and it enters warriors and archers. They follow one of three scripts. **Guard** and **raider** are the Nomadic Age's, the raider crossing the water on its way, embarking where the ground ends and disembarking where it begins, and going for the nearer of the cities its walk reaches, the player's or the neutral's, preparing the capture on either's tile, so the neutral absorbs raids; a unit of the neutral's that goes as the raider goes for the player's city, the one not its own. Embarked, the camp's enemies cross the coast as slowly as the player's units do. The guard keeps the ground around its camp and crosses only once it raids. Embarked, a guard raids until it stands ashore. A raid through the map's edge enters ashore or embarked, as the edge the player's city is reached from is land or coast, so on the archipelago most raids out of the edge enter embarked. **Pillager** goes for the nearest, by its own walk, of either city's workers and of what they built, the improvements and the buildings, a city's own tile and a camp's never, and no tile it would stand on only embarked; it attacks what it can on its way, and standing on something built with nothing to attack it prepares the pillage; with nothing left to go for it raids. A camp opens held by a warrior as a guard. Its roll enters a guard, a warrior the likelier and an archer otherwise, and rarest of all a warrior as a pillager, so a camp's own pressure is guards gathering and now and then one pillager walking out alone. A camp sends out waves: once it has enough guards, some leave together as raiders, the one holding the camp staying, so a raid out of a camp arrives as a band and not one warrior at a time.

A camp's capture deals the Nomadic camp's two trophies as trophies of the age's own, **Pillage** and **Capture**, each gaining influence.
