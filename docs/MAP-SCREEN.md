# The map on screen

> How the map is shown and worked on the chronicle screen: its presses, its marks, its veils, its modes. A design page, under [`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md)'s legend. The screen around the map is [`CHRONICLE-SCREEN.md`](CHRONICLE-SCREEN.md)'s; how any screen is worked is [`INTERFACE.md`](INTERFACE.md)'s; what the map is made of is [`MAP.md`](MAP.md)'s, and how it is painted [`LOOK.md`](LOOK.md)'s.

## Selecting ✅

The chronicle screen has two modes. Out of city mode a left click on a tile selects it, and the map rings it; on a tile a unit stands on, in sight or kept in fog as last seen, it selects the unit, whoever's it is, and the map rings the unit. A left click on a selected unit selects its tile in its place, and one on a tile selected under a unit selects that unit again, so successive clicks there switch between the two, the unit first. A left click on a selected tile no unit stands on changes nothing, the player's city's own tile excepted: a left click on that tile selected enters city mode, a unit standing on it or not. Nothing more.

## A unit's step and its attack ✅

A selected unit of the player's lights every tile its move points reach, as one region with its own tile among them, and marks with a ring in the enemies' own colour every unit its attack reaches, from where it stands or after a step that brings it in range; a tile selected under a unit lights and marks nothing. A click on a lit tile is that unit's next step and selects nothing; a click on a marked unit is that unit's attack on it, the step that brings it in range first where it takes one, and selects nothing either. A unit that lights or marks anything is being aimed from the moment it is selected, at the tiles it lights and the units it marks: nothing of that kind is refused, a click on any other tile lets it go and selects that tile or the unit on it, and a click on its own tile lets it go for that tile. The selection follows the unit to where it stands after either — where it landed, or where it attacked from — lit and marked again with what it has left. A unit with no action left marks nothing, and a press on the enemy beside it selects that enemy like any other unit.

A drag from the unit onto a lit tile or a marked unit is the same step or the same attack: a unit dragged lights and marks from the start of its drag as a selected one does, wears no ring, and leaves the selection where it was; let go anywhere else, the unit comes home, what was selected lit again as it stood, and a unit that can do nothing is grabbed and comes home just the same, without a word.

**A unit being aimed shows its path.** The lit tile the pointer is on wears the unit's path to it, the tiles it would cross from where it stands, and at its end the move points the unit would have left; a unit dragged over a lit tile shows the same. Once the pointer leaves the lit tiles the path stays on the last one it was on, until the pointer is on a lit tile again, which takes the path, on a marked unit, which reads the attack in its place, or on the unit's own tile, which wears none, or the unit is let go. A step or an attack lights the unit anew and keeps no path, and a tile lit no longer loses its path. The path is the pointer's reading as any other: it stands under a pointer that never moved.

**A unit being aimed reads its attack.** The marked unit the pointer is on wears the attack: an arrow from the unit being aimed to it, and on its health bar the health the attack would remove, the unit's damage as its card reads it or all the health the marked unit holds where that is less, with a skull in the heart's place where the attack would kill it; a unit dragged over a marked unit shows the same. Where the attack takes a step first, the unit's path to the tile it would attack from is drawn with it, the move points left at its end as on any path, the arrow leaves from that tile, and the health read is the damage the unit would carry there. The attack is read as the path is and in its place, one of the two at a time: it stays on the last marked unit the pointer was on until the pointer is on another marked unit, which takes it, on a lit tile, which takes the path, or on the unit's own tile, which wears neither, or the unit is let go, and a step or an attack keeps none.

## What a unit wears ✅

A unit of the player's that can do nothing on the map is dimmed where it stands, so the units that still have something to do there read at a glance: it has no tile in its reach, its attack reaches no unit, and, a worker, it has no action left, the card its action goes to being what a worker waits for. An attack that would declare war counts for nothing there, and a step counts wherever it lands. The dim is read off the chronicle as it stands, so a dimmed unit is lit again as soon as a tile comes in its reach or a unit in its attack's. No unit is dimmed on the settle phase, which is no turn. An enemy is never dimmed: the enemy phase spends its action and the same end of turn refreshes it.

A unit that has prepared wears a badge on itself, a flame where it will pillage and a flag where it will capture, from the enemy phase that prepared it until the next resolves it or the unit is killed; a unit a tile in fog keeps as last seen prepared wears it still, darkened with the rest of it.

A unit below its full health wears a health bar on itself, whoever's it is, from the change that hurt it until it is healed to full or killed; a unit a tile in fog keeps as last seen hurt wears it still, at the health last seen, darkened with the rest of it.

An embarked unit's mark shows it embarked.

## What a tile wears ✅

**A tile's marks stand in four places.** Its feature and its improvements stand in one row above its middle, the feature first and then the improvements in the order they were placed, the row centred whatever it holds; its building stands in the middle, and the unit on the tile over it, below the middle; the mark of an assigned tile stands below the middle, under the unit standing there.

A city's own tile wears a badge below its middle, over the unit standing there: a disc in that city's colour reading its population, widening for a second digit; it is drawn where the tile is drawn live and not in fog, so the player's city wears it always and the neutral's while its tile is in sight. The player's city wears two bars beside its badge, along the bottom of its building and over the unit too: the food stock against the growth threshold, over the culture stock against the culture threshold. Each fills from its left, is full from its threshold on, and past what the stock holds shows, paler, what ending the turn would add to it; a turn that would take from a stock shows nothing more on its bar. The paler part is down whenever the resource bar's reading is. The neutral's city wears no bar.

The halo round a unit of the player's, the player's city's badge, its border and the emblem on a card's back are in the civilization's colour. The halo round a unit of the neutral's, its city's badge, and its border are in the neutral's colour. The border is drawn from each tile as the map draws it, so a tile in fog keeps the stretch of border it wore when last seen.

## The veils ✅

**The debug console switches the two veils that hide a tile**, one entry to each, `uncharted` and `fog`, which the chronicle screen alone holds. Both veils stand until a switch takes one off, and each answers the state its own is left in; they change what is drawn and nothing the rules read. With the **uncharted veil** off, every tile of the disc is drawn, an uncharted one included, and the rivers along them with it; a tile out of sight is still darkened, and one that has no snapshot is drawn as it stands. With the **fog veil** off, every tile the map draws is drawn live — as it stands, with whoever stands on it, and darkened by nothing — so the map shows every tile charted clearly and leaves the uncharted ones out. Both off draw the whole map as the rules hold it.

**The rest of the screen reads what the map draws.** The yield overlay glyphs the tiles the map draws, each from the faces it draws, the tile's and those beside it, so a tile in fog is read as it was last seen. An inspection reads that same face, and a tile in fog holds the card of the unit its snapshot keeps, read as the unit stood when last seen; whether a unit of the neutral's is hostile is no unit's own, and is read as it stands. A press on an uncharted tile lands off the map — it selects nothing, inspects nothing and acts on nothing — and no card is aimed at one: the map lights only the tiles it draws. Each veil taken off widens all of it with the map.

## The infopanel ✅

An inspected tile shows one card in the infopanel per step. A tile has at most three, in this order: the unit standing on it; the building with the tile's improvements; and the terrain with its feature and the river running along it. A card is absent when nothing fills it, and the terrain card never is. After the last card comes the first again, and a tile of a single card holds it: the cycle never falls back to the bare ring.

The unit card is headed by the unit's kind and reads its stats, and a line under the name reads the unit's state: Raft for an embarked unit, whose stats it reads as it has them embarked, Pillaging or Capturing for a unit that has prepared, Hostile for a unit of the neutral's while it is hostile, joined where a unit has more than one, and no line for a unit in no state; the other two are headed by their outermost layer and show a row per thing they hold — the river's among them on the terrain card — with what it gives at income, a row that gives nothing saying so. The terrain card reads the tile's movement cost small in the top-right corner of its head, in move points — the cost a unit pays, or, where none does, the cost an embarked unit pays — and a dash where nothing crosses the tile at all. The river's row carries one line under it: a crossing ends the move. A building that gives to the tiles beside it carries one line under its row saying so, and its row reads what it gives the tile it stands on; the terrain card of a tile it gives to holds a row for it, drawn as the building's own with what it gives that tile and no line, once however many of its kind stand beside the tile. A building that takes upkeep carries one line under its row reading it, the word and what it takes as a chip, after the beside line where it carries both.

A name on a card that names a terrain, a feature, an improvement, a building or a unit kind shows that thing as one of these cards, small at a rest and large at a right click, drawn at the size it is shown at: headed by the thing named, with its one row and what it gives at income, the terrain's movement cost in its corner and no other card's; a unit kind's card reads the kind's stats as a unit fresh of it reads them, full over full, its halo in the colour of the faction the card's text names it for. Its rows raise no tooltip.

## City mode ✅

In **city mode** a left click on a tile selects the tile, whoever stands on it and never the unit, and lights no unit: no press there moves one or attacks with one. A left click on the selection is the player's city's act on that tile — one idle population assigned to it, or the one standing there unassigned, by turns, its own tile among the tiles it works; or the tile claimed; it stays selected after any of them. A drag from a tile the population stands on onto a tile the player's city holds and nobody stands on takes it off the one and puts it on the other in that one gesture, the tile it lands on selected; let go anywhere else, the population comes home and nothing changes. A tile the player's city may claim wears the culture threshold in its middle from the moment it is selected — a minus, the number and culture's glyph — and it raises no note. An act the rules refuse plays nothing and says why over the tile, in the note a refused card raises; a claim the player's city cannot pay for says what it costs, in that same note, the tile going on wearing the culture it asks for. A click on a tile the player's city neither holds nor can claim selects it like any other and acts on nothing.

A press beside the tiles leaves the mode standing; leaving the mode drops the selection and the inspection with it. It is entered by the **city key**, by a left click on the player's city's own tile once that tile is selected, or by pressing culture or idle on the resource bar, and left by that key, by the back key, or by the chip naming the mode; the map's frame drawn in city mode's own colour and that chip are how the chronicle screen shows the mode is on. Coming into city mode lets go of a card being aimed, of the selection and of its inspection, and a window standing over the chronicle screen takes the city key instead.

The map marks the tiles the player's city can claim for as long as the mode is on, and every tile inside the border shows what it yields — every resource, whatever the yield overlay is showing, the dim of a tile nobody stands on no exception, and with no dim over the map: that dim is the overlay's alone. In city mode the map marks the assigned tiles and dims the held ones that are not; a unit stands over the mark and over the dim, darkened by neither.

## The settle phase ✅

**On the settle phase** the chronicle screen shows the centre part and nothing else, the hand holds the settle cards, the player's city has no border, and the end-turn button reads the phase and is dead until the player's city stands. The map's frame drawn in the settle phase's own colour, a chip naming the phase where city mode's chip stands, and the end-turn button filled in that same colour are how the chronicle screen shows the phase is on; the chip answers no press, and all three go as the turn ticks to turn 1. City mode is not entered while the player's city stands nowhere: the city key and the two readings that enter it do nothing. Entered once the player's city stands, its own frame and chip stand in place of the settle phase's until it is left, and the button keeps the phase's colour.

## The yield overlay ✅

The **yield overlay** shows what every tile the map draws yields: a glyph for each point of each resource, in the colour that resource is known by, over a map dimmed under them. It reads the faces the map draws, the tile's and those beside it, and the rule the capstone lays over the yield once it has landed, and nothing else — whoever stands there and whatever occupies it change what the tile gives at income, never what the overlay shows, and a tile in fog is read as it was last seen. Pressing one of the five core readings on the resource bar toggles that resource in or out of the overlay, several at a time, and the reading of a resource shown is latched down in the bar; the **yield key** clears the overlay, or, from nothing, shows every resource, culture included, and a window standing over the chronicle screen takes the key instead. The dim darkens the land and what is built on it, city mode's marks among it, and nothing else: the marks of the selection and the aim, every unit, the culture threshold the selected tile wears and the infopanel stand over it. It is a display and not a mode: the back key leaves it standing, city mode stands with it, and either is entered and left without touching the other.

## The Icons button ✅

The **Icons** button shows or hides the discs of every tile's row, the features' and the improvements', with what they wear. Hidden, a feature and an improvement are told by what lies on the land alone, and an improvement under a building that fills its tile by nothing on the map. The button stands in the map's bottom-left corner, as far from the map's left edge as a mode's chip stands from its right one and 8 over the draw pile, drawn as the Menu button is, and sunk in a well while the discs are shown, as a latched reading of the resource bar is. A press on it or the **icons key** switches them at once, and a window standing over the chronicle screen takes the key instead. It is a display and not a mode, as the yield overlay is: the back key leaves it standing, city mode and the settle phase stand with it, and every chronicle screen opens with the discs shown. It changes the tiles' rows and nothing that reads the map: a tile in fog loses its discs with the rest, and the yield overlay, the infopanel, the selection and an aim read on as they did. It raises no tooltip.
