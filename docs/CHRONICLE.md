The map they are played on is [`MAP.md`](MAP.md); the other factions on it are [`FACTIONS.md`](FACTIONS.md)'s and the schedule they live through [`SCHEDULE.md`](SCHEDULE.md)'s; the screen that shows them is [`CHRONICLE-SCREEN.md`](CHRONICLE-SCREEN.md).

## The settle phase ✅

A chronicle opens before its first turn on the **settle phase**: the map is dealt and the timeline rolled, the player's city stands nowhere yet and has no population, the map's **centre part** — the tiles within a radius of the disc's centre the region names — is in sight for the whole of the phase and nothing else is, and the hand holds the city section's card first and the deck's settle cards after it, in the deck's order, played on this phase and no other: the one that **settles** the player's city on a charted tile its building stands on, and the ones that enter the band's first units on charted tiles.

The section holds settle cards and nothing else, and a settle card played is banished.

**The settle phase is not a turn**: none of the cycle below runs on it, and no unit moves or attacks on it — a unit entered on the settle phase stands where it entered until turn 1. It is ended as a turn is, refused while the player's city stands nowhere, and the settle cards still in hand are banished with it. Turn 1 follows, its draw and its events phase as on any turn.

## The turn ✅

A chronicle is a sequence of **turns**. Each turn, in this order:

1. **Draw.** The player draws a **hand** of **five** cards from the draw pile. 🔧 Buildings, a civilization or an effect may change the number. An empty draw pile is refilled by shuffling the discard pile into it.
2. **Events.** On a due turn the schedule deals one of its events and that event deals its answers as cards; the player chooses one answer, which lands and resolves at once, on what the player's city has; play waits on the choice. On the capstone's turn the capstone lands instead, and nothing is dealt. Events land after the draw: an event turns on the player's city's stocks and on what stands on the map, which the hand does not change, so the turn opens on its hand every time and a card an answer adds to a pile waits there for its cycle.
3. **Play.** The player plays cards from the hand, in any order, paying each card's resource cost, and moves and attacks with the units on the map. Nothing else limits play: the hand, the player's city's resources and what the units hold are the whole budget.
4. **End.** The player ends the turn.
5. **Income.** Standing things do their standing thing: assigned tiles and their buildings yield their resources, and then each city pays its upkeep out of what the yields left. So what the player sees when drawing holds for the whole turn: a building built this turn yields, and is kept, from the next.
6. **Strikes.** Each card in the hand carrying a strike strikes, in hand order, each on the chronicle the one before it left, and then the rest of the hand is discarded. A strike is measured against the stock the income left.
7. **Growth.** A food stock that has reached the growth threshold is spent on one idle population, at most one a turn. Growth comes after the strikes so the income reads as a gain, a strike as a loss, and growth takes what they leave: a strike never takes the person the same turn's growth made.
8. **Enemy phase.** 🔧 First every enemy that prepared on a tile through the player's turn and still stands there resolves what it prepared, a city's capture ahead of every pillage, the player's ending the chronicle on the spot; then each camp sends its wave; then the neutral acts, its units and then its city, by its script; each enemy in turn, on the chronicle as the enemy before it left it, moves by its script, which draws from the seeded generator where it draws, and then attacks the unit its script names within its range or prepares on the tile it stands on, spending its action on either as any unit does. The enemies that act are those standing when the phase begins: one a fellow has killed before its turn acts no more, and one entered during the phase waits for the next. Once the enemies have acted, each camp rolls whether an enemy enters on it or beside it.

## Sight ✅

The map is hidden. The player's city and its units have **sight** over the tiles near them, and every tile of the disc stands in one of three states: **in sight**, **fog** — seen before, out of sight now — or **uncharted**, never seen at all. A tile in sight is drawn live. A tile in fog is drawn as it was last seen, darkened, with nothing live on it. An uncharted tile shows nothing of what it is. A river runs along the edge between two tiles and is drawn where either of them is drawn, so a course through the dark shows only the stretches the player has charted.

**Fog draws the tile as it was last seen.** The chronicle keeps a snapshot of every tile that has ever been in sight — its terrain, its feature, its improvements, its building, the city whose border it stands inside, and the non-player unit standing on it. Rivers never move, so none is kept; a unit of the player's carries sight with it and is never stale, so none is kept either. The snapshot is taken again each time a command changes the chronicle, so an enemy crossing in sight is recorded as it crosses, and a unit killed reveals nothing after the attack that killed it.

**A unit in the snapshot leaves it when the turn ticks**: the enemy phase has moved whoever stood in the fog, so a mark kept past it would say where a unit is not. Within a turn nothing but the player moves, so a unit the fog shows is where it was seen.

**A unit of the player's is not moved onto an uncharted tile**, nor across one to reach past it. Every tile beside a unit is in sight, so this shuts nobody in — walking into the dark costs a step, never a plan.

**Sight is a unit's stat**, how far in tiles it sees, and the player's city has a sight of its own, the number its city section names.

**Every terrain has an elevation**, how high it stands over the ground, a number on its content; flat ground stands at nothing.

**Sight is a line over the ground.** A tile within a unit's sight is seen when the line from the unit's tile to it meets no tile between them that is raised — an elevation above 0 — and stands at least as high as the tile the unit is on. So flat ground never stops a line; a forest hides what lies behind it from a unit on the plain but not from one on the hills; and hills stop hills. The seen tile's own elevation is never checked, or no mountain would ever be seen. Where the line runs exactly along the edge two tiles share it has two ways to go, and the tile is seen when **either** of them is clear — the generous reading, so that a tile is never hidden by which side of an edge the arithmetic fell on.

**Every tile the player's city holds is in sight** 🔧: one population works it.

**An answer may chart a tile as it lands.** The tile is charted where it was not, and its snapshot is taken as it stands, whoever stands on it; it is in fog from then on unless something sees it.

**On the settle phase the centre part of the map is in sight**, and it is the only sight there is: nothing sees on the settle phase — not the player's city once it stands, not a unit entered on it — so nothing is charted before turn 1 but the centre part, and the settle is chosen on what it shows. From turn 1 the player's city and its units see for themselves, and the centre part falls into fog wherever they do not.

**A unit of the player's attacks only a unit standing on a tile in sight**, whatever sees the tile: range says how far an attack goes, sight whether it has a target, and a unit the fog shows is none. An adjacent tile is always in sight, so melee never meets the rule. The enemies read the whole map, no tile uncharted to them, and an enemy attacks only a unit its own sight reaches: within its sight from its own tile, the line over the ground clear, whatever its fellows see. So an enemy of range one attacks beside it as ever, and one of a longer range shoots over no hill it stands under.

## The city ✅

A city holds a **stock** of each resource: income adds to them, and every cost and the upkeep are paid out of them. A stock never stands below nothing: what is taken from it past nothing, by a harm or by the upkeep, the city pays in population, one for each stock taken past nothing, however far past, a city's last no exception. A cost is paid or refused, never taken: a card or an answer a city cannot pay for is refused, so what reaches a city's population is what is done to it and what it keeps.

A city pays **upkeep** at income for what it keeps: what each building on a tile it holds declares as its upkeep, taken as one sum once its tiles have yielded. Which buildings take upkeep and what each takes is content.

**Population** is a city's inhabitants. Each may be **assigned** to a tile inside the border, at most one to a tile, and only an assigned tile yields its income — the city's own tile no exception. One population assigned to no tile is **idle**: a unit card turns one idle population of the player's city into a unit, and is refused when none is idle, or when it would be that city's last. The settle puts one population on the tile the player's city stands on, and the rest the chronicle opens with are idle; how many idle is content. Assigning is free, instant and reversible, never a card: it is staffing, not a change to the map.

The player's city has no population before the settle brings it. Population **grows**. At the growth phase a food stock that has reached the **growth threshold** is spent, and the city gains one population, arriving idle; at most one a turn, and whatever the threshold leaves stays in the stock. The growth threshold is **twice** the population and the units of the player's on the map counted together 🔧, so each population is dearer than the one before.

A city holds the tile it stands on alone; every tile more is claimed. The player's city **claims** any charted tile adjacent to one it holds by spending culture — an uncharted tile is not claimed, as it is not walked onto, so the border grows only where a unit has seen 🔧; claiming needs no card, like assigning, and the **culture threshold**, what a claim costs, is twice the tiles the city holds, its own counted 🔧. A claimed tile takes one idle population at once when the city has one. A tile a camp fills is not claimed, nor by the player's city a tile the neutral holds, and neither is a tile a unit of another faction stands on: the border grows around them, never onto them, and none takes one population. A camp's tile is claimed like any other once its capture empties the slot, the neutral's tiles once its fall frees them, and a tile a unit stood on the turn it walks off. A site's tile is claimed like any other, the site standing on it until it is captured. There is no limit to how far the border reaches.

## Units and combat ✅

A unit has **health**, **damage**, **range**, **move**, **action** and **sight**. An **attack** removes the attacker's damage from the target's health, and a unit at zero health is killed. A unit enters at its kind's health, its full health; lost health stays lost until a **heal** brings it back up, never above full. A unit's kind may carry a **bonus**, damage it adds to every unit of its faction standing beside it, read wherever a unit's damage is read, at the blow and on its card, and a unit beside several carries every one of them; a unit carries no bonus of its own. There is no retaliation: the target answers only when its own attack comes, so combat is an exchange across turns, never settled within one.

- **A unit moves on its own move points.** It holds **move points**, refreshed to its move at the start of the player's turn and lost at the end of it, and spends a tile's **movement cost** to enter it, in as many steps as the player likes. An instant can **refresh** them, one unit's at a time, so a unit that has spent its move points crosses again in the same turn; the refresh is refused on a unit whose move points are full. A unit enters a tile only where the move points it has left cover that cost in full, so a unit left with fewer strands them until the refresh — no step is taken on credit — and a tile costing more than a unit's move is beyond that unit for good. Crossing to a tile costs the cheapest way there, and the tile a unit already stands on costs it nothing. Of the ways that cost that, a unit's path is the one over the fewest tiles, and of those the one that keeps nearest the straight line from the unit to the tile. A terrain names the movement cost a unit enters it for, the one an embarked unit enters it for, or neither: a tile is entered by the units its terrain names a cost for, and a terrain that names neither is crossed by nothing. A step over an edge a river runs along spends every move point the unit has left, and is taken only where those cover the tile it enters in full, so a crossing is the last step of that unit's turn. An enemy reading a walk over the whole map has no points to drain and weighs it in moves: each step weighs its movement cost against the move the enemy has on the tile it enters, ashore or embarked, and a crossing, an embark or a disembark weighs one whole move, which is what each costs at worst. A unit passes through the units of its own faction and never through another's, and it lands only on a free tile.
- **A unit attacks on its own action.** It holds **action**, refreshed at the same tick as its move points, and spends one per attack on a unit of another faction within its range; a melee unit's range is one. The player attacks by the same press as a move — the target standing on the tile is what makes it an attack. A step spends no action, and an action spent spends the move points the unit has left with it, on an attack and on a card played through a worker alike, so a unit moves and then acts, never the other way about, and where it acts is where it stays until the refresh; an instant that refreshes its move points brings them back after an action as after a crossing. A worker attacks nothing because it is a worker, whatever its range or damage; what a unit spends its action on is written on the unit, never read off a stat. A worker spends its action on a card played through it — a building, an improvement, a feature removed, a terraform — one to a card, and a worker with none left is refused them. An instant that refreshes a unit refreshes its move points, never its action.
- **A unit embarks and disembarks through a card.** To **embark** is to step from the tile a unit stands on onto a tile beside it that embarked units enter; to **disembark** is the step back, onto a tile beside it that the unit stands on ashore. Either lands only on a free, charted tile and spends one of the unit's action, so it is the last thing that unit does that turn. An **embarked** unit is itself: it keeps its health, its sight and, a worker, the cards played through it. Its move is the one the card that embarked it names, and it attacks nothing; disembarked, its move, its damage and its range are its own again. A unit whose range is one attacks no embarked unit, and a unit of a longer range attacks one anywhere within its range. An enemy embarks and disembarks on its script's walk, through no card: each is the last thing it does that turn, as the player's is, and its embarked move is the one its camp's content names; a camp naming none keeps its enemies ashore.
- **One unit per tile.** A unit on a tile is a gate: an enemy must kill it to pass.
- **A unit hurts a city by standing on it.** A tile a city holds yields nothing at income while a unit **occupies** it: an enemy on a tile either city holds, and, hostile, a unit of the neutral's on a tile the player holds and one of the player's on a tile the neutral holds. An enemy **prepares** on the tile it stands on when its script says so, spending its action as an attack does, and the player sees it coming: the tile wears its faction's colour and the unit's card says what is prepared. When the next enemy phase begins with that enemy still standing there, what it prepared lands before anything else of the phase: on a city's tile that city is captured; on any other tile the enemy **pillages** it, removing everything a city built on it, the building and every improvement at once, and nothing where nothing is left. An enemy killed before then prepared nothing. A script that prepares where nothing is to take is a defect of the content. Which enemies prepare a pillage is content, and an embarked enemy prepares nothing, as it attacks nothing.
- **Defeat is capture.** An enemy that prepares on the player's city's tile and still stands there when the next enemy phase begins **captures** it. Population reaching zero is another defeat, and so is the building of the player's city removed, whatever removes it: the player's city falls on the spot, and nothing of play resolves after it.
- **A unit enters the map on its city's tile**: the player's blocked while a unit holds that tile, the neutral's landing around it as [`FACTIONS.md`](FACTIONS.md) says.

## Cards ✅

Eight kinds, five of them the player's own. Every card has a resource cost, possibly none. A card in a chronicle carries **counters**: named numbers its content declares with what each starts at. Whatever makes a card in a chronicle — the deck at the opening, an event adding one, a capture's reward — makes it at those values or sets them otherwise, and sets none its content does not declare; the card's own strike reads them and may set them, the card going on in the hand at the values set, and its face reads them, so what a card does and what it says are one fact. Every card in a chronicle is a card of its own, its copies included, and stays so through every pile it goes to and through becoming the card it names. Every kind cycles: played or discarded, a card goes to the discard pile and comes around again. There are two ways out of the cycle. A card **exhausted** goes to the **exhaust pile**, a pile of the chronicle's own that keeps it out of the cycle and in view; a card **banished** is out of the chronicle for good, kept nowhere. Which way a card takes is its own: a building card played is exhausted, a settle card played, a trophy played and a hazard paid are banished, as their entries say, and a card of any other kind carries **exhaust** or **banish** as a keyword, or neither; a card carrying either and discarded unplayed comes around like any other. A card no deck may hold carries no exhaust, so the exhaust pile holds only what a deck could have dealt. The deck's settle cards never cycle: in hand when the chronicle opens, played on the settle phase alone, a settle card is banished once played, and the end of the settle phase banishes the ones left in hand. **Becomes** is a keyword too: a card that carries it names another card, and played, it goes to its pile as that card, made as its content makes it, the exhaust pile where it is exhausted and the discard pile otherwise; discarded unplayed, it comes around as it is. The map's rewards are chronicle-only by nature.

- **Settle** — the kind of the city section's card and of the settle section, which holds no other: in hand on the settle phase alone, banished once played, and what it does is content, played through whatever aim it declares; one aimed at a tile refuses an uncharted one before its own reasons. The one that **settles** the player's city is the city section's, aimed at a charted tile the city section's building stands on, and puts that city there: its building in the tile's slot, held alone with one population on it and the idle population the city section names besides; what it refuses beyond the charting is content, and it leaves the terrain as it stands. One that enters a unit is aimed at a charted tile the unit can stand on with no unit on it, and puts the unit there with its move points and action full; it takes no population: the band arrives with its units.
- **Building** — builds a building on a tile inside the border where a worker stands, and that worker spends one of its action. The map is the cap: no free tile of a terrain it names, no building — and a building card with nowhere to go is a blank draw, which is what keeps a deck from being all buildings. A building card builds once: played, it is exhausted, so the copies a deck holds are how many of that building the player's city can build, and a copy bought in the meta makes it bigger; it carries no keyword for it, exhausting being the kind's own.
- **Unit** — puts a unit on the map, made of one idle population of the player's city, never its last: one population off the tiles becomes the unit, and is gone when the unit is killed. Where a unit enters is a rule of the units, above.
- **Action** — a card a unit's action goes to: played through a unit standing on the tile it is aimed at or beside it, which spends one of its action, and what it does is content. The card names the unit it is played through, and its label reads that name before the kind's; one that names none is played through any unit.
- **Instant** — an immediate effect that no unit's action goes to, and what it does is content.
- **Hazard** — a kind that is not the player's own. It sits in no deck: an event adds a hazard to a chronicle's piles, and from there it cycles like any other card. It **strikes** at the end of any turn it is still in the hand, after the income and before the hand is discarded — and again every cycle it comes around, so a hazard left unanswered hurts the player's city again every cycle until it is dealt with. Playing it is how it is dealt with: aimed at nothing, it does nothing at all, and paying its cost is what banishes it. That cost is the whole of the card's decision — what the player's city gives up to be rid of the strike.
- **Trophy** — a kind a capture deals, and no deck holds one. Played, through whatever aim its content declares, its effect lands and it is banished, the banishing being the kind's own as exhausting is a building's; it carries no keyword for it. Kept in any pile to the ending, it gains the influence its content declares, on victory or defeat alike.
- **Challenge** — the kind an event's answer may deal, as its harm or on top of one, and no deck holds one. It is played by nothing: it cycles until the ending, and kept in any pile to it, it gains the influence its content declares on victory alone. A challenge may **strike** as a hazard does, at the end of any turn it is still in the hand after the income, and nothing pays it off, so the strike comes around every cycle for the rest of the chronicle: the influence is the price of living with it.

An **influence card** is a trophy or a challenge: it carries influence of its own, paid into the campaign when the chronicle ends with the card still in it, in whichever pile; a card banished pays none. Why the split between the kinds: a trade the player chose paying on a defeat makes the defeat worth chasing, and a trophy paying only on victory makes a lost chronicle pay nothing new.

A card **aimed** at a tile lands on no tile its aim does not admit, and the map lights the tiles it does. A card aimed at a **unit** is refused on a tile no unit of the player's stands on, before what the card itself asks of that tile. A card that aims at nothing needs nothing of the map: it is played where it stands. A card aimed at a tile may be played through a unit standing beside that tile, whose action it spends: where one unit beside the tile can be the one, the play goes through it; where several can, the card is then aimed at a unit, among those.

A card aimed at the hand lands on another card of the hand, whichever the player chooses, and never on itself. A hand holding no other card blocks it in the hand. The card played goes to its pile before it lands, the discard pile or the exhaust pile, so a card it discards lies over it.
