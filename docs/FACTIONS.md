# Factions

> The other factions on the map and what is nobody's: the enemies and their camps, the neutral, and the sites. A design page, under [`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md)'s legend. The rules a chronicle is played by are [`CHRONICLE.md`](CHRONICLE.md)'s, and the map they stand on is [`MAP.md`](MAP.md)'s.

## Enemies and camps ✅

**Enemies enter from camps.** The generator places **camps** on the map, each filling its tile's building slot, each uncharted until seen, and the chronicle opens with one enemy of the camp's standing on each. A camp comes to stand in two more ways, placed by an event's answer or left where the neutral's city fell to the enemies, and a camp is a camp in everything however it came to stand: it rolls its own enemies, it is a raid's door, and its capture deals the same rewards. A camp an answer places is drawn near the player's city among the tiles a camp lies on, the slot empty, no unit standing, the ground running to that city from them, no nearer than three tiles 🔧 from that city's own tile and apart from every camp standing 🔧; where no such tile stands, the answer says what it does instead. An enemy a camp enters — at the opening, by its roll, or through an answer that enters it on a camp the answer places — is the camp's, and stays so whatever script it comes to carry; a raid's enemies are no camp's, whichever door they come through.

**An event's enemies enter together**, as one raid through one door, drawn seeded: a camp still standing, likelier by far, or a tile of the disc's outer ring that the raid's first enemy stands on, ashore or embarked, and the player's city is reached from, over the ground and, where the age's enemies embark, the water, a raid through a coast door entering embarked; a door with no free tile around it for the raid's first enemy is never drawn; the odds between the two are content 🔧. The first enemy enters on the door's tile where it is free and it stands on it, and each one after on the nearest free tile it stands on that the door's own ground runs to for it, or, through a coast door, its own water, ring by ring around the door, seeded among tiles equally near, and never on a city's tile, the neutral's as the player's; a tile a city holds is entered like any other, and the enemy on it occupies it. So a raid through a camp across the water enters on the camp's island and crosses from there. With no camp standing the outer ring is the only door, and an event's raid always enters; a map where nothing the player's city is reached from touches the outer ring is a defect of the content, never a map the generator deals.

**A camp enters on its own too**: at every enemy phase, each camp standing rolls, seeded, whether an enemy enters, its kind and its script drawn as any entry's, on the camp's tile where it is free and the kind stands on it and otherwise on the nearest free tile it stands on that the ground runs to the camp from, ring by ring around the camp, seeded among tiles equally near, never on a city's tile, and nowhere where none is free; the odds are content. A camp that has just come to stand, or whose roll has just entered an enemy, waits as many turns as the content names before it rolls again.

**What an entry enters is content**: every entry — the opening, the camp's roll, a raid, an answer — draws from a unit entry's table of its own, rows of a kind of unit and a script, each with a weight; each enemy's row is drawn seeded among them by their weights, one draw for each enemy, so a raid of several comes as a mix, and a table of one row draws nothing. The camp's content names its opening's table and its roll's, and an answer's content names the answer's. A table naming a kind or a script the catalogue does not hold, a weight not above nought or no row at all is a defect of the content: a camp's is refused before any chronicle is dealt, an answer's where it is met, and play stops. Where the enemy lands is read on the kind drawn: a tile it stands on, reached on its own move ashore and the camp's embarked move on the water. What enters a raid draws its enemies' rows before the door, and may put rows of its own in front of the drawn ones; the door is read on the first enemy, whichever way it came to be first. The opening draws among the rows whose kind stands on the camp's tile, so every camp opens held; a camp lying on a terrain none of its opening's kinds stands on is a defect of the content, refused before any chronicle is dealt.

**An enemy follows its script**, the one the row drawn for it names; what a script does, whom it attacks, where it prepares, and whether it attacks from the player's city's tile rather than preparing the capture there, is content. The camp's content names which script is its guard, the one its wave counts, and which its raider, the one its wave sends. A camp sends its guards off in a wave: at the start of every enemy phase, once the prepares have landed and before any enemy acts, each camp standing counts its own guards standing ashore, wherever they stand; where they are as many as its content names, as many as it names leave together as raiders, the camp's still, and act as such from that phase on. The guards off the camp's tile leave first, in the order they entered, and the one on its tile last, so a wave smaller than the band leaves the camp held. How many gathered and how many sent are the camp's content, and a camp naming no wave sends none. So a camp's own enemies come as a band and not one by one, and a guard killed on the player's turn holds the wave back. Scouting is how a chronicle learns where the enemy comes from.

A camp is **captured** by a unit of the player's or of the neutral's, any unit, that stands on it through the enemy phase — still there when the next turn begins; neither prepares, standing is enough. A captured camp leaves the map, its slot empty, so it enters nothing again and is no raid's door, and its enemies are no camp's from then on; the outer ring stays open, so capturing every camp ends the camps' own enemies and never the events' raids.

A camp the player captures deals its **rewards**, cards added to the chronicle, each a card and how many copies of it the take adds: the capture deals them as an event deals its answers, in the same window, and the end of turn waits on the choice: nothing after the captures resolves until every camp's reward is chosen, and the one chosen is added to the discard pile in the copies it names; a condition the capture itself meets ends the chronicle on the capture, and its rewards are never dealt — the deck no longer matters; what a camp deals, one reward or several and how many copies each, is content.

## The neutral ✅

A second city stands on the map, the **neutral**'s: a third faction, with a city, a border, population, stocks and units of its own. The generator places its city first, within a band of the centre the region names, and the camps and the sites keep from it as they keep from their own kind; the chronicle opens with it standing, its tile held with one population on it and the units its content names at its opening standing on and around that tile, each entering as a raid's enemies enter around their door, the first on the tile itself and each after on the nearest free tile its kind stands on that the ground runs to from it, seeded among tiles equally near, its own city's tile open to it, and each under the script its kind carries, a worker its workers' and any other its guard's; a neutral naming none opens with no unit. It is a city in everything [`CHRONICLE.md`](CHRONICLE.md)'s _The city_ says: its assigned tiles yield into its stocks at income, it grows at the growth threshold, it claims at the culture threshold, and a tile it holds is no claim of the player's. Where the enemy phase says the neutral acts, its city claims as many tiles as its culture pays for, each at the culture threshold as it stands, its script choosing which among the tiles its border may grow onto; it reads the whole map as the enemies do, so it claims a tile charted or not. A card played through a worker of the player's lands on no tile the neutral holds, its city's included, and one played through a worker of the neutral's on no tile the player holds. Where the neutral acts its units act first, then its city claims, then its script assigns its population over the tiles its city holds — each population off the tile it stood on and onto the one its script names, as the player assigns, and the rest idle — and then it enters one unit as the player's city plays a unit card: its script names the card, the card's cost is paid out of the neutral's stocks, one idle population becomes the unit and never its last, and the unit enters around its city's tile as its opening's units enter, on the tile itself where it is free and otherwise on the nearest free tile its kind stands on that the ground runs to from it, seeded among tiles equally near; where no tile is free nothing enters and nothing is paid; a unit entered waits for the next enemy phase, as an entered enemy does. Its workers build as the player's do: where its units act, a worker of its plays an improvement or a building card its content names through itself, on the tile its city holds that it stands on, as the player plays one through a worker — the card admits the tile as it admits the player's, the cost is paid out of the neutral's stocks, the worker spends its action, and a card the neutral cannot pay for is not played; the neutral has no deck, so the map is the only cap on how many of a building it builds. A pillage prepared on a tile the neutral holds lands as on the player's, and never on its city's own tile, where an enemy prepares its capture. What the neutral does with what it has is its script, content: which tile it claims, which tiles its population works, which unit it enters out of its population and its stocks, which card a worker of its plays and where, and where its units go, chosen on the chronicle as it stands; it never draws a hand.

Its units act where the enemy phase says the neutral acts, each by its script, moving, embarking and attacking as an enemy does by its; at peace they attack no unit of the player's and neither land on nor cross a tile the player holds, and no unit of another faction passes through them. The enemies and the neutral's units attack one another within reach. The neutral keeps guards and sends waves as a camp does: its content names which script is its guard and which its wave, and how many guards gathered send how many, the guards off its city's tile leaving first, in the order they entered. They go for the camps: a unit of the neutral's standing on a camp through the enemy phase captures it, and the camp leaves the map dealing nothing.

The neutral's **relations** with the player are at peace or **hostile**, at peace when the chronicle opens. The player **declares war** by a landing on a tile the neutral holds, its city's own among them, or by an attack on a unit of its, and the neutral is hostile from that change for the rest of the chronicle. At peace a unit of the player's crosses no tile the neutral holds, as the neutral's cross none the player holds, and lands on one only by declaring war; hostile, each side's tiles are open to the other. Hostile, a unit of the neutral's is an enemy in everything an enemy does and the neutral's still: it attacks the player's units, is kept off no tile, occupies the tile it stands on, prepares on the player's city and captures it, which is defeat; it enters as it entered at peace, and the neutral sends no wave while hostile. The enemies and the neutral's units attack one another at peace and hostile alike.

The player destroys the neutral: its city is captured by a unit of the player's standing on it through the enemy phase, as a camp is, and by a unit of the player's alone — its own guard resting on its tile captures nothing, and an enemy standing there captures nothing by standing. Its building leaves the map, its slot empty, and the neutral's population, its stocks and its border go with it: its tiles are nobody's from then on, claimed like any other. The capture deals the neutral's rewards as a camp's are dealt, in the same window, and what it deals is content. Its military units of no city are the neutral's still and hostile for the rest of the chronicle, going for the player's city as the raider does; nothing enters for a city that has fallen.

The enemies destroy the neutral too: an enemy that prepares on its city's tile and still stands there when the next enemy phase begins captures it, as the player's city is captured. Its population, its stocks and its border go as when the player captures it, and nothing is dealt; its building leaves the map and the age's camp stands in its slot, held by the enemy that took it, which is that camp's from then on and carries its guard's script, so a camp stands where the neutral's city stood. Its relations with the player stand as they were, and its units of no city keep acting by their scripts, which say what each does of no city. An age whose camp does not stand on every terrain its neutral's city's building stands on is a defect of the content, refused before any chronicle is dealt.

The neutral's city falls on the player's city's other conditions too: its population reaching zero, and its building removed, whatever removes it. It falls on the spot, as when the player captures it: its building leaves the map, its slot empty, and its population, its stocks and its border go with it, its tiles nobody's from then on; nothing is dealt, its relations with the player stand as they were, and its units of no city keep acting by their scripts.

## Sites ✅

A **site** is a place on the map that is nobody's and pays once. The generator places sites as it places camps, each filling its tile's building slot, each uncharted until seen, and no enemy enters from one. A unit of the player's or of the neutral's standing on a site through the enemy phase captures it, as a camp is captured: the site leaves the map, its slot empty; the player's capture deals its rewards as a camp's are dealt, and the neutral's deals nothing. A site's tile is claimed and terraformed like any tile: a terraform into a terrain the site does not stand on removes it as it removes any building, and it then deals nothing. No enemy and no unit of the neutral's goes for a site, and a pillage leaves it standing. What a site is, where it lies and what it deals are content, and what it deals is the age's: one site deals one thing in one age and another in the next.
