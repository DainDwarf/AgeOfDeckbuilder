# Chronicle screen

> How the chronicle is shown and worked on its screen, around the map: its windows, its bar, its hand and its piles. A design page, under [`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md)'s legend. How the map is shown and worked there is [`MAP-SCREEN.md`](MAP-SCREEN.md)'s; how any screen is worked is [`INTERFACE.md`](INTERFACE.md)'s; the rules this screen shows are [`CHRONICLE.md`](CHRONICLE.md)'s, [`MAP.md`](MAP.md)'s, [`FACTIONS.md`](FACTIONS.md)'s and [`SCHEDULE.md`](SCHEDULE.md)'s.

## The deal window ✅

The events phase's deal stands in a **window of its own**, which the end of turn's play-out ends on, and which a chronicle opened on a deal opens on; a capture's rewards stand in the same window, under the name of what was captured, each capture's in turn and before the turn ends, a reward of one copy drawn as a card of the deck is and one of several as a browse draws a stack, its count reading the copies. The window rises out of nothing in a short beat, whatever speed the motions are set at, since it is the beat a second click waits out and no motion of the chronicle's, and what it deals reacts to no left click until it stands whole. The window is laid out from its title down: the title, naming the event, stands on the kit's ribbon ([`LOOK.md`](LOOK.md)) at one height whatever is dealt; the lore stands under it, centred, in the pale ink, broken as a line of a window of the menu is; and what is dealt stands under the lore in one row, centred, in the order dealt: an event's answers as text, a capture's rewards as cards. A row under a short lore so starts higher than one under a long lore, and each stands clear of the hand. A capture's window reads the lore of what was captured the same way. A card's deal stands in the same window, rising where the play-out of the card played ends, under the card's name, reading `Choose one` where the lore stands, each card it deals drawn as a reward of one copy is, as the card of the pile it is.

An answer is its name over its text on a panel ([`LOOK.md`](LOOK.md)), of one width, name and text centred, and the answers of a row stand as tall as the tallest. Its text says what it does on this turn — how many enemies it enters, the card it adds, the text of its case where it declares cases, a line to each thing where it does several; what it costs the player's city reads in that text and wears no chip. An answer is no card: it reads no kind, and no press shows it large. A name in its text reacts to the rest and the right click as a name on a card does, the card it names shown as the answer will add it, and a left click on the name is the answer's. An answer a left click would take is lighter under the pointer, as a pile's round is. An answer the player's city cannot pay for, or one refused, stands on its panel darkened, its words and its glyphs darkened with it, and is no lighter under the pointer; the number of a cost the city cannot pay reads in the red an unaffordable cost's disc wears on a card, and is not darkened.

A left click on one is the choice, and the window closes on it with the landing playing out after — the next deal's window rising where one still waits, and where none does, the rest of the end of turn after a reward, the next turn's draw among it, and nothing after an answer or a card's deal; a left click on an unaffordable or a refused answer says why over it, in the note a refused card raises, and the window stands. The pointer resting on such an answer, off its names, raises the same line in a tooltip over it, as a pile's name stands over its pile. A press takes it down. Nothing in the window is selected, so a press beside what is dealt does nothing. The right click shows a card large over the window, and a press beside it or the back key takes it down onto the window where it stood. The back key takes down the card shown large, else raises the menu as it does from a clean chronicle screen: the window closes on nothing but the choice, the menu opens over it and closes back onto it, and a new chronicle leaves it behind like anything else.

**A button at the right end of the title's ribbon minimizes the window**: a small square wearing its sign, an eye struck through, and no word, the pointer resting on it raising a tooltip under it reading `Minimize`. A left click on it, once the window stands whole, minimizes the window: the scrim falls, the lore and what is dealt go, and the ribbon and the button alone stay where they stood, the button sunk in its well. A left click on the sunk button, a click on the ribbon, left or right, or the back key brings the window back, whole at once and as it stood. Over the ribbon of a minimized window the pointer is a hand, and resting there raises nothing. Every deal's window rises whole, the one a chronicle is opened on and the next one after a choice alike.

**Under a minimized window the chronicle screen is read, and nothing is commanded.** The map pans and zooms, the right click inspects and the rest raises what it raises, on the map, on the bar and on a card of the hand, which lifts under the pointer as on any turn; what shows and hides a display reacts as on any turn, a pile opens its browse, the ledger folds, scrolls and raises the capstone's window, the log folds, scrolls and centres the map on a line's tiles, and the menu opens. A left click selects nothing and acts on nothing, on the map or on a card of the hand, and a held press takes hold of nothing, so every drag on the map pans it and none lifts a card out of the hand; End turn is dead, and nothing enters city mode. City mode standing as the window rose stands as it stood. A browse, the capstone's window and the menu each close back onto the screen with the window still minimized. The back key takes down a card shown large, closes a browse or the capstone's window, drops the inspection, then brings the window back; from the window it raises the menu.

## The capstone's window ✅

The capstone gets a **window of its own**, raised when the chronicle screen opens, on a chronicle begun and on one resumed alike; when the capstone lands, where the end of turn's play-out ends on it over the hand that turn drew, and its landing plays out once it is closed; and by a click on the capstone's row of the ledger, whenever the player asks. The opening's window closes onto the screen as the chronicle stands, a deal's window rising where a deal waits, and one the row raised closes onto the screen as it stood. Every raising shows the capstone under one title naming it the capstone, on its ribbon at the height a deal's stands at, with the capstone's lore standing under the title as a deal's does: until the capstone has landed, the promise of what passes it and nothing of the turn it lands on; from its landing on, that the time has come. Under the lore the capstone stands on one panel ([`LOOK.md`](LOOK.md)), centred on the screen: its name over its text in a column at the panel's left, drawn as an answer's are and as wide as an answer, and beside the column, in one row and in the order the text names them, everything the text names, each once, the column centred against the row. A card stands there as the landing will add it, drawn as a reward of the deal window is, and as one stack with its count where the text writes a number before its name; anything else the text names stands as the face it wears. The panel runs from the column to a little past the last thing of the row, a stack's count included, and a capstone whose text names nothing stands on its column alone. Its text says what its landing does, a line to each thing where it does several. The capstone is no card: it reads no kind, and no press shows it large. The panel is lighter under the pointer wherever the pointer is on it off the row's cards, as an answer is. The window offers nothing to choose, and the inspection key does nothing while it stands; a left click on the panel closes it, a name in its text and a card of its row included, as the back key and a press beside it do. A name in its text reacts to the rest and the right click as a name on a card does, a card of the row reacts to them as a reward does, and the right click anywhere else on the panel does nothing; what is shown large stands over the window, and a press beside it or the back key takes it down onto the window where it stood, as on the deal.

## The war warning ✅

A press that would land a unit of the player's on a tile the neutral holds or attack a unit of its, while the neutral is at peace, raises a **warning** before it lands, a window of the menu on its scrim titled Declare War: one line asking whether the player is sure and saying what follows, then the press itself, Move or Attack, and Back. Back, the back key or a press on the scrim takes it down and nothing moves, the unit selected and lit as it was; the press gone through lands as it would have. Hostile, the press lands with no warning. An answer that declares war raises none: its text says so, and the choice is the press.

## The ending screen ✅

A chronicle that has ended stands on its **ending screen**, which rises with its scrim out of nothing as the ending plays out and never comes down: it reads the outcome — victory, or defeat with what ended the chronicle and the turn it ended on — and under it a ledger of what the chronicle paid, as [`META.md`](META.md) says: one row per achievement reached, a check mark and its technology's name, and at the row's end the influence it paid, influence's glyph and the number, which a row that paid none does not read; then one row per influence card kept that carries influence, its name and, where it kept several copies, their count after it, and at the row's end the influence all its copies paid; a challenge kept to a defeat keeps its row, greyed, reading the influence its copies would have paid, and a line in that same grey strikes the whole row through, from the name's left edge to the number's right edge; a card that carries no influence has no row; then, under a rule where a row stands over it, the influence paid in all, influence's glyph, the word and the number, which reads 0 where nothing was paid.

The outcome's word stands on the kit's ribbon, a larger one than a window's title stands on. The outcome, the ledger and the **End chronicle** button under it are one block, centred on the screen. The button is drawn as a button of the menu is and opens the campaign screen; it rises with the screen and reacts to no press until the screen has risen. The scrim swallows every other press, the back key opens the menu over the screen, which closes back onto it, and the campaign screen is the way out, by the button or by the menu's **Campaign**. The ending screen is seen once: the chronicle has left the save, and the game boots next on the campaign screen.

## The right click, the back key and the menu ✅

The right click finds a tile, a card of the hand, a pile or a card in a browse, in city mode and while a card is being aimed alike; a tile it lands on afresh comes up at its first card, the selection's own tile excepted: there an inspection, by the right click and by the inspection key alike, comes up at the card of what is selected, the unit's for a unit and for a tile the first of the tile's own, and steps on through every card the tile holds.

The back key takes one step more here than the presses name: after the selection comes city mode.

The menu opens over the chronicle screen as over any screen, and the screen waits under it: the selection, being aimed or not, and the inspection.

## The pointer ✅

**What the pointer rests on reads as under it, however the pointer came to be there.** The end-turn button wears its hover look whenever the pointer stands on it while it is live: the next turn rolls in on its chip during the end of turn's play-out, and the moment the play-out ends under a pointer that never moved, the button wears that look again, so a turn ends where the last one did. A pointer that left the game over a thing and comes back straight onto it finds that thing as a pointer moved onto it does.

**Beside the things** on the chronicle screen is the map beyond the tiles it draws, the band the hand and the piles stand in, and the resource bar's panel between its readings: a press there lands on no thing.

## The resource bar ✅

The resource bar is one plate hung from the middle of the screen's top edge, as wide as its seven readings, and the map runs to the top of the screen beside it. Each reading is its glyph and its number, and no word, in the order the bar reads them: the five core resources, culture, then the idle population under population's glyph, read over the population, `1/3`. Food and culture each wear a bar under their reading, as long as the reading: the food stock against the growth threshold and the culture stock against the culture threshold, drawn as the player's city's bars are ([`LOOK.md`](LOOK.md)) and filled as they are.

After each value, at a smaller size, the bar reads what ending the turn now would do to it. A stock reads the income less what the upkeep and the hand's strikes take, signed: `+1`, `−2`. Idle reads the population growth brings or the upkeep and the strikes take: `+1`, `−1`. What the turn would add reads in the colour of a gain, and what it would take in the colour of a loss. A stock the upkeep or the strikes take past nothing reads the income less the whole of what the upkeep and the strikes ask of it, `−6` on a stock of nothing, on a pill, and the idle reading of the population taken stands on a pill too. Nothing is read where the end of turn changes nothing. The reading is the end of turn's own run on the chronicle as it stands, the stocks read as the strikes leave them, before growth spends food, and idle read after growth. It stands on every turn the screen stands on, never on the settle phase nor while a deal stands, and is down from the End turn press until the screen stands on the next hand, the choice in a deal's window and what plays out after it included; every other play-out leaves it standing, and it changes with each change that lands, as the value does. The paler part of a threshold's bar is down whenever this reading is.

Culture and idle are the two readings that enter city mode, and each stands on a button's gold while city mode has that act waiting: culture's while the stock covers the culture threshold and a tile the player's city may claim stands, idle's while one population is idle and a tile the player's city holds has nobody on it, its own tile and an occupied one no exception. The gold stands where the well a latched reading sits in would, the reading sunk the same way; a reading latched with its act waiting stands on the gold.

The pointer resting on a reading raises its tooltip under it: the reading's name over its sentence, as a stat on a unit's card raises its own. Food's and culture's name is followed by the stock over its threshold.

## The pinned achievements ✅

A **ledger** stands against the screen's left edge, a little under the height the resource bar hangs to, at the same place on every window: one panel, a row to the capstone at its top, then a row to each achievement the chronicle reads whose technology the campaign pins, one under the other in the order the chronicle reads them, a thin rule between two rows. The capstone's row reads the capstone's name and, at the right end of its line, where an achievement's count stands, the word naming it the capstone; it is as tall as that one line, and never fainter. It is lighter under the pointer, as an answer is, and a click on it, left or right, raises the capstone's window, as a click on a pile opens its browse; the pointer resting on it raises nothing. An achievement's row reads its technology's name; at the right end of the name's line, the achievement's count over its need, which a need of one does not read; and under the name the goal as the plate reads it, wrapped onto as many lines as it takes, the row as tall as they stand. Once an achievement is reached its row reads the check mark and the name alone, fainter, as tall as that one line, and the rows under it close up. A name in a goal reacts to the rest and the right click as on a plate. An achievement's row reacts to no press. The ledger reaches no lower than a little over the log's top: one whose rows ask for more scrolls them as a browse scrolls, by the wheel and by a held press dragging it, under the browse's scrollbar, its tab holding still. The wheel over the ledger never zooms the map, whether the ledger scrolls or not. The ledger stands on the settle phase and in city mode as on any turn.

A **tab** stands on the ledger's outer edge, at its top: a button wearing a triangle and no word, the triangle pointing at the screen's edge. A left click on it folds the ledger into the edge: the panel and its rows are gone, and the tab alone stands on the screen's edge at the height it stood at, its triangle pointing away from the edge, under the tally, the achievements reached over those the ledger holds, `1/5`, the capstone's row not counted; a ledger holding no achievement folds to its triangle alone. A left click on the folded tab brings the ledger back. The pointer resting on the tab raises a tooltip under it reading `Goals`, folded or not, and no key folds the ledger. It is a display and not a mode, as the Icons button's is: the back key leaves it as it stands, the settle phase and city mode stand with it, and every chronicle screen opens with the ledger unfolded. A pinned technology whose achievement the chronicle does not read stands there as nothing, and a campaign pinning none the chronicle reads leaves the capstone's row alone on the ledger.

## The summary ✅

A **summary** of what is selected stands in the screen's top right corner, under the four buttons there, its right edge on the Menu button's: one panel ([`LOOK.md`](LOOK.md)), of one size whatever it reads, at the same place on every window, in city mode and on the settle phase as on any turn. It stands from the left click that selects a unit or a tile until that selection is dropped, and a selected card of the hand raises none. It reads the chronicle as the map draws it, and reads again at each change that lands, as a value of the resource bar does: a step spends the move it reads.

**A unit's summary** reads, on its first row, a disc of the unit's faction wearing the faction's sign, whatever the Faction signs button shows, then the unit's name, then the badge of a unit that has prepared, as the map's; and at the row's right end the sentence the unit's card reads on the infopanel, where it reads one. Its second row reads four numbers, each after its sign: the health, the move and the action the unit holds over its kind's own, and its damage, with the bonus it carries after a plus sign, each as the unit's card on the infopanel reads it ([`MAP-SCREEN.md`](MAP-SCREEN.md)), a unit a tile in fog keeps read as last seen and an embarked one as it has them embarked. Its range and its sight stand on its card alone.

**A tile's summary** reads, on its first row, the name of the building standing on it, and at the row's right end its terrain's name; a tile with no building reads its terrain's name alone. Its second row reads what the tile yields, a plus sign and one glyph to each point, in the order the resource bar reads the resources: every part of it, its building's among them, read as the yield overlay reads a tile ([`MAP-SCREEN.md`](MAP-SCREEN.md)). After it, behind a minus sign, stands one glyph to each point of its building's upkeep, and last move's glyph and what entering the tile costs in move points, as the terrain card's disc reads it. A tile that yields nothing reads no plus, and one with no upkeep no minus.

The pointer resting on one of a unit's four numbers, or on what entering a tile costs, raises that stat's tooltip, as on a card ([`INTERFACE.md`](INTERFACE.md)). The summary reacts to no press, and stops one as anything standing over the map does.

## The log ✅

A **log** stands against the screen's left edge under the pinned achievements' ledger, its top at one height on every window: one panel ([`LOOK.md`](LOOK.md)), as wide as the ledger, reading in sentences what the chronicle did, a line to each thing, the newest at its foot. It is as tall as the lines it holds, never shorter than its tab asks, and grows down to just over the height the hand's resting cards rise to. Holding more than that, it scrolls as a browse scrolls, by the wheel and by a held press dragging it, under the browse's scrollbar, and a line landing brings it back to its foot. The wheel over the log never zooms the map, whether the log scrolls or not. A log holding no line does not stand, and neither does its tab.

**A line lands as its change plays**, on the player's turn and through the end of turn alike, and only where the map plays that change in the player's sight: what the fog or the uncharted hid is never a line. A play-out a change of the window's shape cuts short lands at once the lines of what it skipped, in order, each where the map would have played it in sight. The log reads an attack, with the attacker, the unit attacked and the damage; an attack that kills; a unit entering the map; an enemy preparing, on a city's tile or on a tile it will pillage; a pillage, with what it removed, the building or else the improvements; a capture, with what was captured; the income, with each stock it moved; a strike, with its card and what it took; and the player's city growing. Population a city pays for a stock taken past nothing reads on the line that took it. A unit is named by its kind after a disc of its faction wearing the faction's sign, as the summary names one, whatever the Faction signs button shows. A unit standing on a tile out of sight is not named: the line reads the unit in sight alone. A stock or the population moved reads as a signed number before its glyph, as the resource bar's end of turn reads one. Nothing else is a line: not a walk, not a card played, and nothing that has a window, a row or a warning of its own. Nothing is a line on the settle phase.

**Lines stand under a heading to their turn**, `Turn 40`, a turn with no line having none. The log holds the turn the chronicle stands on and the four before it, and older lines leave it. It keeps what happened on this screen and no more: a chronicle opened again from its save opens on an empty log.

A **tab** stands on the log's outer edge, at its top, as the ledger's stands on the ledger. A left click on it folds the log into the edge, the tab alone standing there with its triangle pointing away from the edge, and a second brings the log back. The pointer resting on the tab raises a tooltip under it reading `Log`, folded or not. It is a display as the ledger's fold is: no key folds it, the back key leaves it as it stands, and every chronicle screen opens with the log unfolded. The log stops a press as anything standing over the map does. A line that reads a tile, which is every line but the income's, a strike's and growth's, is lighter under the pointer, as the capstone's row of the ledger is, and while the pointer is on it the map rings its tiles ([`LOOK.md`](LOOK.md)): the tile its change happened on, and for an attack the attacker's tile and the tile of the unit attacked, each in the colour of the faction whose unit the line names there. The tile of a unit the line does not name wears none. The rings mark where it happened, wherever those units stand since. It is the pointer's reading as any other: it stands under a pointer that never moved, passes to the line a scroll or a landing brings under the pointer, and goes when the pointer leaves the line. A left click on such a line centres the map on its tiles: the map pans, its zoom as it stood, until their middle stands in the middle of the map's frame, or as near it as the map's bounds let it come. The click lets go of a card being aimed, as a left click on the ledger's tab does, and leaves the selection standing; it pans the map wherever a drag would. The right click does nothing there, and a line that reads no tile reacts to nothing.

## The hand and the aim ✅

**The hand rests sunk under the screen's bottom edge.** Its cards stand in a lane in the middle of that edge, between the draw pile and the discard pile, each showing its picture and its name over the edge and the rest of it under. The lane is never wider than the smallest window gives it, so on a wider window the hand and the piles keep together in the middle of the edge. A card lifted out of the lane stands whole, its bottom edge just over the screen's.

A left click selects a card of the hand, unaffordable or not. A selected card that aims at a tile or at a unit is **being aimed** from that moment: the map is already there, and lights the tiles its aim admits, or rings the units it admits where it aims at a unit. A second click plays the card — one that aims at nothing is played where it stands, and refused over the card with its reason when it is unaffordable; one that aims at a tile or at a unit lands nowhere, no tile lying under a card in the hand, so it stays selected; one that aims at the hand is being aimed from then, where it lies, or refused over the card as one that aims at nothing is. A card aimed at the hand waits for it because a click on another card of the hand selects that card under any selection, and is the play only once the card is being aimed.

**A card being aimed says so over the hand.** One line stands over the hand, naming the card by its own name and what it is played at, a tile, a unit or a card of the hand, in the selection's colour, the one the map rings the selected tile in. It comes up the moment the card is being aimed and goes when it is let go of, so a card selected and waiting to be played reads apart from one being aimed without reading the map. The card itself wears nothing for it: it stands lifted, as every selected card does.

**Every card being aimed is aimed at one kind of thing.** A card that aims at a tile or at a unit is aimed at a drawn tile, and one that aims at a unit is refused on a tile no unit of the player's stands on before its own reasons are asked; a card that aims at the hand is aimed at every other card of the hand. A play refused says the one reason it is turned down, and the card stays selected. A drag that lifts a card of the hand lets the selected card go as it lifts, the lifted card itself and a card being aimed among them, whatever it was aimed at, and the card stays let go where the lifted one comes home.

**A card being aimed reads the yield it would give.** While the pointer is on a tile the card's aim admits, and the card's play would place a building or an improvement on it, that tile wears what the thing placed would give it, and each tile beside it wears what the building placed would give it, fainter where one of its kind already gives to that tile; a tile the play would give nothing wears nothing. It is read as the play would leave the tile, whether the player's city can pay for the card or not, on the faces the map draws and under the rule the capstone lays over the yield once it has landed, as the yield overlay reads. It is the pointer's reading as any other: it stands under a pointer that never moved, goes when the pointer leaves the tile, and goes with the aim.

**The drag is the two clicks in one gesture.** A card lifted clear of the hand and released there is selected and played at once: one that aims at a tile or at a unit stays selected, being aimed; one that aims at the hand stays selected, being aimed where it lies; an unaffordable one stays selected under its refusal note.

## The piles ✅

A click on a pile, left or right, opens its **browse**, under a title on the kit's ribbon reading the pile's name and the count of its cards. Every card the pile holds stands once, as a stack of its copies, a count under the front card's bottom right corner reading them as on the civilization's browse, eight stacks to a line from the frame's top left, in the order of the collection, so the draw order is given away to nobody. Copies stand in one stack only where they read the same: a copy whose counters differ stands as a stack of its own, beside the others of its card, the smaller numbers first. A browse holding more than its frame scrolls as a panel of the collection screen does. A card there reacts to the right click and the rest as a card does anywhere, shown large over the browse and taken down onto it where it stood, and reacts to no left click: a card in a browse is there to be seen and no more, and nothing is selected. A press beside the cards or the back key closes the browse.

Each pile is a button in the band, a round wearing the pile's sign, the count of its cards on a chip at its top corner: the draw pile left of the hand, its count on the corner away from the hand, and the discard pile then the exhaust pile right of the hand, theirs on the corner away from it. A pile shows no card of its own: the last card discarded is seen in the discard pile's browse. Under the pointer a pile's round is lighter, and the pointer resting on a pile raises a tooltip over it reading the pile's name. A pile holding no card keeps its place, its sign darkened and its count reading 0, and opens its browse as any other.

A card discarded or exhausted flies from the hand to its pile, turning over on the way into the card it became where it became another, shrinking as it goes and gone as it reaches the pile, and the pile's count ticks once it has landed. A card drawn leaves the draw pile as a small back, grows as it flies and turns face up where it lands. The shuffle carries one small back from the discard pile to the draw pile. A card banished leaves the hand where it lies, with no flight.

## The end-turn button ✅

The end-turn button is a square wearing its sign, an hourglass, and no word. The turn the chronicle stands on reads on a chip at its top right corner, the bare number, as a pile's count reads; on the settle phase it wears no chip. A left click on it ends the turn, or the settle phase, a click on its chip being a click on it. The pointer resting on it raises a tooltip over it reading `End turn`, or `End settle phase` on the settle phase, dead or live. As the turn ticks, the number that stood rises out of the chip and the next rises in.

It stands in the screen's bottom right corner, a little in from both edges, on a window wide enough that it stands clear of the band there; on a narrower one it stands over the exhaust pile, its right edge on the pile's and its foot just over the height the hand's resting cards rise to.

## Motion ✅

**A change plays on the map as it stood, and the map draws what the change leaves once it has played**: a unit killed where its own sight alone held the tiles is seen attacked and gone before the fog covers them.

**Every motion on the chronicle screen plays at the speed set in the Graphics window of Settings**, the one it was designed at or a faster one, and never none: however fast a change plays, it is seen. Why never none: a player skipping the motions would miss what they tell, and a test running past them would prove nothing of them.
