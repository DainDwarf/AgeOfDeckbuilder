# Chronicle screen

> How the chronicle is shown and worked on its screen: its UI, its cards, its map. A design page, under [`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md)'s legend. How any screen is worked is [`INTERFACE.md`](INTERFACE.md)'s; the rules this screen shows are [`CHRONICLE.md`](CHRONICLE.md)'s, [`MAP.md`](MAP.md)'s, [`FACTIONS.md`](FACTIONS.md)'s and [`SCHEDULE.md`](SCHEDULE.md)'s.

## The UI ✅

### The windows

The events phase's deal stands in a **window of its own**, which the end of turn's play-out ends on, and which a chronicle opened on a deal opens on; a capture's rewards stand in the same window, under the name of what was captured, each capture's in turn and before the turn ends, a reward of one copy drawn as a card of the deck is and one of several as a browse draws a stack, its badge reading the copies. The answers dealt stand in one row, centred, as cards at the deal window's own width, in the order dealt, under a title naming the event; the event's lore stands just above the row, centred, in the title's ink, and the row stands where it would without it. A capture's window reads the lore of what was captured the same way. An answer's card reads its name, `EVENT` where a card reads its kind, and what it does on this turn — how many enemies it enters, that it adds a hazard to the top of the draw pile, the text of its case where it declares cases; what it costs the player's city reads in that text and wears no chip of its own. An answer the player's city cannot pay for, or one refused, is drawn in the window as an unaffordable card of the hand is, and plain once shown large, as every card shown large is. A left click rings one, unaffordable or not; a left click on the ringed one is the choice, and the window closes on it with the landing playing out after — the next deal's window rising where one still waits, and where none does, the rest of the end of turn after a reward, the next turn's draw among it, and nothing after an answer; the choice of an unaffordable or a refused answer says why over the card, in the note a refused card raises, and the ring stays; a press beside the cards drops the ring and nothing else. The right click shows a card large over the window, and a press beside it or the back key takes it down onto the window where it stood. The back key takes down the card shown large, else drops the ring, else raises the menu as it does from a clean chronicle screen: the window closes on nothing but the choice, the menu opens over it and closes back onto it, and a new chronicle leaves it behind like anything else.

The capstone gets a **window of its own**, raised twice: when the chronicle screen opens, on a chronicle begun and on one resumed alike, and when the capstone lands, where the end of turn's play-out ends on it over the hand that turn drew, and its landing plays out once it is closed. The opening's window closes onto the screen as the chronicle stands, a deal's window rising where a deal waits. Both show its card alone, drawn as the deal window draws an answer and at the same width, `CAPSTONE` where a card reads its kind, under one title naming it the capstone, with the capstone's lore for that raising standing over the card as the deal's stands over its row — at the opening, the promise of what passes it and nothing of the turn it lands on; at the landing, that the time has come. The window offers nothing to choose, so no press rings its card and the inspection key does nothing while it stands; a left click on the card closes it, as the back key and a press beside the card do, and it is closed for good — the chronicle screen it stood on raises it again at the landing and never else, while a new chronicle opens on its own. The right click shows the card large over the window, and a press beside it or the back key takes it down onto the window where it stood, as on the deal. The menu opens over the window and closes back onto it.

A press that would land a unit of the player's on a tile the neutral holds or attack a unit of its, while the neutral is at peace, raises a **warning** before it lands, a window of the menu on its scrim titled Declare War: one line asking whether the player is sure and saying what follows, then the press itself, Move, Attack or Play, and Back. Back, the back key or a press on the scrim takes it down and nothing moves, the unit selected and lit as it was, a card being aimed still being aimed with nothing paid; the press gone through lands as it would have. Hostile, the press lands with no warning. An answer that declares war raises none: its card says so, and the choice is the press.

A chronicle that has ended stands on its **ending screen**, which rises with its scrim out of nothing as the ending plays out and never comes down: it reads the outcome — victory, or defeat with what ended the chronicle and the turn it ended on — and under it a ledger of what the chronicle paid, as [`META.md`](META.md) says: one row per achievement reached, a check mark and its technology's name, and at the row's end the influence it paid, a diamond and the number, which a row that paid none does not read; then one row per influence card kept that carries influence, its name and, where it kept several copies, their count after it, and at the row's end the influence all its copies paid; a challenge kept to a defeat keeps its row, greyed, reading the influence its copies would have paid, and a line in that same grey strikes the whole row through, from the name's left edge to the number's right edge; a card that carries no influence has no row; then, under a rule where a row stands over it, the influence paid in all, a diamond, the word and the number, which reads 0 where nothing was paid. The outcome, the ledger and the **End chronicle** button under it are one block, centred on the screen. The button is drawn as a button of the menu is and opens the campaign screen; it rises with the screen and answers no press until the screen has risen. The scrim swallows every other press, the menu opens over the screen and closes back onto it, and the campaign screen is the way out, by the button or by the menu's **Campaign**. The ending screen is seen once: the chronicle has left the save, and the game boots next on the campaign screen.

### The right click, the back key and the menu

The right click finds a tile, a card of the hand, a pile or a card in a browse, in city mode and while a card is being aimed alike; a tile it lands on afresh comes up at its first card, the selection's own tile excepted: there an inspection, by the right click and by the inspection key alike, comes up at the card of what is selected, the unit's for a unit and for a tile the first of the tile's own, and steps on through every card the tile holds.

The back key takes one step more here than the presses name: after the selection comes city mode.

The menu opens over the chronicle screen as over any screen, and the screen waits under it: the selection, being aimed or not, and the inspection.

### The pinned achievements

While the campaign pins technologies whose achievements the chronicle reads, those achievements stand in the map's top left corner, one under the other in the order the chronicle reads them, 8 apart, the first as far from the map's top and left edges as a mode's chip stands from its top and right ones, each on the panel's paper and as wide as a plate of the tree. Each reads its technology's name; at the right end of the name's line, the achievement's count over its need, which a need of one does not read; and under the name the goal as the plate reads it, wrapped onto as many lines as it takes, the paper as tall as they stand. Once an achievement is reached it is sunk in a well and reads the check mark and the name alone, the paper as tall as that one line, and those under it close up. A name in a goal answers the rest and the right click as on a plate. They answer no press; the wheel over them zooms the map, as over the resource bar. They stand on the settle phase and in city mode as on any turn. A pinned technology whose achievement the chronicle does not read stands there as nothing, and a campaign pinning none the chronicle reads shows nothing there.

### The shown event

While a card has shown the turn of the next landing, that turn stands in a strip just over the end-turn button, as wide as the button, as a pinned achievement is: one line, centred, reading `Event on turn` and the turn, whatever is to land. It answers no press; the wheel over it zooms the map, as over a pinned achievement. It stands in city mode as on any turn, and goes as the turn it reads begins.

### The resource bar

The resource bar reads food as the stock over the growth threshold, culture as the stock over the culture threshold, and the idle population alone, under the word idle.

After each value, at a smaller size, the bar reads what ending the turn now would do to it. A stock reads the income less what the upkeep and the hand's strikes take, signed: `+1`, `−2`. Idle reads the population growth brings or the upkeep and the strikes take: `+1`, `−1`. A stock the upkeep or the strikes take past nothing reads the income less the whole of what the upkeep and the strikes ask of it, `−6` on a stock of nothing, in the colour of a stock taken past nothing, and the idle reading of the population taken is in that colour too. Nothing is read where the end of turn changes nothing. The reading is the end of turn's own run on the chronicle as it stands, the stocks read as the strikes leave them, before growth spends food, and idle read after growth. It stands on every turn the screen stands on, never on the settle phase nor while a deal stands, and is down from the End turn press until the screen stands on the next hand, the choice in a deal's window and what plays out after it included; every other play-out leaves it standing, and it changes with each change that lands, as the value does.

Culture and idle are the two readings that enter city mode, and each fills its well in a colour of its own while city mode has that act waiting: culture's while the stock covers the culture threshold and a tile the player's city may claim stands, idle's while one population is idle and a tile the player's city holds has nobody on it, its own tile and an occupied one no exception. The well is the one a latched reading sits in, sunk the same way, in that colour in place of grey; a reading latched and filled at once is in that colour.

## The cards ✅

### The hand and the aim

A left click selects a card of the hand, unaffordable or not. A selected card that aims at a tile or at a unit is **being aimed** from that moment: the map is already there, and lights the tiles its aim admits, or rings the units it admits where it aims at a unit. A second click plays the card — one that aims at nothing is played where it stands, and refused over the card with its reason when it is unaffordable; one that aims at a tile or at a unit lands nowhere, no tile lying under a card in the hand, so it stays selected; one that aims at the hand is being aimed from then, where it lies, or refused over the card as one that aims at nothing is. A card aimed at the hand waits for it because a click on another card of the hand selects that card under any selection, and is the play only once the card is being aimed.

**A card being aimed says so on the card itself.** It wears a point on its ring, over its top edge and towards the map, and one line stands over the hand, naming the card by its own name and what it is played at, a tile, a unit or a card of the hand. The ring, the point and the line are in the selection's colour, the one the map rings the selected tile in. Point and line come up the moment the card is being aimed and go when it is let go of, so a card selected and waiting to be played reads apart from one being aimed without reading the map.

**Every card being aimed is aimed at one kind of thing.** A card that aims at a tile or at a unit is aimed at a drawn tile, and one that aims at a unit is refused on a tile no unit of the player's stands on before its own reasons are asked; a card that aims at the hand is aimed at every other card of the hand. A play refused says the one reason it is turned down, and the card stays selected. A drag that lifts a card of the hand lets the selected card go as it lifts, the lifted card itself and a card being aimed among them, whatever it was aimed at, and the card stays let go where the lifted one comes home.

**A card being aimed reads the yield it would give.** While the pointer is on a tile the card's aim admits, and the card's play would place a building or an improvement on it, that tile wears what the thing placed would give it, and each tile beside it wears what the building placed would give it, fainter where one of its kind already gives to that tile; a tile the play would give nothing wears nothing. It is read as the play would leave the tile, whether the player's city can pay for the card or not, on the faces the map draws and under the rule the capstone lays over the yield once it has landed, as the yield overlay reads. It is the pointer's reading as any other: it stands under a pointer that never moved, goes when the pointer leaves the tile, and goes with the aim.

**A card played through a unit beside its tile asks which unit only where it must.** A click on a tile one such unit stands beside plays the card through it. Where several do, the click plays nothing: the card is from then being aimed at a unit, its line says so, the map rings those units, the tile clicked keeping its rim, and a click on one of them plays the card through it at that tile. A click on any other unit of the player's is refused over it and the aim stands, as any card aimed at a unit; letting the card go lets go of the tile with it.

**The drag is the two clicks in one gesture.** A card lifted clear of the hand and released there is selected and played at once: one that aims at a tile or at a unit stays selected, being aimed; one that aims at the hand stays selected, being aimed where it lies; an unaffordable one stays selected under its refusal note.

### The piles

A right click on a pile opens its **browse**, under a title reading the pile's name and the count of its cards. Every card the pile holds stands once, as a stack of its copies, a badge on the front card reading them, eight stacks to a line from the frame's top left, in the order of the collection, so the draw order is given away to nobody. Copies stand in one stack only where they read the same: a copy whose counters differ stands as a stack of its own, beside the others of its card, the smaller numbers first. A browse holding more than its frame scrolls as a panel of the collection screen does. A card there answers the right click and the rest as a card does anywhere, shown large over the browse and taken down onto it where it stood, and answers no left click: a card in a browse is there to be seen and no more, and nothing is selected. A press beside the cards or the back key closes the browse. A left click on a pile does nothing of its own. The exhaust pile is no stack of cards: it is an upright tab coming out of the screen's right edge beside the discard pile, 32 wide, as tall as a resting card and standing on its baseline, its word and its count read upwards, and the discard pile and the end-turn button keep from the tab the margin they keep from the screen's edge elsewhere. It answers the right click with its browse and the left click with nothing, as a pile does, and rests on nothing. A card exhausted flies from the hand to the tab as a discarded card flies to the discard pile, turning over into the card it became on the way, and the tab's count ticks once it has landed; a card banished leaves the hand where it lies, with no flight. A name on the discard pile's top card answers the rest and the right click as a name does anywhere, and its kind label answers the rest.

## The map ✅

### The map's presses

The chronicle screen has two modes. Out of city mode a left click on a tile selects it, and the map rings it; on a tile a unit stands on, in sight or kept in fog as last seen, it selects the unit, whoever's it is, and the map rings the unit. A left click on a selected unit selects its tile in its place, and one on a tile selected under a unit selects that unit again, so successive clicks there switch between the two, the unit first. A selected unit of the player's lights every tile its move points reach, as one region with its own tile among them, and marks with a ring in the enemies' own colour every unit its attack reaches, from where it stands or after a step that brings it in range; a tile selected under a unit lights and marks nothing. A click on a lit tile is that unit's next step and selects nothing; a click on a marked unit is that unit's attack on it, the step that brings it in range first where it takes one, and selects nothing either. A unit that lights or marks anything is being aimed from the moment it is selected, at the tiles it lights and the units it marks: nothing of that kind is refused, a click on any other tile lets it go and selects that tile or the unit on it, and a click on its own tile lets it go for that tile. The selection follows the unit to where it stands after either — where it landed, or where it attacked from — lit and marked again with what it has left. A drag from the unit onto a lit tile or a marked unit is the same step or the same attack: a unit dragged lights and marks from the start of its drag as a selected one does, wears no ring, and leaves the selection where it was; let go anywhere else, the unit comes home, what was selected lit again as it stood, and a unit that can do nothing is grabbed and comes home just the same, without a word. A unit with no action left marks nothing, and a press on the enemy beside it selects that enemy like any other unit. A unit of the player's that can do nothing on the map is dimmed where it stands, so the units that still have something to do there read at a glance: it has no tile in its reach, its attack reaches no unit, and, a worker, it has no action left, the card its action goes to being what a worker waits for. An attack that would declare war counts for nothing there, and a step counts wherever it lands. The dim is read off the chronicle as it stands, so a dimmed unit is lit again as soon as a tile comes in its reach or a unit in its attack's. No unit is dimmed on the settle phase, which is no turn. An enemy is never dimmed: the enemy phase spends its action and the same end of turn refreshes it. A unit that has prepared wears a badge on itself, a flame where it will pillage and a flag where it will capture, from the enemy phase that prepared it until the next resolves it or the unit is killed; a unit a tile in fog keeps as last seen prepared wears it still, darkened with the rest of it. A unit below its full health wears a health bar on itself, whoever's it is, from the change that hurt it until it is healed to full or killed; a unit a tile in fog keeps as last seen hurt wears it still, at the health last seen, darkened with the rest of it. A left click on a selected tile no unit stands on changes nothing, the player's city's own tile excepted: a left click on that tile selected enters city mode, a unit standing on it or not. Nothing more.

**A unit being aimed shows its path.** The lit tile the pointer is on wears the unit's path to it, the tiles it would cross from where it stands, and at its end the move points the unit would have left; a unit dragged over a lit tile shows the same. Once the pointer leaves the lit tiles the path stays on the last one it was on, until the pointer is on a lit tile again, which takes the path, on a marked unit, which reads the attack in its place, or on the unit's own tile, which wears none, or the unit is let go. A step or an attack lights the unit anew and keeps no path, and a tile lit no longer loses its path. The path is the pointer's reading as any other: it stands under a pointer that never moved.

**A unit being aimed reads its attack.** The marked unit the pointer is on wears the attack: an arrow from the unit being aimed to it, and on its health bar the health the attack would remove, the unit's damage as its card reads it or all the health the marked unit holds where that is less, with a skull in the heart's place where the attack would kill it; a unit dragged over a marked unit shows the same. Where the attack takes a step first, the unit's path to the tile it would attack from is drawn with it, the move points left at its end as on any path, the arrow leaves from that tile, and the health read is the damage the unit would carry there. The attack is read as the path is and in its place, one of the two at a time: it stays on the last marked unit the pointer was on until the pointer is on another marked unit, which takes it, on a lit tile, which takes the path, or on the unit's own tile, which wears neither, or the unit is let go, and a step or an attack keeps none.

**Beside the things** on the chronicle screen is the map beyond the tiles it draws, the band the hand and the piles stand in, and the resource bar's paper between its readings: a press there lands on no thing.

### The pointer and the tile's marks

**What the pointer rests on reads as under it, however the pointer came to be there.** The end-turn button reads `End turn` whenever the pointer stands on it while it is live, `End settle phase` on the settle phase: the next turn rolls in on the button during the end of turn's play-out, and the moment the play-out ends under a pointer that never moved, the button reads `End turn` again, so a turn ends where the last one did. A pointer that left the game over a thing and comes back straight onto it finds that thing as a pointer moved onto it does.

**A tile's marks stand in four places.** Its feature and its improvements stand in one row above its middle, the feature first and then the improvements in the order they were placed, the row centred whatever it holds; its building stands in the middle, and the unit on the tile over it, below the middle; the mark of an assigned tile stands below the middle, under the unit standing there. A city's own tile wears a badge below its middle, over the unit standing there: a disc in that city's colour reading its population, widening for a second digit; it is drawn where the tile is drawn live and not in fog, so the player's city wears it always and the neutral's while its tile is in sight. The player's city wears two bars beside its badge, along the bottom of its building and over the unit too: the food stock against the growth threshold, over the culture stock against the culture threshold. Each fills from its left, is full from its threshold on, and past what the stock holds shows, paler, what ending the turn would add to it; a turn that would take from a stock shows nothing more on its bar. The paler part is down whenever the resource bar's reading is. The neutral's city wears no bar. The halo round a unit of the player's, the player's city's badge, its border and the emblem on a card's back are in the civilization's colour. The halo round a unit of the neutral's, its city's badge, and its border are in the neutral's colour. The border is drawn from each tile as the map draws it, so a tile in fog keeps the stretch of border it wore when last seen. An embarked unit's mark shows it embarked.

### The veils and the infopanel

**The debug console switches the two veils that hide a tile**, one entry to each, `uncharted` and `fog`, which the chronicle screen alone holds. Both veils stand until a switch takes one off, and each answers the state its own is left in; they change what is drawn and nothing the rules read. With the **uncharted veil** off, every tile of the disc is drawn, an uncharted one included, and the rivers along them with it; a tile out of sight is still darkened, and one that has no snapshot is drawn as it stands. With the **fog veil** off, every tile the map draws is drawn live — as it stands, with whoever stands on it, and darkened by nothing — so the map shows every tile charted clearly and leaves the uncharted ones out. Both off draw the whole map as the rules hold it.

**The rest of the screen reads what the map draws.** The yield overlay glyphs the tiles the map draws, each from the faces it draws, the tile's and those beside it, so a tile in fog is read as it was last seen. An inspection reads that same face, and a tile in fog holds the card of the unit its snapshot keeps, read as the unit stood when last seen; whether a unit of the neutral's is hostile is no unit's own, and is read as it stands. A press on an uncharted tile lands off the map — it selects nothing, inspects nothing and acts on nothing — and no card is aimed at one: the map lights only the tiles it draws. Each veil taken off widens all of it with the map.

**A change plays on the map as it stood, and the map draws what the change leaves once it has played**: a unit killed where its own sight alone held the tiles is seen attacked and gone before the fog covers them.

**Every motion on the chronicle screen plays at the speed set in the Graphics window of Settings**, the one it was designed at or a faster one, and never none: however fast a change plays, it is seen. Why never none: a player skipping the motions would miss what they tell, and a test running past them would prove nothing of them.

An inspected tile shows one card in the infopanel per step. A tile has at most three, in this order: the unit standing on it; the building with the tile's improvements; and the terrain with its feature and the river running along it. A card is absent when nothing fills it, and the terrain card never is. The unit card is headed by the unit's kind and reads its stats, and a line under the name reads the unit's state: Raft for an embarked unit, whose stats it reads as it has them embarked, Pillaging or Capturing for a unit that has prepared, Hostile for a unit of the neutral's while it is hostile, joined where a unit has more than one, and no line for a unit in no state; the other two are headed by their outermost layer and show a row per thing they hold — the river's among them on the terrain card — with what it gives at income, a row that gives nothing saying so. The terrain card reads the tile's movement cost small in the top-right corner of its head, in move points — the cost a unit pays, or, where none does, the cost an embarked unit pays — and a dash where nothing crosses the tile at all. The river's row carries one line under it: a crossing ends the move. A building that gives to the tiles beside it carries one line under its row saying so, and its row reads what it gives the tile it stands on; the terrain card of a tile it gives to holds a row for it, drawn as the building's own with what it gives that tile and no line, once however many of its kind stand beside the tile. A building that takes upkeep carries one line under its row reading it, the word and what it takes as a chip, after the beside line where it carries both. After the last card comes the first again, and a tile of a single card holds it: the cycle never falls back to the bare ring. A name on a card that names a terrain, a feature, an improvement, a building or a unit kind shows that thing as one of these cards, small at a rest and large at a right click, drawn at the size it is shown at: headed by the thing named, with its one row and what it gives at income, the terrain's movement cost in its corner and no other card's; a unit kind's card reads the kind's stats as a unit fresh of it reads them, full over full, its halo in the colour of the faction the card's text names it for. Its rows raise no tooltip.

### City mode

In **city mode** a left click on a tile selects the tile, whoever stands on it and never the unit, and lights no unit: no press there moves one or attacks with one. A left click on the selection is the player's city's act on that tile — one idle population assigned to it, or the one standing there unassigned, by turns, its own tile among the tiles it works; or the tile claimed; it stays selected after any of them. A drag from a tile the population stands on onto a tile the player's city holds and nobody stands on takes it off the one and puts it on the other in that one gesture, the tile it lands on selected; let go anywhere else, the population comes home and nothing changes. A tile the player's city may claim wears the culture threshold in its middle from the moment it is selected — a minus, the number and culture's glyph — and it raises no note. An act the rules refuse plays nothing and says why over the tile, in the note a refused card raises; a claim the player's city cannot pay for says what it costs, in that same note, the tile going on wearing the culture it asks for. A click on a tile the player's city neither holds nor can claim selects it like any other and acts on nothing. A press beside the tiles leaves the mode standing; leaving the mode drops the selection and the inspection with it. The map marks the tiles the player's city can claim for as long as the mode is on, and every tile inside the border shows what it yields — every resource, whatever the yield overlay is showing, the dim of a tile nobody stands on no exception, and with no dim over the map: that dim is the overlay's alone. It is entered by the **city key**, by a left click on the player's city's own tile once that tile is selected, or by pressing culture or idle on the resource bar, and left by that key, by the back key, or by the chip naming the mode; the map's frame drawn in city mode's own colour and that chip are how the chronicle screen shows the mode is on. Coming into city mode lets go of a card being aimed, of the selection and of its inspection, and a window standing over the chronicle screen takes the city key instead.

In city mode the map marks the assigned tiles and dims the held ones that are not; a unit stands over the mark and over the dim, darkened by neither.

### The settle phase

**On the settle phase** the chronicle screen shows the centre part and nothing else, the hand holds the settle cards, the player's city has no border, and the end-turn button reads the phase and is dead until the player's city stands. The map's frame drawn in the settle phase's own colour, a chip naming the phase where city mode's chip stands, and the end-turn button filled in that same colour are how the chronicle screen shows the phase is on; the chip answers no press, and all three go as the turn ticks to turn 1. City mode is not entered while the player's city stands nowhere: the city key and the two readings that enter it do nothing. Entered once the player's city stands, its own frame and chip stand in place of the settle phase's until it is left, and the button keeps the phase's colour.

### The yield overlay

The **yield overlay** shows what every tile the map draws yields: a glyph for each point of each resource, in the colour that resource is known by, over a map dimmed under them. It reads the faces the map draws, the tile's and those beside it, and the rule the capstone lays over the yield once it has landed, and nothing else — whoever stands there and whatever occupies it change what the tile gives at income, never what the overlay shows, and a tile in fog is read as it was last seen. Pressing one of the five core readings on the resource bar toggles that resource in or out of the overlay, several at a time, and the reading of a resource shown is latched down in the bar; the **yield key** clears the overlay, or, from nothing, shows every resource, culture included, and a window standing over the chronicle screen takes the key instead. The dim darkens the land and what is built on it, city mode's marks among it, and nothing else: the marks of the selection and the aim, every unit, the culture threshold the selected tile wears and the infopanel stand over it. It is a display and not a mode: the back key leaves it standing, city mode stands with it, and either is entered and left without touching the other.

### The Icons button

The **Icons** button shows or hides the discs of every tile's row, the features' and the improvements', with what they wear. Hidden, a feature and an improvement are told by what lies on the land alone, and an improvement under a building that fills its tile by nothing on the map. The button stands in the map's bottom-left corner, as far from the map's left edge as a mode's chip stands from its right one and 8 over the draw pile, drawn as the Menu button is, and sunk in a well while the discs are shown, as a latched reading of the resource bar is. A press on it or the **icons key** switches them at once, and a window standing over the chronicle screen takes the key instead. It is a display and not a mode, as the yield overlay is: the back key leaves it standing, city mode and the settle phase stand with it, and every chronicle screen opens with the discs shown. It changes the tiles' rows and nothing that reads the map: a tile in fog loses its discs with the rest, and the yield overlay, the infopanel, the selection and an aim read on as they did. It raises no tooltip.
