# Chronicle screen

> How the chronicle is shown and worked on its screen, around the map: its windows, its bar, its hand and its piles. A design page, under [`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md)'s legend. How the map is shown and worked there is [`MAP-SCREEN.md`](MAP-SCREEN.md)'s; how any screen is worked is [`INTERFACE.md`](INTERFACE.md)'s; the rules this screen shows are [`CHRONICLE.md`](CHRONICLE.md)'s, [`MAP.md`](MAP.md)'s, [`FACTIONS.md`](FACTIONS.md)'s and [`SCHEDULE.md`](SCHEDULE.md)'s.

## The deal window ✅

The events phase's deal stands in a **window of its own**, which the end of turn's play-out ends on, and which a chronicle opened on a deal opens on; a capture's rewards stand in the same window, under the name of what was captured, each capture's in turn and before the turn ends, a reward of one copy drawn as a card of the deck is and one of several as a browse draws a stack, its badge reading the copies. The answers dealt stand in one row, centred, as cards at the deal window's own width, in the order dealt, under a title naming the event; the event's lore stands just above the row, centred, in the title's ink, and the row stands where it would without it. A capture's window reads the lore of what was captured the same way.

An answer's card reads its name, `EVENT` where a card reads its kind, and what it does on this turn — how many enemies it enters, that it adds a hazard to the top of the draw pile, the text of its case where it declares cases; what it costs the player's city reads in that text and wears no chip of its own. An answer the player's city cannot pay for, or one refused, is drawn in the window as an unaffordable card of the hand is, and plain once shown large, as every card shown large is.

A left click rings one, unaffordable or not; a left click on the ringed one is the choice, and the window closes on it with the landing playing out after — the next deal's window rising where one still waits, and where none does, the rest of the end of turn after a reward, the next turn's draw among it, and nothing after an answer; the choice of an unaffordable or a refused answer says why over the card, in the note a refused card raises, and the ring stays; a press beside the cards drops the ring and nothing else. The right click shows a card large over the window, and a press beside it or the back key takes it down onto the window where it stood. The back key takes down the card shown large, else drops the ring, else raises the menu as it does from a clean chronicle screen: the window closes on nothing but the choice, the menu opens over it and closes back onto it, and a new chronicle leaves it behind like anything else.

## The capstone's window ✅

The capstone gets a **window of its own**, raised twice: when the chronicle screen opens, on a chronicle begun and on one resumed alike, and when the capstone lands, where the end of turn's play-out ends on it over the hand that turn drew, and its landing plays out once it is closed. The opening's window closes onto the screen as the chronicle stands, a deal's window rising where a deal waits. Both show its card alone, drawn as the deal window draws an answer and at the same width, `CAPSTONE` where a card reads its kind, under one title naming it the capstone, with the capstone's lore for that raising standing over the card as the deal's stands over its row — at the opening, the promise of what passes it and nothing of the turn it lands on; at the landing, that the time has come. The window offers nothing to choose, so no press rings its card and the inspection key does nothing while it stands; a left click on the card closes it, as the back key and a press beside the card do, and it is closed for good — the chronicle screen it stood on raises it again at the landing and never else, while a new chronicle opens on its own. The right click shows the card large over the window, and a press beside it or the back key takes it down onto the window where it stood, as on the deal. The menu opens over the window and closes back onto it.

## The war warning ✅

A press that would land a unit of the player's on a tile the neutral holds or attack a unit of its, while the neutral is at peace, raises a **warning** before it lands, a window of the menu on its scrim titled Declare War: one line asking whether the player is sure and saying what follows, then the press itself, Move, Attack or Play, and Back. Back, the back key or a press on the scrim takes it down and nothing moves, the unit selected and lit as it was, a card being aimed still being aimed with nothing paid; the press gone through lands as it would have. Hostile, the press lands with no warning. An answer that declares war raises none: its card says so, and the choice is the press.

## The ending screen ✅

A chronicle that has ended stands on its **ending screen**, which rises with its scrim out of nothing as the ending plays out and never comes down: it reads the outcome — victory, or defeat with what ended the chronicle and the turn it ended on — and under it a ledger of what the chronicle paid, as [`META.md`](META.md) says: one row per achievement reached, a check mark and its technology's name, and at the row's end the influence it paid, a diamond and the number, which a row that paid none does not read; then one row per influence card kept that carries influence, its name and, where it kept several copies, their count after it, and at the row's end the influence all its copies paid; a challenge kept to a defeat keeps its row, greyed, reading the influence its copies would have paid, and a line in that same grey strikes the whole row through, from the name's left edge to the number's right edge; a card that carries no influence has no row; then, under a rule where a row stands over it, the influence paid in all, a diamond, the word and the number, which reads 0 where nothing was paid.

The outcome, the ledger and the **End chronicle** button under it are one block, centred on the screen. The button is drawn as a button of the menu is and opens the campaign screen; it rises with the screen and answers no press until the screen has risen. The scrim swallows every other press, the menu opens over the screen and closes back onto it, and the campaign screen is the way out, by the button or by the menu's **Campaign**. The ending screen is seen once: the chronicle has left the save, and the game boots next on the campaign screen.

## The right click, the back key and the menu ✅

The right click finds a tile, a card of the hand, a pile or a card in a browse, in city mode and while a card is being aimed alike; a tile it lands on afresh comes up at its first card, the selection's own tile excepted: there an inspection, by the right click and by the inspection key alike, comes up at the card of what is selected, the unit's for a unit and for a tile the first of the tile's own, and steps on through every card the tile holds.

The back key takes one step more here than the presses name: after the selection comes city mode.

The menu opens over the chronicle screen as over any screen, and the screen waits under it: the selection, being aimed or not, and the inspection.

## The pointer ✅

**What the pointer rests on reads as under it, however the pointer came to be there.** The end-turn button reads `End turn` whenever the pointer stands on it while it is live, `End settle phase` on the settle phase: the next turn rolls in on the button during the end of turn's play-out, and the moment the play-out ends under a pointer that never moved, the button reads `End turn` again, so a turn ends where the last one did. A pointer that left the game over a thing and comes back straight onto it finds that thing as a pointer moved onto it does.

**Beside the things** on the chronicle screen is the map beyond the tiles it draws, the band the hand and the piles stand in, and the resource bar's paper between its readings: a press there lands on no thing.

## The resource bar ✅

The resource bar reads food as the stock over the growth threshold, culture as the stock over the culture threshold, and the idle population alone, under the word idle.

After each value, at a smaller size, the bar reads what ending the turn now would do to it. A stock reads the income less what the upkeep and the hand's strikes take, signed: `+1`, `−2`. Idle reads the population growth brings or the upkeep and the strikes take: `+1`, `−1`. A stock the upkeep or the strikes take past nothing reads the income less the whole of what the upkeep and the strikes ask of it, `−6` on a stock of nothing, in the colour of a stock taken past nothing, and the idle reading of the population taken is in that colour too. Nothing is read where the end of turn changes nothing. The reading is the end of turn's own run on the chronicle as it stands, the stocks read as the strikes leave them, before growth spends food, and idle read after growth. It stands on every turn the screen stands on, never on the settle phase nor while a deal stands, and is down from the End turn press until the screen stands on the next hand, the choice in a deal's window and what plays out after it included; every other play-out leaves it standing, and it changes with each change that lands, as the value does.

Culture and idle are the two readings that enter city mode, and each fills its well in a colour of its own while city mode has that act waiting: culture's while the stock covers the culture threshold and a tile the player's city may claim stands, idle's while one population is idle and a tile the player's city holds has nobody on it, its own tile and an occupied one no exception. The well is the one a latched reading sits in, sunk the same way, in that colour in place of grey; a reading latched and filled at once is in that colour.

## The pinned achievements ✅

While the campaign pins technologies whose achievements the chronicle reads, those achievements stand in the map's top left corner, one under the other in the order the chronicle reads them, 8 apart, the first as far from the map's top and left edges as a mode's chip stands from its top and right ones, each on the panel's paper and as wide as a plate of the tree. Each reads its technology's name; at the right end of the name's line, the achievement's count over its need, which a need of one does not read; and under the name the goal as the plate reads it, wrapped onto as many lines as it takes, the paper as tall as they stand. Once an achievement is reached it is sunk in a well and reads the check mark and the name alone, the paper as tall as that one line, and those under it close up. A name in a goal answers the rest and the right click as on a plate. They answer no press; the wheel over them zooms the map, as over the resource bar. They stand on the settle phase and in city mode as on any turn. A pinned technology whose achievement the chronicle does not read stands there as nothing, and a campaign pinning none the chronicle reads shows nothing there.

## The shown event ✅

While a card has shown the turn of the next landing, that turn stands in a strip just over the end-turn button, as wide as the button, as a pinned achievement is: one line, centred, reading `Event on turn` and the turn, whatever is to land. It answers no press; the wheel over it zooms the map, as over a pinned achievement. It stands in city mode as on any turn, and goes as the turn it reads begins.

## The hand and the aim ✅

A left click selects a card of the hand, unaffordable or not. A selected card that aims at a tile or at a unit is **being aimed** from that moment: the map is already there, and lights the tiles its aim admits, or rings the units it admits where it aims at a unit. A second click plays the card — one that aims at nothing is played where it stands, and refused over the card with its reason when it is unaffordable; one that aims at a tile or at a unit lands nowhere, no tile lying under a card in the hand, so it stays selected; one that aims at the hand is being aimed from then, where it lies, or refused over the card as one that aims at nothing is. A card aimed at the hand waits for it because a click on another card of the hand selects that card under any selection, and is the play only once the card is being aimed.

**A card being aimed says so on the card itself.** It wears a point on its ring, over its top edge and towards the map, and one line stands over the hand, naming the card by its own name and what it is played at, a tile, a unit or a card of the hand. The ring, the point and the line are in the selection's colour, the one the map rings the selected tile in. Point and line come up the moment the card is being aimed and go when it is let go of, so a card selected and waiting to be played reads apart from one being aimed without reading the map.

**Every card being aimed is aimed at one kind of thing.** A card that aims at a tile or at a unit is aimed at a drawn tile, and one that aims at a unit is refused on a tile no unit of the player's stands on before its own reasons are asked; a card that aims at the hand is aimed at every other card of the hand. A play refused says the one reason it is turned down, and the card stays selected. A drag that lifts a card of the hand lets the selected card go as it lifts, the lifted card itself and a card being aimed among them, whatever it was aimed at, and the card stays let go where the lifted one comes home.

**A card being aimed reads the yield it would give.** While the pointer is on a tile the card's aim admits, and the card's play would place a building or an improvement on it, that tile wears what the thing placed would give it, and each tile beside it wears what the building placed would give it, fainter where one of its kind already gives to that tile; a tile the play would give nothing wears nothing. It is read as the play would leave the tile, whether the player's city can pay for the card or not, on the faces the map draws and under the rule the capstone lays over the yield once it has landed, as the yield overlay reads. It is the pointer's reading as any other: it stands under a pointer that never moved, goes when the pointer leaves the tile, and goes with the aim.

**A card played through a unit beside its tile asks which unit only where it must.** A click on a tile one such unit stands beside plays the card through it. Where several do, the click plays nothing: the card is from then being aimed at a unit, its line says so, the map rings those units, the tile clicked keeping its rim, and a click on one of them plays the card through it at that tile. A click on any other unit of the player's is refused over it and the aim stands, as any card aimed at a unit; letting the card go lets go of the tile with it.

**The drag is the two clicks in one gesture.** A card lifted clear of the hand and released there is selected and played at once: one that aims at a tile or at a unit stays selected, being aimed; one that aims at the hand stays selected, being aimed where it lies; an unaffordable one stays selected under its refusal note.

## The piles ✅

A right click on a pile opens its **browse**, under a title reading the pile's name and the count of its cards. Every card the pile holds stands once, as a stack of its copies, a badge on the front card reading them, eight stacks to a line from the frame's top left, in the order of the collection, so the draw order is given away to nobody. Copies stand in one stack only where they read the same: a copy whose counters differ stands as a stack of its own, beside the others of its card, the smaller numbers first. A browse holding more than its frame scrolls as a panel of the collection screen does. A card there answers the right click and the rest as a card does anywhere, shown large over the browse and taken down onto it where it stood, and answers no left click: a card in a browse is there to be seen and no more, and nothing is selected. A press beside the cards or the back key closes the browse. A left click on a pile does nothing of its own.

The exhaust pile is no stack of cards: it is an upright tab coming out of the screen's right edge beside the discard pile, 32 wide, as tall as a resting card and standing on its baseline, its word and its count read upwards, and the discard pile and the end-turn button keep from the tab the margin they keep from the screen's edge elsewhere. It answers the right click with its browse and the left click with nothing, as a pile does, and rests on nothing. A card exhausted flies from the hand to the tab as a discarded card flies to the discard pile, turning over into the card it became on the way, and the tab's count ticks once it has landed; a card banished leaves the hand where it lies, with no flight.

A name on the discard pile's top card answers the rest and the right click as a name does anywhere, and its kind label answers the rest.

## Motion ✅

**A change plays on the map as it stood, and the map draws what the change leaves once it has played**: a unit killed where its own sight alone held the tiles is seen attacked and gone before the fog covers them.

**Every motion on the chronicle screen plays at the speed set in the Graphics window of Settings**, the one it was designed at or a faster one, and never none: however fast a change plays, it is seen. Why never none: a player skipping the motions would miss what they tell, and a test running past them would prove nothing of them.
