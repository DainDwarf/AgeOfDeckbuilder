# Schedule

> The schedule a chronicle lives through: its events and its capstone. A design page, under [`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md)'s legend. The rules a chronicle is played by are [`CHRONICLE.md`](CHRONICLE.md)'s, and the region that names a schedule is [`MAP.md`](MAP.md)'s.

## The schedule ✅

Each region has a **schedule** of its own — regions share events, never a schedule: its events, each with a weight, the spacing between them, its capstones, and the window the capstone's turn is drawn from. The schedule is rolled into the chronicle's **timeline** by a generator of the timeline's own that the opening seeds: the capstone, drawn among the schedule's, each alike, its turn, and the turn the next deal is due on, rolled from the spacing by the landing before it. Nothing the player does steps that generator, so a seed deals on the same turns whatever is played, and the same events wherever the same ones can be dealt. The chronicle has no last turn: the schedule deals for as long as the player's city stands. The player is never shown the timeline, but for the one turn a card may show of it.

On a due turn, and no sooner, the Events phase deals one event, seeded and weighted among the schedule's. An event is dealt on every map and whatever the player has done: it names no need of the chronicle. Its answers stand on an equal footing: all harms, all gains, or all mixed, each a gain and a harm in one. An answer that does nothing is dealt beside mixed answers alone: beside harms it is the plain choice, beside gains the plain refusal. A weight not above nought is a defect of the content, refused before any chronicle is dealt. One event at a time, never two on one turn.

**An event deals its answers** as cards, two or more, each with a cost of its own; an answer is a script run on the chronicle, and what it does is content. The player chooses one; it pays its cost, then lands on the chronicle as it stands. An answer's cost may read the chronicle: the deal shows it as the chronicle stands, and the choice pays that. An answer's reading is what its card says of this turn, and a card the answer names is shown as the answer will add it: made at the counters the reading hands under the names that card declares. An answer may declare cases and name one of them on the chronicle as it stands, and its card then reads the text of that case; an answer declaring none reads its one text. An answer the player's city cannot pay for is unaffordable and is not chosen. An answer may name what refuses it on the chronicle as it stands, as a card's aim does: a refused answer is dealt and not chosen, and the deal says why. Every event carries an answer that costs no stock and is refused by nothing whatever the chronicle, so a deal is never empty. The turn stops on the deal, and the chronicle takes no command but the choice until every deal standing is chosen from — a capture's rewards, dealt before the turn ticks, then the turn's event, dealt onto the hand the turn drew.

**An event lands near a city.** Where an answer or a strike places something onto a tile or takes something off one, the tile is drawn **near a city**: among the tiles that qualify for it, one drawn seeded, each weighing less by a fixed factor 🔧 for every tile it lies from the nearest tile that city holds, the held tiles at full weight, the city's own tile among them, and never a tile the other city holds; so the far tiles are drawn only while none nearer stands, and the draw is never empty while one qualifying tile stands on the map. Where several are taken, each is drawn anew among those left. A draw that qualifies no tile takes nothing, or is refused, as its answer says.

**An event carries lore**: a short fiction, a sentence or three, saying what is happening to the player's city, since an answer read alone makes no sense. A capture that deals rewards carries one too, and the capstone one for each time its window is raised. Lore is fiction and not rules: it decides nothing, what an answer does is on its card, and the glossary does not bind it, so it says tribe where the rules say population.

The schedule escalates in what lands, never in how often or in what it draws: every event weighs the same on every turn, and an event dealt late is harsher than one dealt early, whichever answer is chosen.

Events are not announced: the player learns each one when it lands. The capstone is the exception in what, never in when: the player knows the trial from the chronicle's opening and learns its turn when it lands. A card may show the turn of the next landing: the turn the next event is due on, or the capstone's where it lands first, read as an event's and never saying which. The turn is shown from the card's play until it comes, and a card that would show it is refused while one is shown. What lands is still learned when it lands.

## The capstone ✅

**The capstone is not an event.** It is the region's trial, the one the opening drew: what it lands, one script run on the chronicle as it stands, and a second script it may carry, run on every turn after the one it lands on, after the draw and before whatever that turn deals. A capstone may carry a rule over what a tile yields, laid from its landing to the chronicle's end over every tile of the map and read wherever a tile's yield is read: at income, through a card played on the tile, by the neutral's script and on the screen. It deals no answers and asks nothing: on its turn the events phase lands it alone, whatever else was due, its landing runs straight on the hand drawn as on any turn, and the next deal is due from its landing as from any. 🔧 The schedule keeps dealing through the capstone's own turns: the fewest special rules.

**The capstone lands on a turn drawn when the chronicle opens**, seeded, from a window of turns the region's schedule names; the timeline carries the turn, and the window and what passes it are the levers behind "shorter in the earlier ages". The player knows what the capstone is from the opening and never when: the chronicle screen opens on the capstone's card, and the window is shown nowhere; the landing raises the card again, so the turn is learnt when it comes. A card that shows the turn of the next landing shows the capstone's as an event's, where it lands before the next event.

**A capstone names what passes it**: a count toward a need, read as an achievement's is on the chronicle after the capstone's landing and after every change from then on; the chronicle ends in victory on the spot, on the first change after which the count meets the need, nothing of play resolves after it, and it carries the ending as it carries a defeat. So a building that passes a trial ends the age the moment it stands, mid-play, whatever the card would have done after; a count an enemy phase meets ends it on that change, whoever moved; and one already met when the capstone lands ends it on the landing — the turn's tick, the draw and the timeline's roll on the capstone's turn read nothing, the landing is the first read. The schedule keeps dealing until the count meets the need or the player's city falls. The player's city's fall before that is defeat.

**A capstone may name what fails it** too, a condition on a change, read on every change after its landing as what passes it is read, and the chronicle ends in defeat on the first change after which it holds, nothing of play resolving after it; a trial that names none is lost only with the player's city.

**What the trials are, is content**: a region names its capstones, one or several, and for each what it lands, what passes it, and what its second script does on every turn after the one it lands on.
