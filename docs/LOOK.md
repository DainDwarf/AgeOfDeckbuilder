# The look

> The game's look: the one hand every picture is painted in, how the map, the pieces on it and the cards wear it, and the terms of the packs it comes from. A design page, under [`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md)'s legend. How the chronicle screen is worked is [`CHRONICLE-SCREEN.md`](CHRONICLE-SCREEN.md)'s, and its map [`MAP-SCREEN.md`](MAP-SCREEN.md)'s; what the map is made of is [`MAP.md`](MAP.md)'s; [`../DOGMAS.md`](../DOGMAS.md) holds the rules every asset enters by.

## Two families ✅

Everything drawn is one of two families, and each has one job.

- **Pictures** are paintings of things: a terrain, a river, a building, a feature, a unit's kind, a card's subject. Every picture is **David Baumgart's hand**: his painted hex terrain, his painted locations, his painted objects, his painted resource icons, his painted arms and armour icons, his painted crafting and foraging icons. Code draws geometry, recolours and composes Baumgart's pieces; it paints no picture, and a picture a thing needs that his hand has not painted is composed from his pieces or waits. No picture from another hand stands beside one of his in the same frame.
- **Symbols** are signs of quantities, states, factions, sites, piles and buttons: a resource, population, influence, a unit's stat, a unit's state, a faction, a site, a pile, a button. Every symbol is a **flat, filled, one-colour glyph** from one of two sources, **game-icons.net** where the sign is an object and **Material Design Icons** where it is a plain form, tinted by code in the colour its meaning is known by, so it stays small, sharp and recolourable on any theme. A symbol's shape alone tells it from every other symbol, so no symbol's meaning rests on its colour. A symbol stands without its word where it reads a number or a state, and where it is the sign of a pile or of a button, which names itself on the rest. A symbol is never a picture of a thing on the map.

**A card's frame** is neither: a painted piece of the interface from another hand, chicogames', taken because it sits quietly beside Baumgart's paintings, which stand in its window. The heart and the crossed swords a unit's card reads its health and its damage on are pieces of that frame.

**The chrome** is neither either: DRStudios' metal kit, a piece of the interface from another hand, drawn round every panel, window and button the screens are worked through (_The chrome_, below).

A painted world under flat symbols is the convention of the genre, and the two families do not read as two styles because they never do the same job.

## The cast ✅

Each symbol wears one glyph, and a meaning wears that one sign wherever it is read: health's heart at the foot of a health bar, the flame and the flag on a prepared unit's badge, on the map and in its card's window, move's arrows for a terrain's movement cost, population's sign for the idle count, for a worked tile and for a number of population in a text.

A glyph stands in a square and is as large as that square's side. It is drawn in its colour over a dark outline grown out of its shape, of one width at every size it is drawn at and on every ground, so a pale glyph holds on pale paper and any glyph on any terrain. A glyph standing on a disc that has a rim of its own wears no outline, and neither does a stat's glyph in the text box of a unit's card.

**In a text**, a number of a resource or of population reads as the number and the symbol's glyph right after it, with no word: on a card, on an answer and on a plate. A tooltip, a refusal's note and the ending's sentences draw no glyph and read the word.

## The map ✅

**The terrain** is Baumgart's painted hexes, drawn on his lattice: his hexes are painted for rows closer together than a regular hexagon's, so the map draws its rows that much closer and every hexagon it draws squashed by the same amount, the rules' geometry untouched. His art rises above its hex into the row behind, and a tile's face is drawn behind the row in front of it. A terrain with several paintings spreads them over its tiles, each tile's painting picked by its place on the disc, so a face never changes under the player's eye and one seed always deals one picture.

**The shore** is Baumgart's painted coast laid over a water tile along every edge where the map draws land, chosen among his paintings by which of the tile's six edges meet land, and between the two he painted of each by the tile's place on the disc. It reads the faces the map draws, so a beach comes with the charting of the land beside it and goes with the terraform that takes the land away, fading in and out with the tile that changed; the water meets the uncharted and the edge of the disc bare.

**The river** is Baumgart's painted river set corner by corner along the edges it runs. His rivers are painted crossing a tile between the middles of its edges; set on a corner instead, their legs run the corner's edges, so every corner a river passes wears the one of his pieces whose arms are the river's edges there, each leg reaching the edge's middle where the next corner's piece meets it as his pieces join: a stub at its source, a bend, his fork where two rivers meet. Where a river reaches the water its last half edge is Baumgart's straight river pressed to that length, running into his river mouth, which washes from the corner into the water over the beaches. A piece with several paintings is picked by the corner's place on the disc, as a face is. The river is drawn over the ground, the art risen above a tile's hexagon included.

**The fog and the uncharted.** A tile in fog is drawn as it was last seen with everything of it darkened by the fog's one tint, multiplied into each painting and each mark it carries: Baumgart's terrain, the beach, the river's pieces, the feature's icon and what it lays on the ground, the building, a site's pointer, the unit its snapshot keeps and the stretch of border it wore. The tint is on the painting itself, so the art rising above the hexagon is in fog with its tile and a lit tile's art rising into a fogged row stays lit. A river's piece on a corner is darkened where no tile at that corner is in sight. An uncharted tile wears Baumgart's void, one of his four picked by the tile's place on the disc, and nothing is drawn beyond the disc: it ends where the void meets the page.

**The hex grid.** While the **Hex grid** button has it shown ([`MAP-SCREEN.md`](MAP-SCREEN.md)), every edge of every tile of the map wears a line, the edges of the uncharted tiles and the rim of the disc among them; hidden, which is how every chronicle screen opens, none does. The line runs on the edge itself, straight from corner to corner, in the selection's white over the same line wider in the map's dark outline, both fainter than the selection's own marks, and the lines that meet at a corner close on it, with no gap and nothing stronger there than along an edge. It is the same line on every edge, whatever the two tiles are and whoever holds them. It is drawn over the ground, the art risen above a tile's hexagon included, and under the rivers, the ditches, what lies on the land, the claim marks and the borders, so a river covers it along the edges it runs. An edge with a tile in sight on either side wears the white at its full light; an edge whose tiles are in fog or uncharted wears it darkened by the fog's one tint. City mode's dark leaves it as it is.

**The borders.** A city's border is one line around the tiles it holds, in the city's colour, running along each edge of the ground held, inside the edge with its outer side on it, and rounding every corner on its way, and a band of that colour inside the line, fading bell-shaped inward from the line, a blur and not a gradient, so it has no inner edge. Where the two cities' tiles meet, the two lines stand side by side along their shared edges and round their shared corners a full width apart, each on its own city's side, so neither hides the other. A tile in fog keeps the stretch of border it wore when last seen, darkened by the fog as everything of it is, so the line runs on into the fog in the fog's dark. A city's own tile wears nothing more of it than the rest.

**A camp's border.** A camp's tile wears the enemies' border: a city's line, in the enemies' colour, run straight from corner to corner along the tile's six edges and on the edge itself, and a city's band inside it. It is drawn over a city's border where the two meet and under the pieces, and a camp's tile in fog keeps it as last seen, darkened by the fog.

**The selection.** The selected tile is lit from its edge inward: a rim of the selection's white, fading bell-shaped inward from the edge as the border's band fades, under a thin line of that white along the edge. It is drawn over every dim the map lays and is never darkened by the fog, since it is the screen's reaction and not the tile's, and nothing drawn on the tile moves for it. The tiles a card being aimed admits wear that same rim, each its own, since the card lifted out of the hand is the selection and they are where it lands; the two never stand together on the map. A selected unit's tile wears no rim, the unit wearing its ring, and a tile selected under a unit wears the rim as any tile does, the unit no ring. What tells them apart at rest is nothing.

**The reach.** A selected unit's landings this turn, the unit's own tile among them, are one region: a line along its edges, corner to corner and straight, in the selection's white, as thin as the selected tile's, and inside it a band of that white, fading bell-shaped inward from the line as the selection's rim fades. A tile one of the player's own units holds is crossed and never offered, so the region wears a hole around it, and a region can be several pieces. It is drawn over the buildings and the discs and under the units, and is never darkened by the fog, being the screen's reaction as the selection is. A tile that spends every point the unit has left reads as the rest.

**The path.** A unit's path to a landing is chevrons in the selection's white, pointing the way: two to each step, between the two tiles' middles, each a right angle with its point on the way, stroked with round ends over the same shape wider in the map's dark outline. It runs below the tiles' middles, where a unit's painting stands, from the unit's tile to the landing. At its end, where a unit's painting would stand on the landing, the move points the unit would have left stand on the plate the culture threshold stands on, the number alone. On a city's own tile the city's bars, drawn over the units, cross the lower part of that number. The path and its number are drawn over the selection's other marks and under the units, a unit being dragged among them, and are never darkened by the fog, being the screen's reaction as the selection is.

**The claim marks.** In city mode the tiles the player's city may claim wear a ghost of its border: the border's own line and band, in culture's colour, run round the tiles the city holds and the tiles it may claim together, and kept to the claimable tiles, so a held tile's edge wears the border and nothing more. Where the ghost's line leaves a claimable tile's edge for a held tile's it runs on to the middle of that edge, where the border's line passes over it, so the two meet. It is drawn under the border and under the pieces as the border is, and it is never darkened by the fog, being the screen's reaction as the selection is. The culture threshold the selected tile wears stands on a plate: the feature disc's dark plate with its pale rim, a pill round the number and culture's glyph.

**City mode's tiles.** In city mode a tile one population is assigned to wears population's glyph on a disc, the feature disc's dark with its pale rim, low on the tile, so it passes the tile's bottom point; the glyph wears no outline, the disc having its rim. It stands over the units, a canoe among them, and is never darkened by the fog, being the screen's reaction as the selection is. A held tile nobody is assigned to is darkened as a tile in fog is, by a tint of its own multiplied into its painting, its beach, what lies on its ground, its building, the discs of its row and the pointer of a site standing on it, and that tint darkens as much as the fog's does; the border along it, the river's pieces and the unit on it are not darkened. A held tile in fog wears the fog's dark alone. Every unit is drawn faded for as long as the mode is on: its painting, its halo, its canoe, its badge and its health bar, a unit a tile in fog keeps among them, and a dimmed unit of the player's wears its dim under the fade. The player's city wears neither its bars nor its badge.

**The yield an aimed card would give.** While a card is being aimed, the tile the pointer is on wears a plate where the card's aim admits it and its play would place a building or an improvement there, and so does each tile beside it the building placed would give to. The plate is the culture threshold's, the feature disc's dark pill with its pale rim, centred on the tile's middle. On it stand a plus sign, in the plate's ink and at the size the threshold's number is set at, and a little after it one glyph to each point of yield the tile would be given, the resource's own glyph at the size the threshold's is, in the order the resource bar reads the resources, the glyphs touching as a tile's yield glyphs do. A tile one of the building's kind already gives to wears its plate whole and faded, the play giving it nothing more. The plates stand with the culture threshold, over the units and a city's bars and over the tile's own yield glyphs and their patch, and are never darkened by the fog, being the screen's reaction as the selection is.

**The yield glyphs.** What a tile yields is read on its middle as glyphs, one to each point: the resource's own glyph, in the order the resource bar reads the resources, touching, four to a row, a tile of more points taking a row more under the first, each row centred across on the tile's middle and the rows together centred on it. Under a tile's glyphs lies a patch of dark, soft at its edge as the patch under a building is: an ellipse reaching past the glyphs on every side, near-black over its inner part and falling evenly to nothing at its rim, so under a full row its rim passes the tile's two side edges. Every patch lies under every glyph. Glyphs and patches stand over the units and a city's bars and under the culture threshold and the plates of the yield an aimed card would give, and no fog darkens them, a tile in fog being read as it was last seen. In city mode the glyphs of a tile that is not giving its yield at income are drawn faded, the patch under them as it is. A tile that shows no glyph wears no patch, the tile wearing the culture threshold among them.

**The map's order.** What the map draws stands in one order, the same whatever is switched on. From the ground up: the land, the hex grid, the rivers, the ditches and what lies on the land, the claim marks and the borders; the buildings, the discs and over them the sites' pointers; the marks of the selection and the aim, which are the reach, a unit's ring and the attack's, the selected tile's rim, an aimed card's rims, and the path with its number and the attack's arrow; the units, each with its faction's sign, its badge and its health bar; a city's bars, its badge and its faction's sign, and a camp's sign; the yield glyphs on their patches, over them the marks of the assigned tiles, and over those the culture threshold and the plates of the yield an aimed card would give; and the infopanel, a refusal's note and the tooltip. City mode's dark is no sheet in that order: it is in the paintings themselves, as the fog's is, so it darkens no mark of the selection and no unit, and nothing changes place when it comes on. A unit being dragged is drawn over the other units.

**Features** are Baumgart's resource icons. A feature's tile wears the icon of the thing on a dark disc with a pale rim, in the row above the tile's middle where the improvements stand beside it. The disc is what tells a feature from the ground at every zoom; the rest is flavour, so the tile itself reads as the feature from across the disc: decor lying on the tile's ground, under whatever stands on the tile, or a tint of the terrain's painting.

**Improvements** are told as a feature is, by Baumgart's icon on a disc in the same row: the disc the feature's turned pale with a dark rim, so what was placed reads apart from what the land holds, and two discs side by side stand just apart. An improvement placed on a feature and nowhere else adds no disc: the feature's own disc turns pale, one disc telling the two, under the feature's icon or under a picture of the improvement's own, and the decor the feature laid on its tile leaves it. The rest is flavour lying on the land, under whatever stands on the tile, and it is one of two. Baumgart's painting of the improvement lies on the tile's ground as a feature's decor does: large about the tile's middle while the tile carries no building, small at one side beside a building that leaves it room, and not at all under a building that fills its tile, where the disc alone tells it. An improvement whose painting would lie on treetops opens its tile into his clearing, as a building does. Or ditches run from the river into the tile: the source piece of Baumgart's own river, pressed thin, one from the middle of every edge of the tile a river runs along, reaching about to the tile's middle and turned a little by the tile's place, drawn over the river and under everything else on the ground, under every building alike.

## The pieces on the map ✅

**A building and a city's own building** are Baumgart's painted locations and his painted objects, standing on their tile over the terrain, the terrain showing around them. A building is one painting or a few, each at a size and a place of its own about the tile's middle and drawn back to front, a painting of his made for a whole tile drawn at the terrain's own scale. The player's city and the neutral's wear the same paintings, so the border and the badge are what tell whose it is. A building whose paintings would not read on every ground it can stand on lies on the patch: dark earth, wider than tall, soft at its edge, drawn by code and the same under every building that lies on it. Under a building, a tile whose terrain Baumgart painted a clearing of wears it in place of its painting, picked by the tile's place as a face is, so the building stands in a gap and not on the treetops. The feature's disc and the improvements beside it are drawn over a building, and a feature's decor and an improvement's painting lie under it. On the infopanel a building is a card's face, its art box holding the building as a card that places one holds it. A building Baumgart's sets lack is composed from his pieces by code.

**A site and a camp** are Baumgart's paintings as a building is, in everything the paragraph above says of one: one painting or a few about the tile's middle, his clearing under one, the feature's disc and the improvements over it. A camp's painting is its age's. A site wears its pointer, and a camp's tile the enemies' border; on the infopanel a site, a camp and the neutral's city wear a face of their own ([`MAP-SCREEN.md`](MAP-SCREEN.md)), their painting in its art box, with neither the pointer nor the border.

**A site's pointer.** Every site wears a pointer, the same for all of them, so a site is told from across the map and its painting says which it is. It is the feature's disc, its dark with its pale rim, drawn out under itself into a point: the rim leaves the disc either side of its lowest point and runs straight to a tip under the disc, the corners it turns rounded. It stands above the tile's middle, above the row, so it stands on the two tiles behind its own, its tip ends just inside the tile's top corner, and the site's painting shows whole under it. On it stands the site's sign, in a pale of its own and with no outline, the pointer having its rim. It is drawn with the discs and over them, over the buildings and under the marks of the selection and the units, and it passes onto whatever lies behind its tile: a disc of the row there, or Baumgart's void where the tile behind is uncharted or the map ends. It is darkened with its tile, by the fog as last seen and by city mode's dark, and the Icons button hides it with the discs ([`MAP-SCREEN.md`](MAP-SCREEN.md)). It leaves the map with its site. A camp wears none, its border telling it.

**A unit** is Baumgart's painting of what its kind carries, a tool or a weapon, standing alone in a halo of its faction's colour, and is never a symbol. The painting stands low on its tile, below the tile's middle, each kind at a size of its own, over whatever the tile carries, so a building under it shows its upper part. The halo starts on the painting's own outline and fades outward, bell-shaped as the border's band fades, with a thin dark line between the painting and it. It is the civilization's colour round a unit of the player's, the neutral's round one of the neutral's and the enemies' round an enemy, and a kind wears the same painting whoever it belongs to. An embarked unit stands in Baumgart's canoe, under the painting's middle, and the canoe wears no halo. A unit whose kind stands on the water alone stands in no canoe: its painting is the vessel itself, as wide as the canoe, where any unit's painting stands, with the dark line and the halo any unit wears. While it carries a unit it is not drawn: the canoe under the embarked unit is all that shows of it. In fog the painting, the halo and the canoe are darkened as every painting is. A dimmed unit of the player's has its painting, its halo and its canoe darkened. On the infopanel a unit is its card's face, its window holding the unit as the map paints it.

**A unit's ring.** The selected unit, and each unit a card aimed at a unit admits, wears a ring on the ground under it in the selection's white, the same for both since the two never stand together: an ellipse about the painting's middle, squashed as the map's rows are, a line as thin as the selected tile's along it on its inner side, and inside the line a band of that white, fading bell-shaped inward as the selection's rim fades. It lies over the building and under every unit, and passes a little over the tile's two lower edges. A unit of any faction wears it, embarked or not, and a dimmed one wears it undarkened. It is never darkened by the fog, being the screen's reaction as the selection is. It stays on the ground at the unit's tile while the unit is dragged. A unit a tile in fog keeps as last seen wears it while selected, undarkened as in sight, and a unit on the infopanel wears none.

**The attack's marks.** Each unit the selected unit can attack wears the unit's ring in the attack's colour, the enemies' red whoever the unit belongs to: the same ring in everything but its colour, lying where a selected unit's lies. The attack read under the pointer is an arrow in that colour from the attacking unit to the unit attacked: one straight line between the middles of the two paintings, stroked as the path's chevrons are, starting a little off the attacker's middle and stopping short of the other's, where one of the path's chevrons is its head; it runs over whatever lies between. Where a step comes first, the arrow leaves from where the unit's painting would stand on the tile it attacks from, as far off it, and the path to that tile is drawn as any path is, its number at the arrow's start. The health the attack would remove is read on the attacked unit's health bar. The arrow is drawn where the path is, over the selection's other marks and under the units, and neither it nor the ring is ever darkened by the fog, being the screen's reaction as the selection is.

**A unit's badge.** A unit that has prepared wears a badge: a disc of its faction's colour with the dark rim a city's badge wears, right of its tile's middle and level with it, so it stands at the painting's upper right. On it a white glyph says what the prepare lands as: a flame for a pillage, a flag for a capture. The badge is part of the unit: it is drawn over the painting, goes where the unit goes, and in fog is darkened as the painting is, disc, rim and glyph alike, so a unit a tile in fog keeps as last seen prepared wears it still. A unit on the infopanel wears it in its card's window.

**The faction signs.** While the **Faction signs** button has them shown ([`MAP-SCREEN.md`](MAP-SCREEN.md)), every unit, every city and every camp wears its faction's sign; hidden, which is how every chronicle screen opens, none does, and colour alone tells whose a thing is. A unit's sign is the prepared badge in everything but its place and what it reads: a disc of its faction's colour with the badge's dark rim, left of its tile's middle and level with it, at the painting's upper left, and on it the faction's glyph, dark on the civilization's colour and white on the neutral's and the enemies'. It is part of the unit as the badge is: it is drawn over the painting, goes where the unit goes, and is darkened with it in fog and when it is dimmed, so a unit a tile in fog keeps as last seen wears it still. A hostile unit of the neutral's wears the neutral's sign. A city's own tile and a camp's tile wear the sign as the twin of a city's badge: that badge's disc with its rim, its right edge on the left end of a city's bars and level with the city's badge, the glyph in the same inks; the neutral's city and a camp, which wear no bars, wear it at the same place. It is drawn with a city's badge, over the units, so it crosses the lower part of the health bar of a hurt unit standing on the tile, and it passes the tile's lower-left edge onto the next tile; it is darkened by the fog with its tile, and city mode leaves it standing. A site wears none. A building's face on the infopanel wears none, and a unit's wears its unit's in its window, as does the face of a unit kind a name raises on the chronicle screen.

**A unit's health bar.** A unit below its full health wears a bar upright at its painting's left, thin and the painting's own height, level with the painting's middle, on a track of the map's dark outline a little wider on every side, its corners rounded. The bar fills upward by the share of its full health the unit holds, in health's green, and the rest of it is the grey of health lost; it is the same length for every kind. At the bar's foot stands a heart, red, its middle just under the track's end, drawn over the bar in its outline. A unit at its full health wears neither. The bar is part of the unit as its badge is: it is drawn over the painting, goes where the unit goes, and is darkened with it in fog and when it is dimmed, track, bar, heart and all, so a unit a tile in fog keeps as last seen hurt wears it at the health last seen. While an attack is read on a unit, the part of its bar the attack would remove, from the top of the health held down, is turned the attack's red, a unit at full health wearing its bar for as long as the reading stands; where the attack would kill it, all the health held is red and a skull stands in the heart's place, white, in its outline. A unit on the infopanel wears none, its card reading the numbers.

**A city's bars and its badge.** The player's city wears two bars along the bottom of its building, as a unit's health bar is drawn but lying flat: centred across on the tile's middle and below it, the food bar over the culture bar, each on a track of the map's dark outline a little wider on every side, its corners rounded, so the two tracks touch. A bar fills from its left by the share of its threshold the stock holds, in its resource's colour, and is full from the threshold on; the rest of it is the grey of a threshold not yet met. The food bar reads the growth threshold and the culture bar the culture threshold. Past what the stock holds, the part ending the turn would add to it is the resource's colour faded over the grey, stopping at the bar's end; a turn that would add nothing, or take from the stock, leaves the bar at the stock. At the bars' right end stands the city's badge: a disc of the city's colour with a thin dark rim, its left edge on the bars' end, level with the middle of the two bars; on it the population, bold, dark on the civilization's colour and pale on the neutral's. A second digit widens it to its right into a pill. The neutral's city wears the badge alone, at the same place. Bars and badge are drawn over the units and under the yield glyphs: they cross the lower part of a unit standing on the city and the heart at the foot of its health bar, no dim darkens them, and the badge passes the tile's lower-right edge onto the next tile. They grow and shrink with the map as every mark on it does. City mode takes the player's city's bars and its badge off the map.

## The cards 🔧

A card has one size wherever it stands, and a card shown large is the one other size; the face is one drawing at both, every measure of it a share of its width.

**The frame** is one of the pack's two plain fronts, a silver rim round a window over a text box of paper, drawn so the front's body is the card. A card whose play places a unit, a building or an improvement wears the oval window, whose rim rises a little above the card's top edge, and every other card the arched one. A terrain's face and the river's wear the oval window too. The frame's body wears the colour of the card's kind, the pack's brown paper turned to that colour with its grain kept, the rims and the text box's paper left as painted: stone grey for a settle card, gold for a unit card, teal for a building card, olive for an improvement card, bone for an action and for an instant, crimson for a hazard, maroon for a challenge, ochre for a trophy, and loam brown for a terrain's face and the river's. ## The type ✅

Every word the game shows is set in **Source Sans 3**, regular and bold: Adobe's humanist sans, drawn for interfaces, its figures lining and of one width so a counter ticks in place. The face ships with the build and is loaded before the first text is laid out, so every machine lays out the same screens, and a build whose face cannot be loaded does not start. The debug console's monospace is the machine's own. No text is set under one smallest size, the smallest that reads as a word on the smallest window the game is read in ([`INTERFACE.md`](INTERFACE.md)); a text on the map is measured at the map's designed zoom, the zoom being the player's own magnifier.
