# The look

> The game's look: the one hand every picture is painted in, how the map, the pieces on it and the cards wear it, and the terms of the packs it comes from. A design page, under [`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md)'s legend. How the chronicle screen is worked is [`CHRONICLE-SCREEN.md`](CHRONICLE-SCREEN.md)'s; what the map is made of is [`MAP.md`](MAP.md)'s; [`../DOGMAS.md`](../DOGMAS.md) holds the rules every asset enters by.

## Two families ✅

Everything drawn is one of two families, and each has one job.

- **Pictures** are paintings of things: a terrain, a river, a building, a feature, a unit's kind, a card's subject. Every picture is **David Baumgart's hand**: his painted hex terrain, his painted locations, his painted objects, his painted resource icons, his painted arms and armour icons. Code draws geometry, recolours and composes his pieces; it paints no picture, and a picture a thing needs that his hand has not painted is composed from his pieces or waits. No picture from another hand stands beside one of his in the same frame.
- **Symbols** are signs of quantities and kinds: a resource, a yield, a cost, the icons the screens read. Every symbol is a **flat one-colour glyph from game-icons.net**, tinted by code in the colour its meaning is known by, so it stays small, sharp and recolourable on any theme. A symbol is never a picture of a thing on the map.

A painted world under flat symbols is the convention of the genre, and the two families do not read as two styles because they never do the same job.

## The map ✅

**The terrain** is his painted hexes, drawn on his lattice: his hexes are painted for rows three quarters of a tile's width apart where a regular hexagon's rows are 0.866 of it, so the map draws its rows that much closer and every hexagon it draws squashed by the same amount, the rules' geometry untouched. His art rises above its hex into the row behind, and a tile's face is drawn behind the row in front of it. A terrain with several paintings spreads them over its tiles, each tile's painting picked by its place on the disc, so a face never changes under the player's eye and one seed always deals one picture.

**The shore** is his painted coast laid over a water tile along every edge where the map draws land, chosen among his paintings by which of the tile's six edges meet land, and between the two he painted of each by the tile's place on the disc. It reads the faces the map draws, so a beach comes with the charting of the land beside it and goes with the terraform that takes the land away, fading in and out with the tile that changed; the water meets the uncharted and the edge of the disc bare.

**The river** is his painted river set corner by corner along the edges it runs. His rivers are painted crossing a tile between the middles of its edges; set on a corner instead, their legs run the corner's edges, so every corner a river passes wears the one of his pieces whose arms are the river's edges there, each leg reaching the edge's middle where the next corner's piece meets it as his pieces join: a stub at its source, a bend, his fork where two rivers meet. Where a river reaches the water its last half edge is his straight river pressed to that length, running into his river mouth, which washes from the corner into the water over the beaches. A piece with several paintings is picked by the corner's place on the disc, as a face is. The river is drawn over the ground, the art risen above a tile's hexagon included. A river's row on the infopanel's cards reads a round cut of his straight river, bare, the one an irrigation's disc wears.

**The fog and the uncharted.** A tile in fog is drawn as it was last seen with everything of it darkened by the fog's one tint, multiplied into each painting and each mark it carries: his terrain, the beach, the river's pieces, the feature's icon and what it lays on the ground, the building, the unit its snapshot keeps and the stretch of border it wore. The tint is on the painting itself, so the art rising above the hexagon is in fog with its tile and a lit tile's art rising into a fogged row stays lit. A river's piece on a corner is darkened where no tile at that corner is in sight. An uncharted tile wears his void, one of his four picked by the tile's place on the disc, and nothing is drawn beyond the disc: it ends where the void meets the page.

**The borders.** A city's border is one line around the tiles it holds, in the city's colour, 1.5 units wide, running along each edge of the ground held, half its width inside the edge, and rounding every corner on its way, and a band of that colour inside the line, 60% at the line and fading bell-shaped over 7 units, a blur and not a gradient, so it has no inner edge. Where the two cities' tiles meet, the two lines stand side by side along their shared edges and round their shared corners a full width apart, each on its own city's side, so neither hides the other. A tile in fog keeps the stretch of border it wore when last seen, darkened by the fog as everything of it is, so the line runs on into the fog in the fog's dark. A city's own tile wears nothing more of it than the rest.

**A camp's border.** A camp's tile wears the enemies' border: a city's line, 1.5 units wide, in the enemies' colour, run straight from corner to corner along the tile's six edges and on the edge itself, and a city's band inside it, 60% at the line and fading bell-shaped over 7 units. It is drawn over a city's border where the two meet and under the pieces, the yield overlay's dim covers it as it covers a city's, and a camp's tile in fog keeps it as last seen, darkened by the fog.

**The selection.** The selected tile is lit from its edge inward: a rim of the selection's white, 80% at the edge and fading bell-shaped over 5 units as the border's band fades, under a thin line of that white along the edge, 1 unit wide at 90%. It is drawn over every dim the map lays and is never darkened by the fog, since it is the screen's answer and not the tile's, and nothing drawn on the tile moves for it. The tiles a card being aimed admits wear that same rim, each its own, since the card lifted out of the hand is the selection and they are where it lands; the two never stand together on the map. A selected unit's tile wears no rim, the unit wearing its ring, and a tile selected under a unit wears the rim as any tile does, the unit no ring; a card aimed at a unit lights no tile. What tells them apart at rest is nothing; in motion, the aim's rims are to breathe faster than the selection's, which is Motion's.

**The reach.** A selected unit's landings this turn, the unit's own tile among them, are one region: a line along its edges, corner to corner and straight, in the selection's white, 1 unit wide at 90%, and inside it a band of that white, 60% at the line and fading bell-shaped over 5 units as the selection's rim fades. A tile one of the player's own units holds is crossed and never offered, so the region wears a hole around it, and a region can be several pieces. It is drawn over the buildings and the discs and under the units, and is never darkened by the fog, being the screen's answer as the selection is. A tile that spends every point the unit has left reads as the rest. The enemies the unit can attack glow as they do.

**The path.** A unit's path to a landing is chevrons in the selection's white, pointing the way: two to each step, at three tenths and seven tenths of the way from one tile to the next, each a right angle with its point on the way and its two arms reaching 3.75 units back and 3.75 to either side, stroked 1.5 units wide at 90% with round ends, over the same shape 3 units wide in the map's dark outline at 63%. It runs 8 units below the tiles' middles, where a unit's painting stands, from the unit's tile to the landing. At its end, 8 units below the landing's middle, the move points the unit would have left stand on the plate the culture threshold stands on, the number alone. The path and its number are drawn over the selection's other marks and under the units, a unit being dragged among them, and are never darkened by the fog, being the screen's answer as the selection is.

**The claim marks.** In city mode the tiles the player's city may claim wear a ghost of its border: the border's own line and band, in culture's colour, run round the tiles the city holds and the tiles it may claim together, and kept to the claimable tiles, so a held tile's edge wears the border and nothing more. Where the ghost's line leaves a claimable tile's edge for a held tile's it runs on to the middle of that edge, where the border's line passes over it, so the two meet. It is drawn under the border and under the pieces as the border is, the yield overlay's dim covers it as it covers the border, and it is never darkened by the fog, being the screen's answer as the selection is. The culture threshold the selected tile wears stands on a plate: the feature disc's dark plate with its pale rim, a pill round the number and culture's glyph.

**The map's order.** What the map draws stands in one order, the same whatever is switched on. From the ground up: the land with its rivers, its ditches and what lies on it, the claim marks and the borders; the buildings, the discs and a city's badge; city mode's marks and its dim; the yield overlay's dim; the marks of the selection and the aim, which are a prepared tile's colour, the reach and the glow on what a unit can attack, a unit's ring, the selected tile's rim, an aimed card's rims and the path; the units; the yield glyphs and the culture threshold; and the infopanel, a refusal's note and the tooltip. A dim is a sheet with its own place in that order: it darkens what lies under it and nothing over it, so no dim darkens a mark of the selection or a unit, and nothing changes place when one comes on. A unit being dragged is drawn over the other units.

**Features** are his resource icons. A feature's tile wears the icon of the thing, 14 units wide on a dark disc with a pale rim, in the row above the tile's middle where the improvements stand beside it: the deer, the cow, his tobacco leaf for a fertile plain, his limestone for flint, his water for an oasis. The disc is what tells a feature from the ground at every zoom; the rest is flavour lying on the land, so the tile itself reads as the feature from across the disc. A deer's or a cattle's tile carries three small copies of its animal, 8 units wide, scattered about the tile's middle by its place on the disc, under whatever stands on the tile. An oasis lies on his pond, one of his four picked by the tile's place. A fertile plain's painting is tinted greener and a flint hill's slate: the painting re-painted once at boot in its own shading, a second painting of the face the tile wears. A feature's row on the infopanel's cards reads the same icon, bare.

**Improvements** are told as a feature is, by his icon on a disc in the same row: the disc the feature's turned pale with a dark rim, so what was placed reads apart from what the land holds, and two discs side by side stand a unit apart. An improvement placed on a feature and nowhere else adds no disc: the feature's own disc turns pale, one disc telling the two, under the feature's icon or under a picture of the improvement's own. The rest is flavour lying on the land, under whatever stands on the tile, and it is one of two. His painting of the improvement lies on the tile's ground as a herd and a pond do: large about the tile's middle while the tile carries no building, small at one side beside a building that leaves it room, and not at all under a building that fills its tile, where the disc alone tells it. A megalith, a shelter, a tannery and a farm leave room; a city, the monument's three stages, a site and a camp fill their tile. Or ditches run from the river into the tile: his river's own source piece, pressed thin, one from the middle of every edge of the tile a river runs along, reaching about to the tile's middle and turned a little by the tile's place, drawn over the river and under everything else on the ground, under every building alike. A clay pit is his clay, 14 units wide, on the disc and his worked pit on the hill, 27 units wide alone and 16 beside a building. A pasture is its cattle's disc turned pale and his round corral with his cows in it on the plain, 25 units wide alone and 14 beside a building, and the herd leaves its tile, the cows being in the corral. An irrigation is a round cut of his straight river on the disc, rim to rim and tilted, and its ditches, 18 units long and 2.5 wide, each turned by up to 25° either way. A trapping is its deer's disc turned pale under his deer behind his net, the head showing over it: one picture composed of his two, the deer 14 units wide as on its own disc. That picture lies on the forest too, the deer 16 units wide alone and 10 beside a building, the herd leaves its tile, the deer being in the net, and the forest wears his clearing as it does under a building, so the catch lies in a gap and not on the treetops. An improvement's row on the infopanel's cards reads what its disc wears on the map, on the same pale disc.

## The pieces on the map ✅

**A building and a city's own building** are his painted locations and his painted objects, standing on their tile over the terrain, the terrain showing around them. A building is one painting or a few, each at a size and a place of its own about the tile's middle and drawn back to front, a painting of his made for a whole tile drawn at the terrain's own scale. A city from the Stone Age on is his thatched village, one of his four picked by the tile's place, the player's and the neutral's alike, so the border and the badge are what tell whose it is. The monument's three stages lie on one patch of dark earth, 42 units wide and 30 tall, soft at its edge, drawn by code and the same under all three, so the stones read on the plain, the hills and the desert alike. A forest tile carrying one of them wears his clearing in place of its forest, one of his two picked by the tile's place, so the building stands in a gap and not on the treetops. The feature's disc and the improvements beside it are drawn over a building, and the herd, the pond and an improvement's painting lie under it. On the infopanel a building's row and its card's head read the same paintings, the patch among them, fitted to their box by what is painted. A building his sets lack is composed from his pieces by code.

**A site and a camp** are his paintings as a building is, in everything the paragraph above says of one: one painting or a few about the tile's middle, his clearing under one on a forest tile, the feature's disc and the improvements over it, the same paintings on the infopanel. A camp's painting is its age's. A site wears nothing more, a painting on ground nobody holds being what tells it, and a camp's tile wears the enemies' border, which the infopanel leaves out.

**A unit** is his painting of what its kind carries, a tool or a weapon, standing alone in a halo of its faction's colour, and is never a symbol. The painting stands low on its tile, his icon drawn 18 units wide with its middle 8 units below the tile's, over whatever the tile carries, so a building under it shows its upper part. The halo starts on the painting's own outline and fades outward, 90% at the outline and fading bell-shaped over 4 units as the border's band fades, with a dark line 0.25 units wide between the painting and it. It is the civilization's colour round a unit of the player's, the neutral's round one of the neutral's and the enemies' round an enemy, and a kind wears the same painting whoever it belongs to. A worker is his mallet, a warrior his spiked club, a scout his leather shoes, an archer his short bow, drawn 19.8 units wide, and a leader his decorative axe, drawn 20.7. An embarked unit stands in his canoe, 30 units wide, its middle 8 units under the painting's, and the canoe wears no halo. In fog the painting, the halo and the canoe are darkened as every painting is. On the infopanel a unit's card is headed by the same painting in its halo, in its canoe while embarked, fitted to its box by the painting and the canoe, the halo reaching past them.

**A unit's ring.** The selected unit, and each unit a card aimed at a unit admits, wears a ring on the ground under it in the selection's white, the same for both since the two never stand together: an ellipse about the painting's middle, 15 units to either side and squashed as the map's rows are, a line 1 unit wide at 90% along it on its inner side, and inside the line a band of that white, 80% at the line and fading bell-shaped over 5 units as the selection's rim fades. It lies over the building and the city's badge and under every unit, and passes a little over the tile's two lower edges. A unit of any faction wears it, embarked or not, and one whose turn is done wears it undarkened. It is never darkened by the fog, being the screen's answer as the selection is. It stays on the ground at the unit's tile while the unit is dragged. A unit a tile in fog keeps as last seen wears it while selected, undarkened as in sight, and a unit on the infopanel wears none.

## The cards 🔧

A card's art box holds **one painted object** from the one hand over a ground tinted in the card's kind: a building card its building's sprite, a unit card its unit's picture, an action card the object of the action, a hazard its cause. The chips, the cost and the glyphs in a card's text are symbols. A card face is one size wherever it stands, and a card shown large is the one other.

## The type ✅

Every word the game shows is set in **Source Sans 3**, regular and bold: Adobe's humanist sans, drawn for interfaces, its figures lining and of one width so a counter ticks in place. The face ships with the build and is loaded before the first text is laid out, so every machine lays out the same screens, and a build whose face cannot be loaded does not start. The debug console's monospace is the machine's own. No text is set under 11 units of the design, the smallest that reads as a word on the smallest window the game is read in ([`INTERFACE.md`](INTERFACE.md)); a text on the map is measured at the map's designed zoom, the zoom being the player's own magnifier.

## The packs ✅

- **David Baumgart's hex sets and icon sets**, bought one pack at a time as a region, an age or a screen needs it: the hex terrain, the hex rivers, coasts and seas, the hex locations, his painted objects, his resource icons and his arms and armour icons, and the ancient objects he sent by mail. His terms: free and commercial games allowed, the files modified and recombined at will, never repackaged or given away on their own, and credit to "David Baumgart" asked, with a link to dgbaumgart.com. His files therefore never sit in the public repository: they live in a store of their own the build reads, and the credit stands on the credits screen.
- **game-icons.net**, CC BY 3.0: each glyph's author credited on the credits screen and in the licence record.
- **Source Sans 3**, Adobe's, under the SIL Open Font License 1.1: the licence ships beside the files, and a modified version may not keep the name.

Every pack keeps its licence entry in `public/assets/LICENSES.md` the moment it enters, as the dogmas say, and nothing in the look comes from a generative model.
